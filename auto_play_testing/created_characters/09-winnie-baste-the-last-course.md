# Winnie Baste, the Last Course

*Built by: subagent, seed: Something funny. The magic chapter's spell table mentions a "Food" realm — flung hams, feud-ending pies. A gonzo, comedic traveller whose weird specialty shouldn't be useful but somehow is, 2026-09-23.*

*Rules source: `rulebook.tex` + `chapters/`.*

## Concept

Winifred "Winnie" Baste was catering the Duke of Marrowby's wedding when
the wound in the world opened right through the marquee. It swallowed the
bride, half the guest list, and the ice sculpture. Winnie had been **paid
in full, in advance**, for seven courses, and she had only served six.

So she followed them through. She drives a battered food truck called *The
Gravy Boat* from realm to realm, and in the back, strapped down and under a
sealed dome, rides a seven-tier wedding cake. She means to serve it to the
bride if it kills her. It may well kill her: she's a short, round,
fifty-eight-year-old woman with a bad knee and Body 1.

Her magic is food and nothing but food. She can conjure a roast, taste a
lie in a sauce, and bake a pie that makes two brothers weep and hug. Her
late father was a butcher who always asked the animals' permission first,
and she can still talk to livestock with his brass ear-trumpet. The pigs
are excellent informants. She can eat anything, and she steps out of any
pantry into any other pantry nearby. She's loud and relentlessly
hospitable. She doesn't think of herself as an adventurer. She thinks of
herself as a caterer with an outstanding booking.

## Magic (12 tags, Weird 3, 2 Feats): gastromancy

Magic rank 4 gets 12 Tags Known, Weird 3, and 2 Feats.

- **Techniques (4):** `Create`, `Perceive`, `Transform`, `Control`
- **Forms (3):** `Plant`, `Animal`, `Fire`. Food is vegetables, meat, and
  cooking heat. I left out `Water` (soups, sauces) to leave room for
  specifics.
- **Specific (5):** `Food`, `Pies`, `Ham`, `Tasting`, `Feasts`

Count: 4 + 3 + 5 = **12 / 12**.

Is `Food` legal? The rule says a Specific tag must be "more specific in
application than the Techniques and Forms above." `Food` isn't narrower
than any *single* Form: it covers parts of Plant, Animal, Water, and Fire.
But it's much narrower than all of them put together, and the rulebook's own
Rooster example lists `food` as a tag. So I ruled it legal. `Pies`, `Ham`,
and `Feasts` are narrower than `Food`. `Tasting` is narrower than
`Perceive`. The rules don't forbid a specific inside another specific (see
Rules Issues).

### Magic Feats (2)

1. **Eat Anything** *(custom, Continuous; built with the GM as "Your Feats"
   allows)*. Nothing she swallows can harm her: poison, glass, ghost-pepper
   vindaloo, a live coal, a data-chip. **The hard constraint it bypasses:**
   what you eat can hurt you. It's modelled on Needs No Air, which bypasses
   one bodily vulnerability. Source: *a lifetime of tasting everything
   first*, which the realm re-dresses as magic.
2. **Blink**, re-skinned as **Pantry Step**. She steps into any cupboard,
   larder, fridge, or bread-bin and comes out of another one a short
   distance away. (I narrowed the Feat's fiction. The rules give nothing
   back for narrowing it. See Rules Issues.)

## Stuff (7 points, 4 tags, 1 Feat, Lifestyle: Well Off)

Stuff rank 3 gets 7 Stuff Points, Max Tier 3, 4 Stuff Tags, 1 Feat, and the
Well Off lifestyle.

| Item | Category | Tier | Cost | Tags |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Gravy Boat**, food truck | Vehicle | 3 | 3 | `Rugged`, `Kitchen` *(custom)* |
| **The Long Ladle**, a sprung serving-ladle that lobs a roast sixty yards | Weapon | 2 | 2 | `Ranged` |
| **The Dinner Bell**: everyone hungry within earshot stops what they're doing and comes toward the sound | Gadget | 2 | 2 | `Loud` *(custom)* |

- **Points:** 3 + 2 + 2 = **7 / 7**. Nothing is above Tier 3, the Stuff 3 ceiling. ✓
- **Tags:** 2 + 1 + 1 = **4 / 4**. ✓
- **Lifestyle: Well Off** (automatic, no points): "generally nice things,
  spending money, good tools." A good set of knives, and a larder of hams,
  flour, and butter restocked in each realm.
- **The Cake** isn't on the sheet. It's a story hook with no mechanics.

Notes on the tag choices:
- `Kitchen` is basically the Location tag `Workshop` ("tools and space to
  build or repair things") moved onto a Vehicle. Tags can be invented "with
  your GM's approval", but the Gadget section warns against giving an item
  "a second, unrelated purpose." Is a kitchen in a food truck *unrelated*? I
  say no, because a food truck *is* a kitchen. A strict GM could say yes.
- The Dinner Bell is very close to being "a spell wearing a disguise" (a
  Face effect in a box). I undershot as the book advises: it only draws
  people who are **already hungry**, and it only makes them come toward the
  sound. It doesn't make them friendly.

### Stuff Feat (1)

- **Speak with Animals**: **Papa Baste's Ear-Trumpet**, a dented brass
  horn. Through it, every beast understands her and she understands it.
  (It works on live animals. The pigs have strong views on the ham tag.) As
  the reference character found, the rules don't say whether the item behind
  a Stuff Feat costs Stuff Points. I treated it as a free prop that comes with
  the Feat.

## Four Scenarios

### 1. High Fantasy: brigands on the Marrowby road (Body)

Four brigands step out of the trees and grab the truck's bridle-chains
(it's a horse-drawn chuck-wagon here). Winnie climbs onto the roof with the
Long Ladle, loads a bone-in ham from the larder, and lobs it, steering it in
flight with a wiggle of her fingers, straight into the leader's face. This
is the rulebook's own example: *"Knock a brigand flat with a flung ham —
Food — Body — The brigand."*

- **Base:** Body, because the brigand resists. She's acting *through* the
  Ladle, so Max(Tier 2, Body 1) = **2**.
- **Candidate tags:** `Archery` (thrown weapon), `Ranged` (Ladle), `Ham`,
  `Food`, `Animal` (a ham is animal matter), `Control` (steering it in
  flight). That's **6 candidates**.
- **Applied (cap 5):** `Archery` + `Ranged` + `Control` + `Ham` + `Food` = **5**.
  `Animal` gets dropped.
- **Pool: 2 + 5 = 7 dice.** Expected successes ≈ 3.5.

A Body-1 grandmother matches the ham-hurling of a Body-3 swordsman with
two tags. It happens because `Ham`, `Food`, and `Animal` all describe **the
same ham**, and the cap is the only thing that stops them stacking further.
Without the Ladle (base 1) she'd still roll 1 + 5 = 6.

**The alternate line (a loophole):** she *doesn't throw anything*. She
conjures a 40-lb wheel of Marrowby Blue six feet above the leader's head.
The "what resists?" test gets slippery here. Conjuring is Weird ("only
reality resists"). The rules say "using the effect is a separate roll," but
nobody *uses* the cheese: gravity does. If the GM accepts this as one Weird
roll:

- **Base:** Weird **3**
- **Tags:** `Create` + `Food` + `Animal` (dairy) + `Feasts`? (no, that's a
  stretch) → `Create` + `Food` + `Animal` = **3**
- **Pool: 6 dice**, and Body never enters into it.

If the GM rules that the falling cheese is a second, Body roll, she rolls
1 + (`Food`, `Animal`) = 3 dice. That's pathetic, as intended. **The rules
support either reading.** Compare "Teleport a boulder instead of lifting
it", which is Weird. Dropping the boulder *on* someone isn't covered.

**Fragility:** Body pool **1**. If she fails once, she's at 0 Body. Every
Body roll takes −1 die, and the rule says that includes item-based rolls, so
this becomes 6 dice. After that, every further Body hit goes to the
**party's** Coherence instead of to her.

### 2. Cyberpunk: the poisoned canapé (Mind)

Winnie has talked her way into catering a Kurogane-Halvorsen board gala.
Someone has poisoned one tray of forty. She walks the line, sampling. This
is almost word for word the book's *"Taste which of forty dishes was
poisoned — Food — Mind — The poison is hidden."*

- **Is a roll even needed?** Eat Anything means the poison can't hurt her.
  The Conflicts section says to "roll only when both the outcome **and the
  consequences** are in doubt." Her *personal* risk is gone. What's still at
  stake is **finding the tray before a board member eats from it**. So the
  GM calls a roll against the clock, and the stake on failure is "the CFO
  collapses," not damage to her Mind pool.
- **Base:** Mind **2**. A hidden thing resists. No item applies (the canapés
  aren't hers, and her truck is outside).
- **Candidate tags:** `Perceive`, `Tasting`, `Food`, `Cooking`, `Perception`.
- **Applied:** all 5 = **5, at the cap.**
- **Pool: 2 + 5 = 7 dice.**

How does food magic re-dress in Cyberpunk? The realm chapter says "Magic is
**hacking**" in the sprawl. So is her `Tasting` a tongue-implant chem-sniffer?
Is `Pies` a... pie-hacking daemon? The Magic chapter says only that a tag's
"meaning bends to fit the realm." I played it as a gene-hacked palate with a
tox-spectrometer on her tongue stud. That's fun, but it's all invention. The
realm sections are lipsum.

### 3. Space Adventure: the feud-ending pie (Face)

The Vesh and the Kolloran have been at blood-feud for nine generations.
Their ambassadors sit at opposite ends of the station's mess hall, and the
party needs them to sign a docking treaty. Winnie parks the Gravy Boat
(here a boxy supply shuttle) in the hangar, bakes in its galley, and wheels
out a pie with a pastry lattice in *both* clan sigils. This is the book's
*"Bake a pie that ends a family feud — Food — Face — The grudge, not the
pastry."*

- **Base:** Face **3**. The grudge resists. Could she use Max(Tier 3
  truck, Face 3)? It's a tie either way. It would matter for a Face-1 cook,
  and I don't think "acting through" a food truck to persuade someone was
  the intent. Another gap: the rules don't say whether the Tier-as-base rule
  is limited to Body-ish uses.
- **Candidate tags:** `Pies`, `Food`, `Cooking`, `Performance`,
  `Persuasion`, `Kitchen` (her truck's galley is actually used), `Create`,
  `Plant` (fruit filling), `Fire` (baking). That's **9 candidates.**
- **Applied (cap 5):** `Pies` + `Food` + `Cooking` + `Performance` + `Kitchen` = **5**.
- **Pool: 3 + 5 = 8 dice.** Expected successes ≈ 4.

**How it plays out:** in the NPC-track system, a success takes **1 point**
from the target's track. A nine-generation feud is surely a major opponent,
say Face 4. So one good pie isn't enough. **She needs four courses.** A
starter, a soup, the pie, and a cheese board, each one a separate Face roll
at 8 dice. That's the best emergent comedy in the build, and it comes
straight from the rules as written.

It also raises the bigger issue with this build. Four tags go to waste, and
a narrow "joke" specialty stacks as high as any combat build. See Rules
Issues.

### 4. Cyberpunk: the hunger riot (Weird, then no roll)

The party's convoy is stuck in a Lower Chiba underpass. Two hundred hungry
kids from the flooded arcologies are rocking the lead van. They aren't
hostile, just starving. Winnie rings the Dinner Bell (they're hungry, so it
works with no roll: it's a certainty), and then conjures a feast from
nothing on the hood of the truck: noodles, dumplings, steamed buns, enough
for everyone.

- **Base:** Weird **3**. Straight off the table: *"Conjure a can of soda,
  or a feast from nothing — Food — Weird — Only reality."*
- **Candidate tags:** `Create`, `Feasts`, `Food`, `Plant`, `Animal`, `Fire`
  (it's hot), `Cooking`, `Kitchen`.
- **Applied (cap 5):** `Create` + `Feasts` + `Food` + `Plant` + `Animal` = **5**.
- **Pool: 3 + 5 = 8 dice.**
- **Then:** "using the effect is a separate roll." Here, using it is feeding
  starving people who want to be fed. **Nothing resists, so there's no roll.**
  The riot is over. A cheap button: 8 dice, no Face roll, no Body roll. The
  only real question is the GM's target number for "enough for two hundred."
  That lives in *Setting the Target Number*, which is still lipsum.
- **Exit:** a corp security van arrives anyway. Winnie climbs into the
  truck's walk-in fridge and steps out of a noodle-stall's cooler three
  blocks away (Pantry Step / Blink). No roll, because the Feat just works.
  Her party is still in the underpass.

## Rules Issues Found

1. **Nested specific tags stack on one object.** `Food` ⊃ `Ham`, plus the
   Form `Animal`, all describe a single ham. The Specific-tag rule says only
   "narrower... it stacks with them for a bigger bonus." Nothing stops a
   specific inside another specific (`Food` → `Pies`), so a themed caster
   can pick three or four near-synonyms and hit the cap on every in-theme
   roll.
2. **Is `Food` a legal Specific tag?** It's narrower than the Forms put
   together but not narrower than any one Form (it cuts across
   Plant/Animal/Water/Fire). The Rooster example uses it, but the rule text
   doesn't clearly allow it. The Spell table even treats "Food" as a
   *realm*, which confuses things further. There is no Food realm in Part III.
3. **Magic A overshoots the cap for a themed caster.** 12 tags and a 5-tag
   cap: in scenarios 3 and 4, 4–5 applicable tags went unused. Past about 7
   tags, extra tags buy *breadth*, but a themed caster spends them on
   more-of-the-same. The rules give no reason to spread out.
4. **The conjure-and-drop loophole.** Conjuring something heavy over a
   target might be one Weird roll or Weird then Body. "Weird makes an effect
   happen; using the effect is a separate roll" doesn't settle it when
   gravity does the "using." It decides whether low-Body casters can do
   damage at all.
5. **Feats that remove consequences also remove rolls.** Eat Anything (and
   Needs No Air, and Never Sleep) make "roll only when consequences are in
   doubt" collapse. Poison tests become auto-passes unless the GM moves the
   stakes onto someone else. That's fine, but worth saying outright.
6. **Custom Feats have no calibration beyond "bypasses one hard
   constraint."** Is "what you eat can hurt you" as big as "gravity"? I
   couldn't tell. There's no worked example of building a custom Feat.
7. **Narrowing a Feat earns nothing.** Pantry Step is strictly worse than
   Blink (it needs a cupboard at both ends). The rules neither reward nor
   forbid this. Flavour restrictions are free to players who don't mind
   them, so experienced players won't take them.
8. **Tags from one item category on another.** `Kitchen` is basically the
   Location tag `Workshop` on a Vehicle. The rule is "pick from the tag menu
   listed under that kind of item, or invent your own." There's no rule on
   borrowing a tag from another category's menu.
9. **Does Tier-as-base apply to Face/Mind through arbitrary items?**
   "Working from inside a fortress" suggests yes. Can a Face-1 caterer then
   persuade with Max(Tier 3 food truck, Face 1)? It's a tie for Winnie, but
   it would be a real exploit for someone else.
10. **Tier-as-base for thrown/launched objects.** The ham has no tier, the
    Ladle does. I used the Ladle. The rules don't say which object counts when
    one item launches another.
11. **Lifestyle as an unlimited ammo supply.** "Good tools" and "spending
    money" give her an endless supply of hams with no Stuff cost. That's
    probably fine, but it's the mundane side of Weird conjuring, for free.
12. **Low-Body characters draw on shared Coherence.** Body pool 1 means her
    *second* physical hit, and every one after, goes to the whole party's
    Coherence. The frailty of a Body-D build is partly paid for by
    teammates. Coherence has no refill rule (open `\todo`).
13. **"Your First Skill Adds +1" is redundant or confusing.** Every
    applicable skill already adds one die as a tag. The heading suggests
    only the *first* one counts, or that it gets a special bonus. I added
    both `Cooking` and `Performance` in scenario 3. If only the first skill
    counts, that roll is still 8 (another tag fills the slot), but the rule
    should say which reading is right.
14. **Realm definitions of Magic clash with themed magic.** "In Cyberpunk,
    Magic is hacking." "In Space, Magic is science." A food-witch has to
    invent what `Pies` means as hacking. The rules promise re-dressing but
    give no example for any non-elemental theme.
15. **Target numbers are lipsum**, so I can't judge whether 7–8 dice is
    overkill or just competent. Expected successes are ~3.5–4.

## Reflection on the Build Process

### What seemed good

- **"What resists?" is excellent for comedy.** The pie is Face because
  *the grudge* resists. That one sentence gave a joke concept real
  mechanical weight, and it did it by the same rule a fireball uses. No
  special food subsystem needed.
- **ABCD with Body at D was a real, meaningful choice.** Body 1 and a Body
  pool of 1 made Winnie feel like a fragile grandmother. The rules took the
  character concept seriously.
- **Stuff budgets are easy to audit.** 7 points, 4 tags, max Tier 3, and the
  Lifestyle is automatic. It all fit on a napkin, and the "never above your
  rank" rule meant I never had to second-guess a purchase.
- **Stats from Objects rescues the frail specialist.** The Long Ladle
  (Tier 2) is the kind of thing a caterer would really own, and it turned
  base 1 into 2 in a way that makes sense in the fiction.
- **The NPC track system makes the feud scene great.** Needing four courses
  to beat a Face-4 feud is funny *and* well paced. It came straight from the
  rules without any fudging.

### What seemed bad

- **Theme is free, so there's no cost to being a "joke" character.** The
  seed asks for a specialty that "shouldn't be useful but somehow is." Under
  these rules it's *never* less useful: a food theme reaches the cap as
  easily as a war-mage does. That's generous, but it removes the underdog
  arc the concept wants. Winnie is just a competent caster with a funny hat.
- **Magic A's 12 tags overshoot the 5-tag cap.** In three of four
  scenarios I had 6–9 candidate tags and threw the extras away. Magic A
  ends up buying Weird 3 and Feats, while most of its tag count sits idle
  for a focused caster.
- **Stacking near-synonyms** (`Food`/`Ham`/`Animal`) feels like cheating
  and is fully legal.
- **Body pool 1 is a Coherence tax on the party.** It's fine as fiction, but
  it quietly moves the cost of the build's weakness onto teammates.

### What was cool

- **The book's own Food rows in the spell table** are a great design
  touch. They told me exactly which Stat each of my signature moves uses,
  and they're funny.
- **Eat Anything as a custom Feat.** The "bypass one hard constraint"
  framing made it easy to invent a Feat that fits the character and still
  feels balanced. It also produced the scenario 2 insight: a Feat can turn
  a personal-risk roll into a race to protect someone else.
- **Speak with Animals + a butcher's daughter** is dark comedy the Feat
  list gave me for free. Pigs who know she's carrying ham are great
  roleplay.
- **Cross-realm re-skinning of the truck** (chuck-wagon → food truck →
  supply shuttle) is exactly the fantasy of the game.

### What was disappointing

- **The realm chapters are almost entirely lipsum.** "Magic is hacking"
  gave me nothing to go on for how food magic looks in the sprawl. Every
  scenario outside Fantasy was my own invention.
- **The Weird-vs-Body boundary broke on the first creative thing I tried**
  (the cheese drop). For a rule the book calls "the single most common point
  of confusion," the edge-case table doesn't cover the most obvious
  low-Body exploit.
- **There's no target-number guidance**, so I can't tell whether 8 dice on
  a feud-ending pie is heroic or routine.
- **Narrowing a Feat (Pantry Step) gives nothing back.** I wanted a
  flavourful limit to buy me something.

### What was unclear

- Whether `Food` counts as "more specific" than the Forms.
- Whether a Specific tag nested in another Specific stacks (as written: yes).
- Whether conjuring something that then falls on a target is one Weird roll
  or Weird then Body.
- Whether an item's tier can be the base for Face/Mind actions "through" a
  vehicle or location, and which object's tier counts when one item
  launches another.
- Whether a Vehicle can carry a tag from the Location menu (`Kitchen` ≈
  `Workshop`).
- What "Your First Skill Adds +1" adds beyond the ordinary tag rule.
- Whether a Stuff Feat's item costs Stuff Points.
- How a themed magic re-dresses under a realm's "Magic is X" definition.

### Recommendations to improve the system

1. **Add a "one object, one tag per layer" rule for Magic.** When several
   tags describe the *same* target in nested terms (`Animal` ⊃ `Food` ⊃
   `Ham`), only the narrowest one plus one Technique counts. Or cap
   Specific tags at one per roll. That keeps a theme flavourful without
   letting synonyms fill the cap automatically, and it makes Magic A's 12
   tags buy real *breadth*.
2. **Add two rows to the spell table: "Drop a conjured anvil on a guard"
   and "Hurl a conjured ham."** Rule that conjuring is Weird but anything
   that *strikes* a creature is Body, even with gravity as the middle-man.
   Better still, add a sentence: "Weird never deals harm; the harm is always
   a second roll against what resists it." That closes the loophole and
   keeps "get buff" meaningful.
3. **Say whether Tier-as-base works for Face and Mind** (and name the
   object when one item launches another). Suggestion: an item supplies the
   base only for the Stat its category naturally serves (Weapon/Vehicle →
   Body, Gadget → its one trick's Stat, Location → whatever it's built for).
4. **Give Feats a limitation dial.** A GM-approved restriction (Pantry Step
   needs a cupboard at both ends) could let the player add one extra tag
   slot, or a second use, or put the Feat on a cheaper column. That rewards
   flavourful restrictions.
5. **Clean up or merge "Your First Skill Adds +1."** As written it only
   restates the tag rule, and its heading suggests a special bonus that
   doesn't exist.
6. **Write one worked example per realm of a non-elemental magic
   re-dressing** (e.g. "food magic in Cyberpunk: synth-flavour hacking,
   vat-grown conjuring"). Also clarify that "Food" in the spell table is an
   example theme or realm and not a Part III setting.
7. **Consider a per-character Coherence draw limit, or a Coherence refill
   rule**, so a Body-D build can't lean on the shared pool without limit.
