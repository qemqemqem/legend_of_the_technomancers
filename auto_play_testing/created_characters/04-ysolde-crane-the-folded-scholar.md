# Ysolde Crane, the Folded Scholar

*Built by: subagent, seed: A "fucking wizard" (Magic at A) who is physically frail. Test how far pure magic carries someone, and lean into the Weird stat and the "what resists?" edge cases., 2026-09-23.*

*Rules source: `rulebook.tex` + `chapters/`.*

## Concept

Ysolde was born with lungs like wet paper and legs that never took her weight.
She spent her childhood propped up in a bed in a university infirmary. She
couldn't walk to the library, so she taught herself to reach into it: first
she folded the space between her pillow and the shelf, then the space between
the infirmary and the lecture hall. By sixteen she held three chairs of
thaumaturgy and still couldn't climb a flight of stairs. A standing ward
breathes for her. She rides a wheeled chair she rebuilt herself, and she
never goes around anything she can go *through*. She's brilliant, frightening
and fragile, and she knows that one good shove would kill her.

## Four Scenarios

### 1. High Fantasy: the collapsed gallery (Weird vs. Body)

A dwarven mine gallery caves in. A slab of granite the size of a cart pins the
party's fighter at the hips. Ysolde first uses See Through Solid Matter to
find where the slab bears down, which needs **no roll** since it's a Feat.
Then she has two options.

**Option A, telekinesis (lift it):** the slab's weight resists, so this is
**Body**, per "Does that mean my wizard should work out and get buff?"
- **Base:** Body **1**
- **Tags:** `Control` = **1** (`Earth` isn't a tag she knows)
- **Pool: 2 dice.** If she's already lost her single Body point this world,
  it's **1 die**.

**Option B, fold it (teleport it ten feet down the tunnel):** "Teleport a
boulder instead of lifting it" is listed as **Weird**.
- **Base:** Weird **3**
- **Tags:** `Folding` + `Control` (moving matter) = **2**. `Precise` from the
  Astrolabe arguably fits (placing it exactly), but she isn't acting
  *through* the Astrolabe, and the GM ruled it doesn't *clearly* apply.
- **Pool: 5 dice.** ← **She takes this.**

The move-a-heavy-thing roll goes from 2 dice to 5 because she described it
differently. Weird also ignores pool penalties ("Weird has no pool and no
such penalty"), so the gap is widest exactly when she's hurt.

### 2. Cyberpunk: cracking the Aegis-Kline mainframe (Mind, at the cap)

In the sprawl her Magic re-skins as hacking. She jacks into a corporate
mainframe from a public terminal to pull a prisoner transfer order. What
resists: "Crack a corporate mainframe's security" is listed as **Mind**. The
terminal isn't hers and it's junk (Tier 1–2), so Max(Tier, Mind 4) is still
her own 4.

- **Base:** Mind **4**
- **Tags available:** `Hacking` (skill), `Perceive` (reading the architecture),
  `Control` (commanding the system), `Wards` (the security *is* a ward),
  `Magic` (in this realm, raw code), `Perception` (skill)… **6 tags that fit.**
- **Tags used:** `Hacking` + `Perceive` + `Control` + `Wards` + `Magic` =
  **5 (cap)**. `Perception` is cut.
- **Pool: 9 dice.** The biggest roll on her sheet.

**Consequence:** the GM had said up front that black ICE fries the meat. On a
failure the loss comes out of her **Body** pool (the fiction threatens her
body), even though she rolled Mind. She has **1** Body point. One failure
empties it: every Body roll after that is at −1, and her base-1 Body rolls
drop to *0 base dice*. A second failure goes to the party's **Coherence**.

### 3. Space Adventure: the boarding marine (Face vs. Weird, "what resists?")

Pirates board the survey ship. A marine in a hardsuit grabs her out of her
chair by the collar.

**Step 1, get out of the grip.** She has **Blink** ("out of a grapple").
Treated as **no roll**, because the Feat opens a door. If the GM wants a roll
anyway, "Teleport out of a grappler's hold" is listed as Weird: Weird 3 +
`Folding` = 4 dice. The rules don't say which applies (see Issues).

**Step 2, get rid of him.** Three ways to describe it, three different
answers from the book:

| Description | What resists (per RAW) | Base | Tags | Pool |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fold *him* out through the hull | His will to stay put (changing a creature = Face, like "teleport an unwilling guard into a cell") | Face 2 | `Folding`, `Control`, `Body` | **5** |
| Fold *the deck plate he's standing on* (and so him) to the outside of the hull | The plate doesn't resist. Only reality does | Weird 3 | `Folding`, `Control` | **5** |
| Throw him out the airlock with telekinesis | His mass and hardsuit (striking = Body) | Body 1 | `Control`, `Body` | **3** |

**She takes the deck-plate fold. Final pool: Weird 3 + `Folding` + `Control` =
5 dice.** The pools happen to match here because her Face is 2 and her Weird
is 3, but for a Face-1 wizard the environment-targeting trick is a free +2
base. The follow-up is also unclear. "Weird makes an effect happen; using the
effect is a separate roll", so is the marine getting sucked into vacuum a
second roll (Body? against his Body?), or does it just happen? Needs No Air
means she doesn't care that the compartment is now open to space.

### 4. High Fantasy: the Duke's confession (Face, and "one roll or two?")

The Duke of Harrowmere had his mother poisoned, and the party needs him to
say so in front of witnesses. Ysolde casts a phantasm of the dead dowager
standing behind his chair, whispering in his ear. What resists: his will and
his guilt. That's **Face**, by analogy with "fireball over the guards' heads
to make them run" (one roll, Face).

- **Base:** Face **2**
- **Tags:** `Image` (the phantasm) + `Mind` (working on his emotions) +
  `Deception` (it's a lie) + `Create` (conjuring the image from nothing) =
  **4**. `Intimidation` was argued and cut because it isn't clearly "menace".
- **Pool: 6 dice.**

**But:** the invisibility precedent ("Turning invisible is Weird. Sneaking past
the guards while invisible is whatever sneaking always is") says conjuring
the effect and using it are separate rolls. Read that way:
- Roll A, make the phantasm: Weird 3 + `Create` + `Image` = **5 dice**
- Roll B, break the Duke: Face 2 + `Image` + `Mind` + `Deception` = **5 dice**

Two 5-die rolls give her two chances to fail. One 6-die roll gives her one.
The table went with the single Face roll (6 dice), but the book supports both
readings.

## Things Noticed While Building

- **Nothing stops an item's tier from beating Weird.** "Base Stat = Max(Item
  Tier, Character Stat)" works on "whichever base Stat the action would
  normally use", and Weird is a base Stat. A Tier-4 Gadget ("a teleporter
  bracelet") or a Stuff-4 Tier-5 capstone would let someone roll Weird at 4 or
  5. That breaks the promise in the boulder FAQ that "Weird never rises above
  3, so strength still pays." Decide whether items can stand in for Weird.
- **Teleport beats telekinesis for any weak wizard.** The two FAQs sit next to
  each other and pull against each other. "Get buff to lift boulders" is
  undercut straight away by "or just teleport them at Weird." For Ysolde,
  any "move this heavy thing" roll is 2 dice as telekinesis and 5 as a fold.
  Players will learn to say "teleport" every time. Weird also ignores empty
  pools, so a hurt wizard has even more reason to do it.
- **Target the environment and "changing a creature is Face" stops
  applying.** Teleporting a person is Face, but teleporting the floor, their
  armour, or the air around them is Weird, and the outcome is the same. The
  "ask what resists" test looks at the literal target, not the intent. The
  book should either add an intent clause ("if the goal is to relocate or
  change a creature, its will resists") or accept the loophole.
- **One roll or two?** The invisibility example splits "make the effect"
  (Weird) from "use the effect" (another Stat). The fireball-scare example is
  a single Face roll. Illusions, summons and teleport-then-fall all land in
  between. The rulebook needs a rule of thumb, e.g. "split only when the
  effect lasts beyond the action."
- **Feats overlap with spells.** A Magic-4 wizard with a teleport tag can
  already teleport short distances on a Weird roll. The Blink Feat is never
  said to be roll-free, or to use a particular Stat. Fly says "roll only when
  something about the flight is in doubt", but Blink has no such line. If
  Blink still needs a Weird roll, it's worth nothing to a wizard. If it
  doesn't, it's a strictly better version of a Weird roll. Either way, say so.
- **What counts as a Specific tag is unclear.** "More specific in application
  than the Techniques and Forms." `Folding` (space/teleportation) isn't a
  subset of any Form, since Ars Magica has no space Form. Is it allowed? It
  is also *broader* in practice than most Forms, because it applies to every
  teleport. `Healing` stacking with `Create` + `Body` gives three dice for one
  concept, which the rules call intended ("stacks with them for a bigger
  bonus"), but it makes Specific tags an easy route to the cap.
- **Cyberpunk stacks Hacking twice.** In the sprawl Magic *is* hacking, so the
  `Hacking` skill, the `Magic` Form (as "code"), `Control` and `Perceive` all
  apply to every system intrusion. Ysolde reaches the 5-tag cap without
  trying. The realm chapter is still lorem ipsum, so there's no guidance on
  how Forms re-skin or whether the Hacking skill and hacking-magic are meant
  to stack.
- **A pool of 0 or fewer dice is undefined.** With Body 1 and an empty Body
  pool, a tagless Body roll is 1 − 1 = **0 dice**. The intro promises
  "somewhere between 1 and 10 dice." Is a 0-die roll an automatic failure?
  Does she roll 1 die anyway? Frail characters hit this all the time.
- **Weird has no pool, but Weird failures still cost something.** Failed Weird
  rolls get paid from whatever pool "the fiction threatens," usually Body or
  Mind. A wizard's best Stat is therefore never protected by its own pool,
  and a Body-1 wizard runs through her one Body point and then drains the
  shared **Coherence** pool. That's fine as drama, but in practice the party
  is paying for the frail character. Coherence refill is still a `\todo`.
- **Magic healing could undo the consequence economy.** "Heal a willing ally's
  wound" is Weird. Ysolde rolls Weird 3 + `Healing` + `Create` + `Body` +
  `Medicine` = 7 dice to heal, and nothing limits how often she can do it.
  Recovery "follows what happens in the story" at GM discretion, but the book
  never says whether a successful heal refills Body pools. If it does, a
  healer wizard erases the Body track.
- **Reshaping your own body is Weird, but it's unclear what that gets you.**
  Can she make herself strong for a scene and then roll with `Strong`? Body
  tags are "inherent" and come from rank. Temporary, self-granted tags or Stat
  boosts from magic aren't covered at all.
- **The chair lends her its Body.** Max(Tier 2, Body 1) means her Tier-2
  wheeled chair is stronger than she is. That's charming, but the edges of
  "acting *through*" an item are fuzzy. Does ramming a door in the chair
  count? Dodging a blow while seated? Probably yes, and then the chair *is*
  her Body for most physical actions.
- **Magic A buys breadth more than power.** Because of the 5-tag cap, 12 tags
  vs. Magic B's 7 mostly means more *different* rolls reach the cap. Weird 3
  vs. 2 is only +1 on a narrow set of rolls. Ysolde's best roll (9 dice)
  comes from Mind 4, which Skills paid for, not from Magic. A "fucking
  wizard" ends up about as good at raw spellcasting as a Magic-B character
  with better Stats, and Body D + Magic A can never reach a Stat of 5.
  That's probably fine (the matrix says "tags to spare"), but the
  headline promise is bigger than the math behind it.

## Reflection on the Build Process

### What seemed good

- **Creation is fast and easy to check.** Once the four letters were down,
  every number came straight off the four column tables: 4 Stat Points, 10
  skills, 12 magic tags, Weird 3, 4 Stuff Points and 2 Stuff Tags, 3 Feats. I
  didn't have to redo any arithmetic. Because every column uses the same
  "rank → points / tags / Feats" layout, you learn how to read one table and
  can read them all.
- **Tags are one unit everywhere.** Skills, Body Tags, Stuff Tags and Magic
  tags all add one die each, under one 5-tag cap. When building a roll I never
  had to ask where a tag came from, only whether it fit. That keeps rolls
  quick even with a 12-tag wizard.
- **"What resists?" usually gets you to an answer quickly.** The mainframe
  (Mind), the Duke (Face) and lifting the slab (Body) were settled in seconds.
  The *Spells and Their Stats* table is the most useful page in the Magic
  chapter. It answers the question players actually ask.
- **Body D really feels frail.** Body 1, no tags, a Body pool of 1. The sheet
  says "one shove kills her" and the rules back that up. The priority system
  doesn't let a dump column quietly get patched later.
- **The per-column Feat rule gives a mixed build a real reward.** Skills B
  paying for a Feat (See Through Solid Matter) made the second column feel
  like more than just Stat Points.

### What seemed bad

- **Weird is the easiest Stat to exploit and the least guarded.** Using it
  depends on how the player *describes* the action, not on what happens. A
  frail wizard's best play every time is to describe the action as a
  teleport. Twice in four scenarios I compared a 2–3 die honest pool with a
  5-die Weird pool that did the same thing. Weird also has no pool and takes
  no empty-pool penalty, so it's the one Stat you can lean on at no cost to
  itself.
- **The two boulder FAQs cancel each other out.** "Get buff to lift
  boulders" is followed straight away by "or just teleport them." The
  "Weird tops out at 3" safety valve only holds if items can't lend their
  tier to Weird, and the book never says they can't.
- **Frailty has no floor.** Body 1 plus an empty pool gives a 0-die base, and
  the book doesn't say what that means. After one failure the frail character
  starts draining the party's Coherence. Glass cannons end up paid for by
  shared resources, and the refill rule for that resource isn't written yet.
- **Magic A offers less than its label suggests.** Because of the 5-tag cap,
  going from 7 to 12 tags mostly buys breadth. Weird 3 is lower than her Mind
  4, and her best roll came from Mind, which Skills paid for. Magic A + Body D
  also rules out any Stat 5, so the "fucking wizard" never gets the biggest
  base in the game.
- **Stuff C is dull.** 4 points, Max Tier 2, 2 tags gives exactly two
  cheap items with one tag each. I didn't make any real decisions there.

### What was cool

- **The Tier-2 chair is stronger than she is.** Max(Tier, Stat) produced a
  lovely bit of emergent characterisation: her wheelchair lends her more
  Body than her own body has. That's the item rule doing its best work.
- **Magic re-skinning into hacking.** Carrying the same 12 tags into the
  sprawl and having them read as `Perceive the architecture`, `Control the
  system` and `Wards` = ICE felt great. It's the realm-hopping promise
  delivered at the tag level.
- **Needs No Air as a medical Feat.** A Feat written as "bypass one hard
  constraint" turned into a character-defining disability aid. The Feat
  catalogue is generic enough to be retold and specific enough to play.
- **The edge-case table invites the right arguments.** The marine scenario
  (fold him = Face, fold the floor = Weird) was the most fun I had. The
  system gets players asking sharp, fiction-first questions, even when it
  doesn't yet answer them.

### What was disappointing

- **Feats fell flat for a wizard.** Blink duplicates what her Weird-plus-
  `Folding` teleport already does, and the Feat text never says whether it
  needs a roll. A wizard's Magic Feats should feel like things her spells
  *can't* do. Instead the catalogue overlaps with casting.
- **Two Feats and 12 tags, but no spell-level identity.** There's no way to
  have a signature spell that's better than a generic tag combination.
  Everything is "Stat + tags that fit." That's clean, but at Magic A it feels
  flat. The wizard is broad, not deep.
- **The realm chapters are placeholder.** Every question I had about realm
  specifics (does `Magic` the Form mean code in cyberpunk? What's Weird enough
  in space?) runs into lorem ipsum. The Magic chapter points to "each realm's
  Magic section for local guidance," and none of that guidance exists yet.
- **Appeal to the Gods is a Feat anyone can use but has no mechanic.** It
  would have been a natural thing for a frail character to try in High
  Fantasy, and I couldn't use it.

### What was unclear

- Whether **Max(Tier, Stat)** works for **Weird**.
- Whether "ask what resists" means the **literal target** or the **intended
  outcome** (fold the floor vs. fold the man).
- When a spell is **one roll vs. two** (invisibility splits; the fireball
  scare doesn't).
- Whether **Action Feats** (Blink, Walk Through Walls) need a roll, and on
  what Stat.
- What a **Specific tag** must be "more specific than". Can it name a domain
  no Form covers (space, time, luck)?
- What a **0-or-fewer-dice** pool does.
- Whether **magical healing** restores Body pools, and how often.
- What **self-transformation** (Weird) can give you mechanically: tags? Stats?
  Nothing?
- Where "acting *through*" an item stops: a chair while dodging, a vehicle
  while ramming.
- Whether the **Hacking skill** and **hacking-as-Magic** are meant to stack in
  cyberpunk.

### Recommendations to improve the system

1. **Add an intent clause to "what resists?" and fence off Weird.**
   Suggested wording: *"If the purpose of the effect is to move, harm or change
   a creature or a heavy object, use the Stat that would resist doing it
   directly, however you describe it."* Also say outright that **items can't
   supply Weird**, or cap Max(Tier, Weird) at 3. Together these close the
   teleport-everything and fold-the-floor loopholes, and they make the "get
   buff" FAQ true again.
2. **Set a floor for frail characters.** For example: *"A pool can never drop
   below 1 die"*, or *"at 0 dice, roll 1 die and a 6 counts only as a partial
   success."* Then write the Coherence refill rule so the party knows what
   carrying a glass cannon costs. As it stands, Body D + an empty pool is an
   undefined state that frail builds hit in their first fight.
3. **Say whether Feats need a roll, and make Magic Feats different from
   casting.** Add one line to every Action Feat: *"No roll unless the result
   is in doubt; if rolled, use Weird (Magic source) or [Stat] (other
   sources)."* Then either keep teleport-type Feats out of Magic-granted
   choices, or make Magic Feats cover what Weird can't (e.g. "one Weird effect
   of your choice is always a full success"), so a wizard's Feats add
   something new.
4. **Give "one roll or two?" a single rule of thumb**, e.g. *"Roll the effect
   separately only if it lasts past the action that uses it."* The phantasm
   then becomes one roll, and invisibility-then-sneak stays two.
5. **Say what magic can do to pools and to your own sheet.** For example:
   healing restores one point of a pool per success, once per scene per
   target, and self-transformation can grant one temporary tag for a scene
   but never a Stat change. This protects the consequence economy from a
   Weird-healer.
6. **Make Magic A worth taking.** Options: let Magic A raise the tag cap to 6
   on Magic rolls only; give Magic A 1 Stat Point; or let a Magic-4 caster
   name one "signature" tag combination that counts as one extra tag. Any of
   these makes the top priority feel like the top priority, without breaking
   the unified tag system.
7. **Make low Stuff ranks more interesting.** At Stuff 2, either give 3 tags
   instead of 2, or allow one Tier-1 "trinket" for free. Right now Stuff C
   produces the same two-item sheet every time.
8. **Write realm guidance on re-skinning Forms** (especially `Magic`, `Mind`
   and `Image` in cyberpunk and space), and say whether realm-skill overlap
   (Hacking skill vs. hacking magic) is meant to stack.
