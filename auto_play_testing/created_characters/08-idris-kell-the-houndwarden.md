# Idris Kell, the Houndwarden (and Tallow)

*Built by: subagent, seed: Someone defined by a partner that isn't them — a beloved mount, a loyal hound, a bonded mech, a ship with a personality. The rules have no explicit companion system; figure out how to build one out of what's there (Stuff? Magic? Feats?) and report where it strains, 2026-09-23.*

*Rules source: `rulebook.tex` + `chapters/`.*

## Concept

Idris was kennelmaster to Lord Aubrec of the Greyfells. He was a thin,
quiet man who slept in the straw with the hounds because he liked them
better than the hall. When Aubrec's cousins burned the manor, only one
animal got out: **Tallow**, the last of the Greyfell dire-hounds. She's
pony-sized, scarred and grey around the muzzle, and she carried him out of
the fire on her back. Since then they've never been apart. When the wound
in the world opened, she went through first and he followed her.

Tallow is the protagonist of this pair. Idris is frail, awkward with people
and middling at most things. What he's good at is *Tallow*: riding her,
reading her, mending her, and hearing what she says. He has a hedge-magic
bond with her that lets him feel what she smells. The design question was
simple: can this rulebook make the dog the best thing on the sheet?

## Priorities

| Column | Letter | Rank |
|---|---|---|
| Stuff  | **A** | 4 |
| Magic  | **B** | 3 |
| Skills | **C** | 2 |
| Body   | **D** | 1 |

Why this split: the rulebook has no Companion entry. The only catalogue line
that can be a living creature is the **Vehicle** ("Warhorse ●●", "a griffon,
a great elk, a dire wolf" in High Fantasy). So Tallow has to be Stuff, and
Stuff A is the only way to make her Tier 4, then Tier 5 with the capstone.
Magic B pays for the *bond*. Body D is on purpose: Idris is frail, and
Tallow is the strong one.

## Stats

Stat Points: Body rank 1 → **2**, Skills rank 2 → **1**. Total **3**. All
Stats start at 1.

| Stat | Raise | Cost | Running total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body 1 → 2 | +1 | 1 | 1 |
| Mind 1 → 3 | +1, +1 | 2 | 3 ✔ |
| Face 1 → 1 | — | 0 | 3 |

- **Body 2 · Mind 3 · Face 1**
- **Weird 2** (Magic 3 − 1)
- **Pools:** Body 2 / Mind 3 / Face 1. Weird has no pool.

No 5th pip, so the double-cost rule never came up. With only 3 points it
couldn't have. A Stat of 5 costs 5 points, so a Body-D character only gets
one with Skills A (2 + 4 = 6 points → one Stat at 5, plus 1 point left
over). With Skills B (2 + 2 = 4) the best is a single 4. The double-cost
pip only matters for builds with Body or Skills at A or B.

Tallow is spread across **two sheet lines and five tags** (Vehicle, Armour,

line (`Tallow`, `Scent`). Nothing in the rules says any of this should be
one entry.

**Re-skins** (Stuff re-kits on arrival):

| Realm | Tallow | Barding | Bow | Kennel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| High Fantasy | Pony-sized dire-hound | Leather-and-chain coat | Horn bow | Stone pens in a ravine |
| Cyberpunk | Quadruped K9 security frame, running a neural map of a real dog | Ballistic plating | Compact crossbow-pistol | A shuttered vet clinic |

**Stuff Feats (2).** Both items are free props, following the Tamsin
precedent. The rules don't say whether a Feat's item costs points.

- **Feat: Comes When Called** *(custom, built with the GM under "work with
  your GM to build one")*: **the Silver Whistle.** Continuous. Tallow can't
  be permanently separated from him within a realm. If she's taken,
  impounded or driven off, she finds her way back to him by the next scene.
  The one hard constraint it bypasses is **separation or distance**.
  *Deliberately overlaps "Losing Your Stuff"; see Rules Issues.*
- **Feat: Needs No Air** (Item source: *Rebreather*): **the Kennelmaster's
  Veil**, a fine wet-mesh mask. Idris can't suffocate. *Tallow gets no
  benefit, because Feats belong to the character. See Scenario 2.*

**Lifestyle: Rich**, forced by Stuff rank 4. A kennel-servant who "sleeps in
the straw" now has "the finest version of every tool… and a wallet flush
with cash." I explained it as Lord Aubrec's hunt-purse, which Idris carries
and can't bring himself to spend. But the concept didn't want it, and the
rules don't let me decline it.

## Four Scenarios

### 1. High Fantasy: running the gorge (Body, through Tallow)

Aubrec's cousins send riders with crossbows after them. Idris puts Tallow at
the Hag's Gorge. The gap is too wide for a horse, and she jumps it with him
flattened on her back.

- **What resists:** gravity and the gap → **Body**.
- **Base:** acting *through* a Vehicle → Max(Tier **5**, Body 2) = **5**.
- **Candidate tags (7):** `Piloting` (riding), `Animal Handling`, `Fast`,
  `Rugged` (scree on the far lip), `Tallow`, `Control` (urging her with the
  bond), `Animal`.
- **Applied (cap 5):** `Piloting` + `Fast` + `Rugged` + `Animal Handling` + `Tallow` = **5**.
- **Pool: 10 dice**, the game's ceiling. His own Body of 2 doesn't matter at all.

**1b. Shooting back from the saddle.** On landing, he turns and looses at
the lead rider.
- **Base:** through the bow → Max(Tier 2, Body 2) = **2**. Can he use
  Tallow's Tier 5 instead? He's riding her while he shoots. The rules don't
  say what happens when you act through **two items at once**. I ruled that
  the item doing the thing (the bow) supplies the base.
- **Tags:** `Archery` + `Piercing` (the rider is in mail) = 2. `Piloting` for a mounted shot? I'd allow it = **3**.
- **Pool: 5 dice.** Idris himself is half as good as the dog. That's right
  for the concept, but it's steep: 10 dice to dodge versus 5 to hit back.

*Consequence check:* if the GM's response is a crossbow bolt, it can only
come out of **Idris's Body pool (2)**. Tallow has no pool, and her Tier-3
barding does nothing to protect her, because armour is never something you
"act through." Two bad exchanges and the rider is at 0 and bleeding into
Coherence, while the dog who carries 10 dice has taken no harm at all.

### 2. Space Adventure: the stowaway in the ducts (Mind, companion acting alone)

A saboteur is hiding somewhere in the ventilation of a mining station.
Tallow, now a six-legged crawler, is the only one who fits in the ducts.
Idris sits outside with his eyes shut and follows her nose through the bond.

- **What resists:** a hidden person → **Mind**.
- **Base, and the big question: is sending Tallow "acting through" her?**
  - *Generous reading:* yes. Idris's will is working through an owned
    thing, just like "a cutting deck" in *Stats from Objects*. Max(Tier 5, Mind 3) = **5**.
  - *Strict reading:* Vehicle tier covers *vehicle* things (moving,
    carrying, ramming). Searching isn't one, so the base is Mind **3**.
- **Tags:** `Keen Nose` + `Scent` + `Perceive` + `Perception` + `Tallow` = **5** (cap). `Survival` was left out.
- **Pool: 10 dice (generous) or 8 dice (strict).** Both are very high.
  Three tags (`Keen Nose`, `Scent`, `Perceive`) all describe one fact: she
  smells well. Nothing in the rules stops that stacking.
- **Whose skills?** `Perception` is Idris's skill, but Tallow is doing the
  noticing. "Only players roll" settles *who* rolls, but not *whose tags*
  count when the companion acts alone.

**Follow-up:** the saboteur blows a vent panel and the duct section
decompresses. Idris climbs in after her and **doesn't roll**, thanks to
Needs No Air. Tallow is fine only because *this realm's re-skin* made her a
machine. If the same thing happened in a sealed dwarf-mine in High Fantasy
(bad air), Idris's Feat would save Idris and do nothing for the dog. The
character's defining partner can't benefit from the character's Feats.

**If the roll fails:** the GM has the saboteur put a cutting torch into
Tallow. What happens on the sheet? Either (a) a point comes out of Idris's
pools (Mind, as "bond-shock"? Body?), or (b) Tallow is "lost" under
*Losing Your Stuff* until the next realm. The first is arbitrary and the
second kills the character concept for the rest of the world. There's
nothing in between, like "she's hurt: −1 Tier until mended."

### 3. Cyberpunk: the impound (Face)

At a corporate checkpoint, a sergeant scans Tallow (a K9 security frame
here), flags her as *unlicensed military hardware*, and puts her in a
cage-truck. Idris has to get her back.

- **First try: the purse.** Lifestyle is Rich. Can he just pay? The rules
  say cash is "within reach" with no roll, but nothing about using it to
  get past a person. The GM rules the sergeant is corp-loyal, so it's a roll.
- **What resists:** the sergeant's sense of duty → **Face**.
- **Base:** Face **1**. Could Tallow supply the base ("she sits, tilts her
  head, whimpers")? The *Stats from Objects* rule doesn't restrict which
  Stat an item can stand in for, so read literally a Tier-5 dog gives
  **Face 5**. I didn't allow it. See Rules Issues.
- **Tags:** he has no social skills. The best he can do is quietly work the
  bond into the sergeant's head: `Control` + `Mind` = **2**. `Animal
  Handling` and `Tallow` don't apply, because the sergeant is the one
  resisting.
- **Pool: 3 dice.** His **Face pool is 1**, so one failure empties it and
  every later Face roll drops to 2 dice.

**Comes When Called** is the real answer here. If he fails, Tallow slips
the cage and finds him by the next scene. So the Feat quietly makes this
whole conflict low-stakes. That fits what the Feat is for, but it shows how
a custom companion Feat undoes the one rule (*Losing Your Stuff*) that gave
the companion any vulnerability.

### 4. Cyberpunk: mending her (Weird vs Mind, and re-skinning changes the answer)

During the escape a railgun slug tears through Tallow's flank plating. In a
shuttered vet clinic (the Kennel, re-skinned), Idris works to bring her back.

- **What resists?** It depends on the realm's re-skin:
  - *High Fantasy (flesh-and-blood dog):* "Heal a willing ally's wound" → **Weird 2**.
  - *Cyberpunk (machine):* is repairing a damaged frame "a system" (Mind),
    or does a willing machine "not resist" (Weird)? The rules only cover
    flesh.
- **Weird option:** Weird 2 + `Create` (restore to whole) + `Tallow` +
  `Animal` (still an animal? magic tags "re-dress themselves") + `Medicine`
  (does vet medicine cover servos? I gave it to him, since she's still a
  dog underneath) = **6 dice**.
- **Mind option:** base = working inside the Kennel: Max(Location Tier 3,
  Mind 3) = 3. Tags: `Create` + `Tallow` + `Staffed` (the lads hold the
  light) = 3; he has no `Craft`. = **6 dice**.
- **Pool: 6 dice either way.** I took Weird because it has **no pool**. If
  his Mind pool were empty, only the Mind roll would take the −1 die.
- **What does success restore?** Nothing on the sheet, because Tallow has
  no track to refill. If the GM had ruled her "lost" after the railgun hit,
  can a healing roll undo *Losing Your Stuff*? The rules don't say. In
  practice this whole scene exists only in the fiction, and the sheet shows
  no difference between a healthy Tallow and a dying one.

## Rules Issues Found

**Gaps (the companion problem)**

1. **No Companion or Ally entry.** Vehicle is the only catalogue line that
   can be a creature, and every Vehicle blurb talks about hauling cargo and
   passengers. A hound that isn't a mount (a scout dog, a familiar, a drone
   buddy) has nowhere clean to go. Gadget ("does one specific thing") is
   too narrow, and Weapon and Location are nonsense.
2. **Companions can't be hurt in a proportionate way.** Items have no pools.
   The only options are "take it from the PC's pool" or "the item is lost
   for the rest of the world." There's no *wounded* state, no tier loss,
   and no tag loss.
3. **Healing or repairing Stuff is undefined.** The Magic chapter defines
   healing a willing *creature*, but not what a success restores for
   something with no pool, or whether it can reverse a "lost" ruling.
4. **Whose tags apply when a companion acts alone?** "Only players roll"
   answers *who* rolls. It doesn't answer whether Idris's `Perception`
   counts when Tallow does the searching.
5. **Armour on a companion does nothing.** Armour isn't "acted through,"
   and consequences land on the PC's pools, so Tallow's Tier-3 barding
   (3 points) has no mechanical effect unless its tag happens to apply to a roll.
6. **Feats belong to the PC, never the companion.** Needs No Air, Fly and
   Never Sleep can't be given to the partner, even when the partner is the
   concept.

**Ambiguities**

7. **"Acting through" has no limits.** *Stats from Objects* doesn't limit
   *which* Stat an item can replace. Read literally, a Tier-5 companion
   gives base 5 for Body (riding), Mind (her nose) and even Face (puppy
   eyes). That makes the companion a universal Stat, which is a big
   exploit for a Stuff-A build.
8. **Two items at once** (shooting a Tier-2 bow from a Tier-5 mount): which
   tier is the base? Nothing says.
9. **A Specific magic tag naming one creature (`Tallow`).** It's
   technically "more specific." In practice it adds +1 die to every roll
   that involves her, which is most of this character's rolls.
10. **Re-skinning changes which Stat resists.** Healing a dog is Weird, but
    fixing a drone might be Mind. The same sheet line, re-kitted, now
    triggers different rules.
11. **Re-skinning a *beloved* creature.** The Advancement chapter's own
    example is "a mount becomes a motorbike." Keeping Tallow a dog costs a
    Persistence Point, and the book never says how you earn one.
12. **Invented skills have no default Stat** (`Animal Handling`), and the
    catalogue doesn't include a skill that its own Feat text references.
13. **Can Lifestyle solve problems?** Rich gives a "wallet flush with cash"
    but no tag and no rule for bribes or purchases.
14. **Do Stuff Feats cost points?** Still unanswered (the Whistle and Veil
    are free props here, following the earlier character's precedent).

**Balance concerns**

15. **Double-dipping on one fictional fact.** Riding Tallow gets
    `Piloting` + `Animal Handling` + `Fast` + `Tallow` + `Control`.
    Tracking gets `Keen Nose` + `Scent` + `Perceive` + `Perception` + `Tallow`.
    One companion across three columns reaches the 5-tag cap on its own.
    Combined with the Tier-5 base, **both** of the concept's signature rolls are 10 dice.
16. **Very lopsided build.** 10 dice riding and tracking, 5 shooting, 3
    talking. His pools are 2/3/1, so four failures of the right kind put
    him into Coherence. The dog carries the dice and the frail handler
    takes every hit. That's *thematically* right, but in a fight the GM
    can only ever hurt the wrong member of the pair.
17. **Custom companion Feats can undo the only vulnerability.** Comes When
    Called turns "Losing Your Stuff" into a scene-long inconvenience. The
    Feat guidance ("bypasses exactly one hard constraint") didn't stop it,
    because *separation* counts as one constraint.
18. **Forced Lifestyle.** Stuff A forces Rich even when the concept is
    "poor servant with one priceless animal."
19. **Target numbers are still lorem ipsum**, so there's no way to know
    whether 3 dice is "fails sometimes" or "never succeeds."

## Reflection on the Build Process

**What seemed good**

- **Max(Tier, Stat) is excellent for this concept.** A frail rider on a
  mighty beast rolls the beast's number when the beast is doing the work,
  and his own pools stay small ("changes the dice you roll, not the size of
  your own consequence pools"). One sentence captures the fantasy of the
  frail rider on the mighty beast.
- **The ABCD grid pushed me toward a clear identity.** Body D plus Stuff A
  practically *wrote* "the dog is stronger than he is." The trade-offs felt real.
- **Specific magic tags are a great, flexible tool.** `Scent` and `Tallow`
  let the bond magic feel personal instead of generic.
- **"What resists?" usually gave a clear answer quickly** (the gorge jump,
  the duct search, the sergeant). It only got murky once Stuff was involved.
- **The budgets add up cleanly.** 12/7 Stuff, 7 skills, 7 magic tags and
  3 Stat Points were all easy to audit.

**What seemed bad**

- **The one thing the character is about has no rules.** I had to build
  Tallow out of a Vehicle, an Armour, an invented tag, a magic tag and a
  custom Feat, spread across three chapters, and the result still can't be
  hurt, healed or protected in a way the sheet shows.
- **Harm only ever lands on the PC.** Every consequence comes out of Idris's
  2/3/1 pools. The creature that does all the dangerous things never takes
  any damage. That breaks the fiction in the scenes that matter most for this concept.
- **Stacking tags on one fact is too easy.** With a companion that exists
  in three columns, each good trait (her nose, her speed) shows up two or
  three times. The 5-tag cap is the only brake, and it gets hit every time.
- **Lifestyle can't be declined.**

**What was cool**

- **Re-skinning Tallow** (dire-hound → K9 frame → EVA crawler) is a great
  table moment, and it keeps her identity (Speak with Animals still lets
  her talk in every realm, because her mind is still a dog's).
- **Speak with Animals makes a companion a character at no cost.** A
  talking dog with opinions, from one catalogue Feat.
- **The Tier-5 capstone on a living creature** reads perfectly: "the one
  hound every huntsman has heard of."
- **Persistence Point tattoos** fit this concept beautifully: a dog-shaped
  tattoo appears the day he refuses to let her become a motorbike.

**What was disappointing**

- **The scene I most wanted to play (Scenario 4, mending her) has no
  mechanical weight.** A success or a failure changes nothing on the sheet.
- **Weird dodges empty-pool penalties** (Scenario 4). The best way to heal
  your companion is the one Stat that can't be worn down.
- **The Stuff Feat list doesn't know about companions.** Every item-sourced
  Feat is a gadget on *you* (jetpack, rebreather, scanner). None of them
  are "your mount can fly" or "your hound never sleeps."
- **Idris himself is thin.** Once Tallow takes the 10-dice rolls, his
  own contribution is 3–5 dice and a small set of pools.

**What was unclear**

- Whether "acting through" an item has any limits on which Stat it can
  replace (Face through a dog?).
- Whose tags apply when the companion acts alone.
- Which base to use when acting through two items at once.
- Whether a Specific tag can name a single entity.
- Whether a machine's repair is Weird or Mind, and what a heal restores
  for Stuff.
- How Persistence Points are earned. It matters, because they're the only
  thing that keeps a beloved creature a creature.
- Whether Stuff Feats' source items cost points (still open from earlier builds).
- The default Stat of an invented skill.

**Recommendations to improve the system**

1. **Add a Companion entry to the Stuff catalogue (Tiers 2–4).** Give it a
   tag menu in the usual five paired weight classes (`Loyal`/`Fierce`,
   `Keen Nose`/`Watchful`, `Swift`/`Sturdy`, `Clever`/`Trained`,
   `Mount`/`Small`) and one sentence on how it acts: *when the companion
   does the work, the player rolls Max(Tier, the Stat the action uses)
   plus the companion's tags and any of the PC's skills that describe
   directing it.* Let `Mount` fold in what Vehicle does now.
2. **Give Stuff a damaged state between "fine" and "lost."** A consequence
   can **mark an item** (−1 Tier or one tag unusable until repaired or
   healed), and a second mark means it's lost as now. That gives
   companions, ships and mechs a way to be hurt, makes armour on a companion
   matter, and gives healing and repair something to restore.
3. **Limit "acting through."** An item's tier replaces a Stat only for what
   its category is *for*: Vehicle for movement, pursuit and ramming; Weapon
   for attacks; Location for actions that use the place; a Companion for
   what its tags describe. It should never replace Face unless the item is
   built for social use. Also say that with two items at once, the one
   doing the action supplies the base.
4. **Stop one fact counting several times.** For example: "a Specific tag
   can't name a single individual," or "two tags that describe the same
   trait count once." Otherwise any build centred on one thing (a
   companion, a ship, a signature weapon) reaches the cap for free.
5. **Let living companions re-skin within their kind** (a dog stays some
   kind of creature, or a creature-like machine) without a Persistence
   Point. Define how Persistence Points are earned.
6. **Let Stuff Feats target the item.** "Your Vehicle or Companion gains
   this Feat instead of you." Then Tallow can breathe in the dwarf-mine, or
   the ship can fly.
7. **Make Lifestyle optional downward** ("you may take a Lifestyle lower
   than your rank"), and say whether cash can stand in for a roll.
