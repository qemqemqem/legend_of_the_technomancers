# Ambrose Pell, the Prophet of the Open Door

*Built by: subagent, seed: A silver-tongued talker — grifter, diplomat, cult leader, or con artist. Someone who wins with Face, and who tries to talk their way through problems others would fight, 2026-09-23.*

*Rules source: `rulebook.tex` + `chapters/`.*

## Concept

Ambrose Pell ran a travelling tent-revival. He sold brass "keys to Heaven's
door" at a silver piece each and had a sermon for every town's particular
guilt. One stormy night he was promising his forty regulars that the Door
would open *tonight*, for the faithful, and the wound in the world tore open
right behind his pulpit.

His congregation walked through it singing, and he had to follow them. Now
they think he's a prophet. He knows he's a fraud. He isn't sure any more
whether the Door is a lie, and that scares him more than anything. He can't
afford to be wrong in front of them, so he talks, all the time. He never
throws a punch. Fighting is what happens when a sermon runs out.

The congregation travels with him as his Chapel. It's a patched
tent-cathedral in High Fantasy, a converted multiplex with neon crosses in
the sprawl, and the chapel ring of a decommissioned hospital ship in deep
space.

belongings (Stuff) and a little uncanny push (Magic).

All Stats start at 1.

- **Weird 1** (Magic 2 − 1)
- **Pools:** Body 1 / Mind 2 / Face 5. Weird has no pool.

One thrown bottle empties his Body pool, and after that every Body roll
loses a die. See Scenario 4.

## Body Tags (0, from Body rank 1)

None. The book says an ordinary body "doesn't do anything a Body Tag would
describe". He'd like to be `Striking`, but he isn't. He just looks like
someone you'd trust.

## Skills (14, from Skills rank 4)

**From the printed list (10):** `Persuasion`, `Deception`, `Intimidation`,
`Performance`, `Lore`, `Languages`, `Perception`, `Medicine` (faith-healing
is mostly real first aid), `Stealth`, `Athletics` (running away)

**Invented, with GM approval (4):** `Etiquette`, `Streetwise`,
`Sleight of Hand`, `Forgery`

Count: 10 + 4 = 14 ✔

## Skill Feats (2, from Skills rank 4)

- **Read Minds** (Action). *Skill source: Cold Reading.* He watches the
  pulse in your throat, where your eyes go, what your hands do, and he knows
  what you're afraid of before you do.
- **Turn Invisible** (Action). *Skill source: Master Misdirection.* He
  points at something, the room looks, and he's gone. It only works while he
  holds still. If he runs or shouts, they see him.

## Magic (3 tags, Weird 1, 0 Feats, from Magic rank 2)

The "uncanny push". In High Fantasy it's real, small sorcery that he tells
himself is stagecraft. In the sprawl it's AR overlays and neuro-marketing
hacks. In space it's a Force-style wave of the hand.

- **Form:** `Mind`. Emotions, fear, devotion.
- **Form:** `Image`. Appearances, never substance.
- **Specific:** `Halo`. A corona of light around the speaker, or a
  glowing Door behind him. Narrower than `Image` (only one kind of
  illusion), so it stacks with it.

There's no Technique tag. The rules say tags "are not a prerequisite or a
recipe for casting", so Forms on their own are legal.

## Four Scenarios

### 1. Space Adventure: the impounded ship

A Tessellate trade-envoy, Ssa-Vor, has impounded the party's shuttle. It's a
hive-diplomat with no language in common with anyone. Ambrose asks for an
audience. First he uses **Read Minds** (Cold Reading, applied to an alien's
fluttering mandibles). The Feat is an Action, and nothing in its text says
it needs a roll. He learns that Ssa-Vor is afraid its hive will think it was
swindled. So Ambrose offers to let it be *seen* to win: a public "fine" paid
in Chapel hymn-recordings, which the Tessellate prize.

- **Base:** Face **5**. The envoy's pride and suspicion resist.
- **Candidate tags (6):** `Persuasion`, `Languages` (he's picked up the
  trade-clicks), `Etiquette` (hive protocol), `Imposing` (the vestments),
  `Mind` (a Force-style soothing of its fear), `Deception` (the "fine" is
  worthless to him)
- **Applied: 5, at the cap.** He drops `Deception`, because the deal is
  actually honest-ish.
- **Pool: 10 dice.** Expect ~5 successes. Read Minds adds no dice, since a
  Feat isn't a tag. Whether it should lower the target is up to the GM, and
  the book doesn't say.

### 2. Cyberpunk: digging for dirt

Halcyon Biotech VP Daria Kwon is suing to have the Chapel (the multiplex)
condemned. Ambrose goes through the Ledger, which in the sprawl is an
encrypted confession app his flock logs into, looking for anyone who knows
something about Kwon.

- **What resists:** a secret buried in a lot of data → **Mind**.
- **Base:** acting through the Ledger → Max(Tier 2, Mind 2) = **2**. The
  item doesn't help, because the tier only ties his Stat.
- **Tags:** `Leverage` (the Ledger's own trick) + `Streetwise` (knows which
  names matter) + `Perception` (spotting the pattern) = **3**. The GM
  refuses the magic `Mind` Form: it's data, not a mind.
- **Pool: 5 dice.** He can do it, but it's shaky.

*The pivot a real player would make:* skip the data and phone Kwon's
assistant pretending to be Halcyon's crisis-PR firm. Now "what resists" is
the assistant's suspicion, which is **Face**: 5 + `Deception` +
`Persuasion` + `Etiquette` + `Streetwise` + `Leverage` = **10 dice**. The
rules mostly let the player pick the approach (see issues), so a Face-5
character turns almost any problem into a Face roll.

### 3. High Fantasy: the miracle at the plague-town

A plague town is ready to burn the Chapel as a witch's tent. An Inquisitor
stands at the back. Ambrose stops mid-sermon, spreads his arms, and a Door
of white light opens in the air behind the altar with a halo around his
head.

I ruled this as **two rolls**, following *"Weird makes an effect happen;
using the effect is a separate roll"* (the invisibility precedent):

**3a. Make the Door appear.** Nothing resists the light appearing, only
reality → **Weird**.

- **Base:** Weird **1**. I didn't let the Chapel lend its Tier 3 here: it's
  where he's standing, not what he's casting *through*. The rules don't
  rule it out, though (see issues).
- **Tags:** `Image` + `Halo` = **2**
- **Pool: 3 dice.** Against target 1: 87.5% chance of at least a
  partial success, but only 50% of a clean one (2+ successes).

**3b. Turn the crowd.** The townsfolk's fear and the Inquisitor's suspicion
resist → **Face**.

- **Base:** Face **5**. The Chapel's Tier 3 < 5, so acting "through" it
  doesn't matter.
- **Candidate tags (8):** `Performance`, `Persuasion`, `Deception` (the Door
  leads nowhere he knows of), `Lore` (the right scripture), `Welcoming`
  (inside his Chapel), `Staffed` (his faithful are planted in the crowd,
  weeping on cue), `Image`/`Halo` (the miracle itself helps sell it),
  `Mind` (a push of awe)
- **Applied: 5, at the cap.** `Performance`, `Persuasion`, `Staffed`,
  `Halo`, `Mind`.
- **Pool: 10 dice.**

If the GM had instead ruled it as **one** Face roll (like the book's
"fireball over the guards' heads = Face" case), the shaky 3-die Weird roll
disappears completely and he rolls 10 dice once. The two readings produce
very different odds of success.

### 4. High Fantasy: the miracle goes wrong

On a failure, the Inquisitor names him a fraud, and someone throws a
cobblestone that catches him on the temple. The GM takes **1 Body** (pool
1 → **0**) and **1 Face** (5 → 4) for the humiliation. The mob surges
forward. Ambrose uses **Turn Invisible** (no roll; he freezes in the shadow
of the altar curtain) while the mob pours past. Then he has to *move* to
reach the back flap, which breaks the misdirection.

- **What resists:** the mob's eyes. That isn't matter, a puzzle or a will,
  so "what resists?" doesn't clearly answer it. Stealth's default Stat is
  Body, so I used **Body**.
- **Base:** Body **1**
- **Tags:** `Stealth` + `Athletics` = **2**
- **Empty-pool penalty:** −1 die (Body pool is at 0)
- **Pool: 2 dice** (1 + 2 − 1)

A generous alternative: his faithful bundle him out (`Staffed`) and he's
"working from inside" his own Base, so Max(Tier 3, Body 1) = **3** + `Stealth`
+ `Staffed` − 1 = **4 dice**. That doubles the pool, and it depends entirely
on whether escaping through a building counts as acting *through* a
Location. The rules don't say.

## Things Noticed While Building

### Ambiguities / gaps

- **The letter-to-rank mapping is never stated.** The Priority Matrix uses
  A–D and every chapter uses ranks 1–4. You have to infer A=4 … D=1 by
  matching wording. It needs one sentence.
- **"Your First Skill Adds +1" reads as if later Skills don't.** The heading
  and wording ("the *first* Skill that applies … always adds 1 die")
  suggest a second applicable Skill might add nothing. The Tags section
  says every applicable tag adds a die. I stacked `Persuasion` +
  `Performance` + … as normal tags, but a careful reader could rule
  otherwise, which would gut Skills-A characters.
- **Approach vs. resistance.** Skills says *"the Stat sets the approach"*
  (the player's choice of how). How to Play and Magic say pick the Stat by
  *what resists*, "not just how the action looks". Scenario 2 shows the
  gap: the same goal (dirt on Kwon) is Mind or Face depending on method,
  and a specialist will always pick their 5.
- **"What resists?" has no answer for stealth or perception.** Being seen
  isn't matter, a puzzle or a will. The magic table even says "sneaking
  past them is a separate roll" without naming the Stat.
- **When does a Weird effect need its own roll?** "Turn invisible, then
  sneak" is two rolls. "Fireball over the guards' heads" is one Face roll.
  An illusion meant to sway a crowd sits right between them (Scenario 3),
  and the answer decides whether a 3-die roll gets in the way.
- **Do Action Feats roll?** Fly says no roll is needed unless something's
  in doubt. Read Minds and Turn Invisible don't say. Worse, the spell table
  says reading a guarded mind is a **Face roll**, while the Read Minds Feat
  apparently just works. So a Magic-4 telepath rolls, and a Skills-4 cold
  reader doesn't?
- **Can an item tier replace Weird?** "Base Stat = Max(Tier, Stat)" covers
  "whichever base Stat the action would normally use", and Weird is
  described as a base Stat. A Tier-3 Base could lift Weird 1 → 3 for any
  spell cast "from inside" it. I ruled no, but nothing in the text forbids
  it.
- **"Acting through" a Location is underdefined.** "Working from inside a
  fortress" could cover almost anything done in the building, including
  running out of it (Scenario 4: 2 dice vs 4 dice).
- **Armour tier has no clear use.** Only players roll, and there are no
  defence rolls, so it's unclear when you act "through" Light Armour, and
  the book never says armour reduces consequences. The Vestments were only
  worth buying for the `Imposing` tag. Tier 2 vs 4 armour looks
  mechanically identical except for price.
- **Pools of zero or fewer dice.** The core mechanic says pools run 1–10.
  A Body-1 character with an empty Body pool and no applicable tag has
  1 − 1 = **0 dice**. Does he fail automatically, or roll 1 die anyway?
- **Degrees-of-Success table typo:** the Critical row says it "overrides
  every tier **above**". The prose (and logic) say it overrides the tiers
  *below*/all others.
- **Target numbers have no guidance.** *Setting the Target Number* is still
  `\lipsum`, so none of the pools above can be judged against real
  difficulty.
- **`Staffed` followers have no rules.** A Base full of devotees is
  effectively a retinue. The tag adds a die, but can the faithful act
  independently, run errands, or fight? The book doesn't say.
- **Body D says "base stats across the board"**, but a Body-D/Skills-A
  character legally has Face 5. The matrix blurb is misleading.

### Balance concerns

- **A Face specialist reaches 10 dice with tags to spare.** In Scenarios 1
  and 3b he had 6–8 applicable tags for 5 slots. The 5-tag cap, not his
  sheet, is what limits him. Once a specialist has ~5 tags that fit their
  core activity, the rest of Skills A's 14 skills (and extra Stuff/Magic
  tags) only add breadth. That's probably intended, but it means Skills A
  and Skills B play the same on home turf.
- **Crits are very common at the cap.** With 10 dice against target 1,
  P(≥2 sixes) ≈ **52%**, so more than half of his routine social rolls are
  critical successes. Against target 2 it's still P(≥3 sixes) ≈ 22%.
- **Item tiers do nothing for a high-Stat character.** With Face 5,
  Max(Tier, Face) always returns 5, and his Mind-2 Ledger ties at 2. For
  him, Stuff tiers are just the price of Stuff tags. Stuff rewards
  low-Stat characters much more than high-Stat ones, which may be
  intentional but isn't signposted.
- **Talk-first play works too well.** "What resists?" plus free choice of
  approach means a Face-5 grifter can reframe most obstacles (Scenario 2)
  as 10-die social rolls. His one real weakness is things with no will,
  like drones, rockfalls and vacuum, where he rolls 1–3 dice. That's a good
  spotlight trade-off, but GMs need guidance on when reframing is
  legitimate.
- **Body 1 is extremely fragile.** One point of harm empties the pool
  permanently until the GM restores it, and every hit after that goes
  straight to the party's shared Coherence (whose refill is still a TODO).
  One glass-jawed character can drain the whole party's safety net.

## Reflection on the Build Process

### What seemed good

- **The build is fast and hard to get wrong.** Once I'd picked the ABCD
  letters, every other number (Stat Points, tag counts, Feats, Max Tier,
  Lifestyle) could be read straight off one table per chapter. Building
  Ambrose, including checking the arithmetic, took far less work than a
  comparable build in most point-buy systems. The flat tag pools that
  Stuff now shares with Body, Skills and Magic are the main reason. The
  2026-09-23 rework was the right call.
- **Every tag being worth one die makes rolls easy to audit.** Every pool
  in this file can be checked by anyone: Stat, list of tags, count, cap.
  There are no hidden modifiers, and a Stuff tag, a Skill and a magic Form
  all look the same at the table.
- **Weakness is built in, not optional.** ABCD forced a real hole: Body 1,
  no Body Tags, a 1-point Body pool. Nothing let me buy my way out of it,
  and Scenario 4 was more dramatic because of it.
- **"Ask what resists" works for magic.** In three of my four scenarios it
  gave a clear, defensible answer in seconds, and the spell table's edge
  cases (fireball-to-scare = Face) are well chosen.
- **The Face pool and its recovery rule suit a social character.** Rest
  doesn't restore Face; you have to repair your standing. For a fraudulent
  prophet, "you get Face back by being publicly vindicated" is excellent
  story fuel.

### What seemed bad

- **The 5-tag cap was the only thing limiting him on his home turf.** In
  every Face roll I had 6–8 applicable tags and dropped the extras. At
  that point extra tags only add breadth, and building a specialist stops
  involving real decisions: every social roll comes out at 10 dice.
- **For him, item tiers were just the price of tags.** With Face 5, his
  Stuff tiers could never matter on a social roll, and his Mind-2 Ledger
  only tied his Stat. I spent 7 Stuff Points and got nothing from the
  tier rule, which is half of what Stuff offers. A character with low
  Stats gets far more value from the same purchase.
- **Armour does almost nothing as written.** Only players roll and there
  are no defence rolls, so armour tier never enters a pool. I bought the
  Vestments purely for the `Imposing` tag. A Tier-4 Master Armour would
  have done exactly the same thing for 2 more points.
- **Most of the Feats have nothing to do with social play.** Only Read
  Minds really fits a talker. Turn Invisible needed a stretch ("Master
  Misdirection"), and the Stuff Feat (Never Sleep) is flavour I took
  because nothing social was on offer.
- **Placeholder text in the key places.** Target numbers, travel between
  realms, and three realm chapters are still `\lipsum`. I couldn't tell
  whether a 10-die pool is strong or routine, which is the most important
  balance question for this character.

### What was cool

- **Stuff as a congregation.** Treating a `Staffed` Base as the
  congregation that follows him from realm to realm, and re-skinning it
  (tent-cathedral, neon multiplex, hospital-ship chapel ring), was the
  most satisfying part of the build. The generic catalogue plus
  re-kitting on arrival really does pay off.
- **Feats can come from any column.** Read Minds as *Cold Reading* (a
  skill-sourced Feat) is perfect for a con artist, and the rules
  explicitly support it. Being able to get "psychic" powers out of pure
  training is a good design.
- **Scenario 3's two stages (make the miracle, then sell it) felt great.**
  The spectacle and the persuasion are separate beats. When it failed
  (Scenario 4), the consequences spilling across Body and Face tracks at
  once read like a real story.
- **Invented tags and skills (`Leverage`, `Etiquette`, `Halo`) slot in
  easily.** Letting the player write the words keeps the sheet personal.

### What was disappointing

- **Magic C was nearly worthless for my concept.** Weird 1 means the one
  roll that's actually magical (3a) is 3 dice. On Face rolls, the three
  magic tags just competed with Skills for the same 5 slots, and I usually
  cut one of them. For a Face specialist, Magic C buys spectacle, not
  power. Body C (4 Stat Points + 1 tag) would have been much stronger.
- **Feats don't interact with dice at all.** Read Minds told me exactly
  what the envoy feared, which is the whole point of a con artist, and it
  changed nothing mechanically. Without target-number rules, the obvious
  payoff (a lower difficulty) isn't guaranteed.
- **His weakness can be reframed away.** Almost every obstacle can be
  turned into a Face roll if the player picks the right approach
  (Scenario 2). Body 1 only mattered because I set up a scene with a mob
  and a thrown stone. A GM who doesn't deliberately set up that kind of
  scene will never test him.
- **Cyberpunk quietly shrinks Weird.** Magic there is hacking, and almost
  every digital effect has a system pushing back (Mind). "Nothing resists
  but reality" hardly ever applies in the sprawl.

### What was unclear

(Full list is in *Things Noticed While Building*. These are the ones that
changed a number I actually rolled.)

- **Does a second Skill add a die?** "Your First Skill Adds +1" makes it
  sound as if only the first counts. My 10-die pools depend on the answer.
- **Is the Stat set by approach or by what resists?** Skills and How to
  Play disagree, and that decides whether Scenario 2 is 5 dice or 10.
- **One roll or two for an effect used immediately?** The fireball case
  says one roll, the invisibility case says two, and the choice changed
  Scenario 3's odds.
- **When are you "acting through" a Location?** This changed Scenario 4
  from 2 dice to 4.
- **Can an item tier stand in for Weird?** If yes, his Chapel triples his
  magic.
- **What happens at 0 dice?** A Body-1 character with an empty pool and no
  applicable tag lands there easily.

### Recommendations to improve the system

1. **Resolve the two Stat rules and the Skill-stacking rule, in one
   section.** State outright: (a) "Choose the Stat by what resists *your
   chosen approach*; the player picks the approach, the GM may veto an
   approach that doesn't fit the fiction", and (b) "Every applicable Skill
   adds a die, up to the 5-tag cap." Rename "Your First Skill Adds +1" to
   something like "No Penalty for Lacking a Skill" so the heading stops
   suggesting that only one Skill counts. These three rules decide more of
   the numbers than anything else in the book.
2. **Write the target-number section, and let tags or Feats move the
   target, not only the pool.** With a 10-die ceiling, crits over 50%
   against target 1, and specialists reaching 10 on routine rolls, the
   game needs a target ladder (e.g. 1 routine / 2 hard / 3 heroic /
   4 legendary). It should also say that information from a Feat like
   Read Minds, or a strong position in the fiction, can lower the target.
   That gives Feats and surplus tags a job once the cap is reached.
3. **Give Stuff tiers something to do for high-Stat characters, and give
   armour any function at all.** Options, from simplest up:
   - Tier ≥ Stat gives +1 die (so tools always help a little).
   - Armour tier (or half of it) cancels that many points of Body loss
     per scene.
   - Locations let you spend their tier as a one-off "home ground" bonus.

   Also say plainly whether Max(Tier, Stat) can apply to Weird.
4. **Define "acting through" with a short list.** "You're acting through
   an item when the item is doing the work: the blade strikes, the car
   moves, the terminal computes, the fortress's walls/staff/sensors
   perform the action. Merely standing in a place or wearing a thing isn't
   acting through it."
5. **Add a minimum pool of 1 die, or make 0 dice an automatic failure,
   and say which.** Either works; the book just needs to pick one.
6. **Add two or three social Special Feats** so Face characters have Feats
   that fit them, keeping the "bypass exactly one hard constraint" rule.
   Examples: *Unforgettable* (anyone who has met you remembers you and
   your name, across language and realm), *Speak Any Tongue* (a
   Languages-style Feat for people rather than animals), *Words as Law*
   (once per scene, one plain sentence is believed as literally true
   unless disproven in front of the listener).
7. **Say whether a Weird effect and its use are one roll or two.** A
   one-line principle would do: "If the effect is aimed at someone's will
   and used at once, it's one roll on that Stat; if it has to exist first
   and is used later, roll Weird to create it, then roll again to use it."
8. **Make Magic C worth taking for non-casters.** Either raise Magic 2 to
   4 tags, or let Weird be at least 2 at Magic 2. As it stands, Magic C is
   clearly worse than Body C or Skills C for anyone who isn't building
   around spells. Verify with the "1 tag ≈ 0.5 Stat Point" rate the
   Skills Designer's Note already proposes: Magic 2 = 3 tags + Weird 1
   ≈ 2 points of value (counting Weird at a generous 0.5), vs Body 2 =
   4 Stat Points + 1 tag ≈ 4.5.
