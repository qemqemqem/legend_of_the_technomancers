# Dr. Ines Calloway, the Coroner

*Built by: subagent, seed: An utterly mundane expert with no magic at all — Skills at A, Magic at D. A detective, a surgeon, a mountaineer, a spy. Can competence alone keep up with wizards?, 2026-09-23.*

## Concept

Ines was a field surgeon in a war nobody won. Afterwards she became the
city coroner of a rain-soaked port, where the living lied to her and the
dead didn't. One night a body came in that couldn't exist. Its organs were
arranged for a different world's gravity, and its pockets held coins from
three kingdoms that had never been. She followed its trail to the wound in
the world and stepped through, because an unsolved death has always
bothered her more than danger does.

She has no magic, no blessing, and no strange blood. What she has is thirty
years of practice at noticing things. She's dry, patient, and unsettlingly
calm around corpses, and she's far too honest with the living. She
believes every impossible thing has a cause, and she mostly turns out to
be right.

## Four Scenarios

### 1. Cyberpunk: the body on the slab

A mid-level corp fixer has died "of heart failure" at 34. His family pays
Ines to find out what really happened. In the Cold Room (a tiled clinic
under a noodle bar, lit by one surgical lamp) she opens him up. She's
looking for whatever killed him and then hid itself.

- **What resists:** the hidden cause of death. That's **Mind**. It's the
  direct analogue of the rulebook's "Taste which of forty dishes was
  poisoned" (Mind: the poison is hidden).
- **Base:** she's acting through the Cold Room (Tier 3) and the Black Bag
  (Tier 2). Max(3, Mind 5) = **5**. The item tier is irrelevant.
- **Candidate tags:** `Medicine`, `Forensics`, `Perception`,
  `Keen Senses` (the faint almond-and-ozone smell), `Laboratory`,
  `Precise`, and `Hacking` (pulling the kill-switch log from his cardiac
  implant). That's **7 candidates**.
- **Tags used:** `Forensics` + `Medicine` + `Keen Senses` + `Laboratory` +
  `Precise` = **5 (at the cap)**
- **Pool: 10 dice.** The biggest pool the game allows, and she gets it
  with no magic at all.
  - *If only one Skill may count per roll* (see Issues): `Forensics` +
    `Keen Senses` + `Laboratory` + `Precise` = 4, so **9 dice**.
- **Feat interaction:** See Through Solid Matter would let her spot the
  sub-dermal micro-injector without cutting. Does that turn the whole roll
  into "certain, no roll needed"? The rules don't say.

### 2. High Fantasy: the chamberlain at the feast

The old duke died at his own table, and Ines has proved it was aconite.
Now the Chamberlain stands smiling at the high table. She walks up, meets
his eyes, and reads him (Read Minds). Then she decides whether to accuse
him in front of the court.

**Roll A: reading him.** The Feat is an Action, but it doesn't say whether
it needs a roll. The Magic chapter's table says "Read the thoughts of a
suspect guarding a secret" is **Face** (their will to keep it).
Ruling: the Feat makes the attempt *possible* for a mundane, and the
table sets the roll.

- **Base:** Face **3** (his will resists)
- **Tags:** `Perception` (reading a scene) + `Keen Senses` (the sweat at
  his collar, his pulse) = **2**
- **Pool: 5 dice**

**Roll B: the public accusation.** She lays out the wine-cup residue, the
cellar key, and the thought she just caught ("*the cellar... did she see
the cellar?*"), and demands he submit to the duke's right of inquest.

- **Base:** Face **3** (his nerve, and the court's loyalty to him)
- **Tags:** `Intimidation` + `Lore` (the old inquest law of the realm) +
  `Forensics` (the evidence, presented with authority) = **3** if Skills
  stack, or **1** if only the first Skill counts.
- **Pool: 6 dice** (or **4**)
- **Risk:** Face pool is 3. A botched accusation in front of the whole
  court is the kind of "lie that unravels in front of the wrong person"
  that the GM chapter says can cost 2–3 points at once. One bad roll could
  empty her Face pool.

### 3. Space Adventure: surgery in the dark

A derelict freighter is tumbling, with its hull breached. One survivor is
left, the pilot, with a shard of bulkhead lodged near her heart. Ines
crosses the vented section bare-faced behind the Miasma Mask (Needs No
Air, **no roll**). She stops, concentrates, and looks *through* the
pilot's chest to find the shard (See Through Solid Matter, an Action, **no
roll**, but "you can't do it passively while doing something else
demanding", so she looks first and cuts second). Then she operates in
zero-g by headlamp.

- **What resists?** This is where the rules break. There are three readings:
  - **(a) Mind 5.** `Medicine`'s listed default Stat is Mind, and "the
    complicated anatomy" resists. → Base **5**
  - **(b) Weird 0.** The Magic chapter says "Heal a willing ally's wound:
    Weird. Flesh doesn't resist being mended." The pilot is willing. If
    "what resists?" applies to *all* actions (How to Play says it does:
    "A spell and a mundane action use the same Stats"), a mundane surgeon
    rolls Weird. She has **0**.
  - **(c) Body 2.** Body covers "the handling of objects", and surgery is
    fine motor work. Max(Black Bag Tier 2, Body 2) = **2**.
- **Tags:** `Medicine` + `Precise` = **2**. `Keen Senses` could arguably
  apply (listening for the lung). I left it off.
- **Pool:** (a) **7 dice** / (b) **2 dice** / (c) **4 dice**. I played it
  as **(a), 7 dice**, but the rules as written point more at (b), and (b)
  is absurd: the best surgeon in the multiverse rolls fewer dice than a
  Magic-2 hedge-witch (Weird 1 + `Create` + `Body` = 3).

### 4. High Fantasy: across the rooftops

The Chamberlain's hired poisoner bolts out of a window and across the
slate roofs in the fog. Ines follows as far as she dares. When he's about
to jump a gap to the next street, she draws her flintlock (her re-skinned
Tier-2 revolver) and fires at his leg.

- **What resists:** his body and the distance. That's **Body**.
- **Base:** Max(Weapon Tier 2, Body 2) = **2** (a tie, so no help)
- **Tags:** `Firearms` + `Keen Senses` (finding his silhouette in the fog)
  = **2**. `Concealable` doesn't apply, because he already knows she's
  armed and chasing him.
- **Pool: 4 dice.** For comparison, a wizard with Body 2 throwing a fire
  bolt (`Destroy` + `Fire`) also rolls 4. They're at parity.
- **Risk:** Body pool 2. A bad landing on the slates plus one more failure
  puts her at −1 die on every Body roll.

## Verdict: can competence keep up with wizards?

**In dice, yes.** In her own field (Mind plus anything touching bodies,
evidence, or lies) she reaches the 10-die ceiling as easily as a Magic-A
wizard does. With 14 Skills Known, she almost always has a tag that fits.
Out of her field (Body) she's exactly as mediocre as a frail wizard.

**In kinds of effect, no.** She has none of the Weird actions (conjure,
teleport, transmute), and nothing on her sheet can make one possible. But
her three Feats (x-ray sight, mind reading, not breathing) cover a lot of
what a wizard's utility spells would, and they're flavoured as training
and gear. So the honest answer is "she keeps up because the Feats let her
quietly be a bit magical anyway."

## Things Noticed While Building

- **"Your First Skill Adds +1" is ambiguous, and it decides whether Skills A
  is any good.** As written it's either redundant (every applicable skill
  adds +1, like any tag) or it implies *only the first* skill counts. Under
  the first reading Ines hits the 10-die cap easily. Under the second, each
  roll gets exactly one Skill die however broad her training is, and 14
  Skills Known buys breadth but never depth. Meanwhile a wizard openly
  stacks Technique + Form + Specific on one roll. This needs a one-line
  ruling.
- **Invented narrow Skills have no "more specific" rule.** Magic
  explicitly lets a Specific tag stack with a broader Technique or Form.
  Skills just say "invent your own with your GM's approval". If Skills
  stack, nothing stops a player from inventing `Forensics`, `Toxicology`,
  and `Autopsy` to pile three dice onto one kind of roll. If they don't,
  inventing a narrow skill is strictly worse than taking a broad one.
- **Mundane healing vs. "what resists?"** The rulebook says mundane and
  magical actions use the same Stat logic, and it says healing a willing
  ally is Weird because flesh doesn't resist. Taken literally, every
  mundane surgeon and field medic rolls Weird 0. `Medicine`'s listed
  default of Mind contradicts this. Either healing magic should be Mind
  (or Body), or the Weird rule should say it applies only to *magical*
  effects.
- **Weird 0 contradicts the "Stat 1–5" framing.** The intro says the base
  Stat is 1–5 and pools are 1–10. A Magic-D character attempting anything
  Weird rolls 0 + tags, possibly **0 dice**. Is that an automatic failure,
  "impossible, doesn't happen", or a roll of 1 die? This isn't specified.
- **Item tier is worthless to high-Stat characters.** With Mind 5, Ines's
  Tier-3 Base and Tier-2 Bag never raise a base. Her 7 Stuff Points are
  mechanically just a route to 4 tags and a Feat. Stuff helps characters
  with low Stats and does nothing for strong ones. That may be intended
  (items "can only help"), but it makes Stuff B a poor pick for anyone
  with a Stat of 4 or more, and the priority table doesn't hint at this.
- **Skill-sourced Feats are as magical as magic ones.** Read Minds (Cold
  Reading) and See Through Solid Matter (Structural Reading) are
  mechanically identical to the spells. Nothing limits the skill version,
  so a mundane surgeon can "see through" a vault door by the same rule
  that lets her see through a ribcage. Either give skill-sourced Feats a
  narrower scope, or accept that "mundane" characters at Skills 3+ aren't
  mundane.
- **Do Feats replace rolls?** Fly explicitly says no roll is needed to
  stay aloft. Read Minds and See Through Solid Matter say nothing.
  Meanwhile the Magic table assigns a *Face roll* to reading a guarded
  mind. Is the Feat a permission (then you roll) or an auto-success? It's
  unclear, and it matters for scenario 1 (does x-ray sight make the
  autopsy certain?) and scenario 2.
- **Needs No Air vs. vacuum.** The Feat says the vacuum of space "can't
  suffocate you". It doesn't cover cold, decompression, or radiation. Can
  Ines walk the breached hull in a coat? I ruled yes for fun, but the text
  only covers breathing.
- **The Skills catalogue is too small for rank 4.** There are 18 printed
  skills and rank 4 grants 14, so "choosing what you know" at A is really
  choosing which 4 to *skip*. Either the catalogue needs more entries, or
  rank 4 players will be pushed toward inventing skills, which runs into
  the stacking question above.
- **Re-kitting can strand a skill.** The High Fantasy reskin turns a Tier-2
  Weapon into "a sturdy blade or hunting bow". If Ines took the bow, her
  `Firearms` (and `Concealable`) might not apply, and `Archery` is a
  separate skill. `Firearms` says "and ranged weapons", which overlaps
  `Archery`. The reskin tables say "swap in anything of the same rank", so
  a flintlock is legal, but a player who doesn't know that could lose
  dice by crossing a threshold.
- **Lifestyle "good tools" vs. Gadgets.** Well Off grants "good tools" for
  free. Does that already cover a surgical kit, making the Black Bag
  redundant? Can Lifestyle ever supply a tag or a tier? This isn't
  specified.
- **Designer's Note exchange rate checks out on paper.** At 1 tag = 0.5 SP,
  Skills 4 (4 SP + 14 tags = 11) equals Body 4 (9 SP + 4 tags = 11), and
  the same holds at every rank. But the rate assumes every tag is equally
  likely to apply. The 13th and 14th skills rarely fire, and if only one
  Skill counts per roll, the late skills are worth much less than 0.5.
- **Target numbers are still a placeholder** (GM chapter), so a 10-die pool
  can't be weighed against a real difficulty. For reference, the expected
  successes are 5 at 10 dice, 3.5 at 7, 2 at 4, and 1 at 2.
- **Feat item cost** (repeat of 01): I treated the Miasma Mask as free.
  The rules should say.

## Reflection on the Build Process

### What seemed good

- **The build is fast and easy to audit.** Priorities, then Stats, then a
  count of tags per column, then Feats. The whole character fits in about
  six small tables, and each number comes straight off one table. The
  shared "Stat Points" pool (Body plus Skills) was easy to grasp once I'd
  found it.
- **The double-cost 5th pip does its job.** Mind 5 cost me 5 of my 8
  points. That was a real sacrifice, and it left Body at 2. The rule is
  simple and produces a real trade-off.
- **The core roll is the same for everything.** Every scenario was the
  same formula: Stat + tags, capped at 5 tags. I never had to look up a
  sub-system. The cap actually *mattered* in scenario 1, where I had 7
  candidate tags and had to pick 5.
- **"What resists?" works well for magic and for most mundane actions.**
  The poison-is-hidden → Mind analogy from the spell table carried over
  cleanly to the autopsy. The Face roll for the accusation and the Body
  roll for the shot needed no discussion.
- **Consequence pools tie Stats to risk.** Seeing Body 2 / Face 3 as
  *pools* made her weak spots feel dangerous rather than just "fewer dice",
  which is good design.
- **Losing and re-kitting Stuff is player-friendly.** It's refreshingly
  free of bookkeeping.

### What seemed bad

- **Max(Tier, Stat) made 7 of my Stuff Points mechanically invisible.**
  Stuff B cost me a whole priority letter, and it never adds a die of base
  to this character. The only value it gave was 4 tags plus 1 Feat. I
  could have got most of that feeling from Lifestyle "good tools" for
  free. For anyone with a Stat of 4 or more, item tier is dead weight, and
  the priority table gives no warning.
- **Skills A is either great or weak depending on one unclear
  sentence.** The balance of the whole column rests on "Your First Skill
  Adds +1", which can be read two ways. That's a bad place for ambiguity.
- **Skills A buys breadth that barely matters.** Skills 13 and 14 hardly
  ever came up across four scenarios. 14 Skills Known from an 18-skill
  catalogue means the choice isn't interesting; I was really choosing
  what to *leave out*.
- **"Mundane" ended up meaning very little.** Skills 4 plus Stuff 3 gave
  her three Feats (x-ray sight, mind reading, no need to breathe). A
  Magic-A wizard gets 2 Feats from Magic. My "no magic at all" character
  had more impossible powers than a rank-4 wizard's own column grants.
  It's fun, but the seed's question ("can competence alone keep up?")
  becomes meaningless when competence comes with telepathy.
- **Mundane healing contradicts the Weird rule.** It was the one place
  the core logic gave a plainly absurd answer (a surgeon rolling Weird 0).

### What was cool

- **Skill-flavoured Feats are great character fuel.** "Structural
  Reading" and "Cold Reading" are the best part of the Feat chapter. They
  turned "detective" and "surgeon" into sheet abilities with a clear
  identity. The Miasma Mask (a plague-doctor respirator that becomes a
  void-mask in space) is the kind of cross-realm re-skinning the game
  promises and delivers.
- **The 10-die autopsy with no magic** was a real "competence porn"
  moment. It shows the system *can* make a mundane expert shine.
- **Re-skinning her Base** (clinic under a noodle bar / crypt under a
  chapel / station med-bay) gave each realm a place to start a story at
  no cost.
- **Parity in the rooftop shot** (4 dice for her pistol, 4 for a
  Body-2 wizard's fire bolt) felt fair and showed that the Stats, not the
  source of power, set the ceiling.

### What was disappointing

- **Mind 5 made Stuff irrelevant, when I expected the lab to matter.**
  Owning a morgue should make her *better at autopsies* than she is in a
  field. In practice the lab gives exactly the same +1 tag as a
  pocket-sized Gadget would. A Tier-3 Base and a Tier-2 Bag feel
  identical at the table.
- **Target numbers don't exist yet,** so I can't tell whether 10 dice
  is "trivial" or "necessary", or whether 4 dice is "risky" or "hopeless".
  Most balance questions are stuck until this is written.
- **The realm chapters are still lorem ipsum.** There's no guidance on
  what Skills look like in Cyberpunk or Space, and no local re-skins for
  skills at all.
- **Nothing a Magic-D character can do touches Weird**, and the book
  never says whether 0 dice means automatic failure. The mundane
  character's defining limit is left undefined.

### What was unclear

1. Does a second applicable Skill add a die? ("Your First Skill Adds +1")
2. Can invented Skills be narrower than printed ones and stack with them,
   the way Magic Specific tags can?
3. Which Stat does mundane healing use: Mind (the skill default) or Weird
   (the spell table's "flesh doesn't resist")?
4. What happens with a base of 0 (Weird 0)?
5. Do Action Feats (Read Minds, See Through Solid Matter) auto-succeed,
   or just make a roll *possible*? The Magic table implies a Face roll for
   reading a guarded mind.
6. Do skill-sourced Feats have a narrower scope than magic-sourced ones?
7. Does a Stuff Feat's item cost Stuff Points?
8. Can Lifestyle ("good tools") ever supply a tag or a tier, and does it
   overlap Gadgets?
9. Does Needs No Air protect against all of vacuum, or only suffocation?
10. When several owned items are involved in one action (Base + Bag),
    which one supplies the tier? It didn't matter here because Mind 5
    beats both, but it will for low-Stat characters.
11. `Firearms` "and ranged weapons" overlaps `Archery`. Which one applies
    to a re-kitted bow?

### Recommendations to improve the system

1. **Rule on Skill stacking in one sentence, and pair it with a
   narrowness rule.** Either: "Any number of applicable Skills add a die
   each, like any tag (subject to the 5-tag cap); invented Skills must be
   narrower than a printed one, exactly like Magic Specific tags." Or:
   "Only one Skill counts per roll." I'd recommend the first, since it
   matches Magic and makes Skills A feel as deep as Magic A. Then
   rename or delete "Your First Skill Adds +1", which currently causes
   more confusion than it prevents.
2. **Give item tier value even when your Stat beats it.** Options
   (pick one): (a) an item whose tier is *at least* your Stat also adds +1
   die (a "quality" bonus, counting toward the cap), (b) an item's tier
   limits what's *possible* (only a Tier-3 lab can identify a designer
   toxin at all), or (c) state plainly in the priority table that Stuff
   mainly helps weak Stats, so players can choose with that in mind.
   Right now Stuff B is a trap for high-Stat builds.
3. **Fix the mundane-healing / Weird clash.** Suggested wording: "Weird
   is only for effects that *could not happen* without Magic. If a mundane
   action could achieve the same result, use the Stat that action would
   use." Then move "Heal a willing ally" to Mind (or Body for crude
   field-mending) in the spell table.
4. **Decide how Action Feats resolve.** Suggested: "A Feat makes the
   impossible *possible*; it doesn't make it *certain*. Roll when
   something resists (a guarded mind, a lead-lined wall); don't roll when
   nothing does." Then align the Magic table's thought-reading row with it.
5. **Define the 0-base case.** "With a base of 0, you cannot attempt the
   action unless a tag or item supplies a base." This makes Magic D a
   real, readable limit.
6. **Enlarge the Skills catalogue to about 24–30 entries,** or reduce
   Skills Known at rank 4. Choosing 14 of 18 isn't a choice.
7. **Consider a Feat cap, or limit skill-sourced Feats to a "plausible"
   scope** (e.g., Structural Reading sees through flesh and ordinary
   walls, not vaults), so that "mundane" still means something. Also
   answer the open question in the Feat chapter about stacking up to 8.
8. **Write the target-number guidance next.** Almost every balance
   question here (Is 10 dice too much? Is 4 dice viable?) can't be
   answered without it.
9. **Say whether Feat items cost points and whether Lifestyle can
   substitute for cheap Gadgets.** This has come up in two playtest
   characters now.
