# Hum, the Hive That Walks

*Built by: subagent, seed: Something that is not remotely human. Put Body at A and lean into being monstrous or strange, 2026-09-23.*

*Rules source: `rulebook.tex` + `chapters/`.*

## Concept

Hum is about forty thousand bone-white wasps. When it wants to be understood,
it holds itself in a rough, tall, human-ish shape. There is no queen, no
centre and no single body. The wound in the world opened through the paper
nest it lived in, and the whole colony fell through at once. Somewhere in the
fall it started thinking in one voice. It talks by buzzing its wings in
chorus, which sounds like a choir heard through a wall, and it uses "we"
because "I" makes no sense to it. It's curious about people and it's
horrified by how *alone* each of them is. It owns nothing, because it has no
hands to hold anything.

In each realm it takes the local shape of a swarm: bone-wasps in High
Fantasy, a cloud of chrome micro-drones in the sprawl, and a drifting cloud
of silicate mites in deep space.

## Stats

Stat Points: Body rank 4 gives **9**, Skills rank 2 gives **1**, so **10** in total.
Every Stat starts at 1.

| Stat | Raise | Cost | Running total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body 1 → 5 | 1→2 (1), 2→3 (1), 3→4 (1), 4→5 (**2**, double) | 5 | 5 |
| Mind 1 → 4 | 1→2 (1), 2→3 (1), 3→4 (1) | 3 | 8 |
| Face 1 → 3 | 1→2 (1), 2→3 (1) | 2 | 10 |

- **Body 5** · **Mind 4** · **Face 3** (10/10 points spent)
- **Weird 2** (Magic 3 − 1)
- **Pools:** Body 5 / Mind 4 / Face 3

(Other legal option: Body 5 + Mind 5 = 10 points, with Face left at 1. See
the issues below.)

## Body Tags (4, from Body rank 4)

- `Natural Weapon`: forty thousand stings.
- `Keen Senses`: a thousand compound eyes looking every way at once.
- `Regenerating`: the brood keeps hatching, and lost wasps are replaced within hours.
- `Intimidating`: a person-shaped cloud of wasps that *speaks*.

## Body Feats (2, from Body rank 4)

- **Fly** (Continuous). *Body source: Wings.* It's a swarm. It doesn't walk anywhere.
- **Walk Through Walls** (Action). *Body source: Malleable Form.* The swarm
  pours through keyholes, vents and mortar cracks, then comes back together
  on the other side. It can't stay inside the wall.

## Skills (7, from Skills rank 2)

| Skill | Default Stat | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `Brawling` | Body | Engulfing, stinging, smothering |
| `Stealth` | Body | Breaking apart into a hundred harmless-looking bugs |
| `Perception` | Mind | Hive attention is spread everywhere |
| `Survival` | Mind | Foraging and finding its way by scent and sun |
| `Medicine` | Mind | It knows exactly where soft tissue is. It also makes medicinal honey |
| `Languages` | Mind | It learned speech by listening at windows |
| `Intimidation` | Face | It doesn't need to try very hard |

## Magic (7 tags, 1 Feat, Weird 2): pheromone-witchery

The hive's "magic" is chemical command. It releases scent-signals that other
creatures, and other minds, can't help obeying.

- **Techniques:** `Control`, `Perceive`, `Transform`
- **Forms:** `Animal`, `Mind`
- **Specific:** `Insects` (narrower than Animal), `Pheromones` (narrower than
  Mind: minds reached only through scent)

Count: 3 + 2 + 2 = 7.

- **Magic Feat: Read Minds** (Action). *Magic source: Telepathy*, reskinned as
  "tasting fear on the air". It reads surface feelings and thoughts from the
  sweat and breath of anyone nearby.

## Stuff (Rank 1: 0 points, 0 tags, 0 Feats)

- Nothing at all. Max Tier: none.
- **Lifestyle: Scraping By.** "Basic clothes, pocket change, nothing to
  spare." In Hum's case that means a few coins stuck in the wax of a
  comb-pocket somewhere inside the swarm, and no clothes whatsoever.

## Feat Total

Body 4 gives 2, Magic 3 gives 1, Skills 2 gives 0 and Stuff 1 gives 0, so
**3 Feats**: Fly, Walk Through Walls, Read Minds.

## Four Scenarios

### 1. High Fantasy: the knight in the gatehouse (Body)

A paladin in full plate is holding the gatehouse stair. Hum doesn't fight the
armour. It flows over him and into the visor slits and the gaps at the elbows
and armpits, looking for skin.

- **Base:** Body **5**. The knight's body and his plate resist. No item is
  involved, because Hum has none.
- **Tags:** `Natural Weapon` (stings) + `Brawling` (engulf and grapple) +
  `Medicine` (knows where the soft places are, if the GM accepts a second
  skill) + `Keen Senses` (finding the gaps in a moving suit of plate) = **4**
- **Pool: 9 dice.** `Intimidating` doesn't apply, because this roll attacks
  his Body, not his nerve. The 5-tag cap never comes into play.
- *Rules friction:* "Your First Skill Adds +1" makes it look as if only the
  *first* skill counts. The general Tags rule says every tag that applies
  adds a die. I've read it as both skills counting.

### 2. Cyberpunk: hijacking the drone net (Mind)

In the sprawl, Hum is a cloud of chrome micro-drones. A corp tower's security
drones are hunting it. Hum pushes its own signal-scent into their mesh and
tries to take them over as part of the swarm.

- **Base:** Mind **4**. The table says a secured system resists, the same as
  "crack a corporate mainframe". Hum has no terminal to lend a better tier.
- **Tags:** `Control` + `Insects` (if "insects" reskins to "drones" in this
  realm) + `Pheromones` (if "scent" reskins to "signal") = **up to 3**. It
  has no `Hacking` skill.
- **Pool: 7 dice** if the GM accepts both reskins. It's **5 dice** if only
  `Control` counts. The swing is 2 dice, and it depends entirely on how
  generous the GM is with reskinning *Specific* tags. The rules only give an
  example for a Technique (`Control`).

### 3. Space Adventure: the customs officer (Face)

A station customs officer has sealed the airlock and is calling the thing
behind the glass "a biohazard". Hum leans on the glass and whispers through
the vents. It floods the room with a calming, obedient scent while making
sure the officer knows exactly what's waiting on the other side.

- **Base:** Face **3**. The officer's will resists. Per the table, changing
  what a creature wants against its will is Face.
- **Tags:** `Pheromones` + `Control` + `Mind` (magic Form) + `Intimidation`
  + `Intimidating` = **5 (at the cap)**
- **Pool: 8 dice.** **Read Minds** first shows it what he's afraid of
  (losing his job) without a roll. The Feat adds no dice, but it tells Hum
  which lever to pull. `Keen Senses` would be a sixth tag and the cap
  blocks it.
- *Note:* the least human thing in the party rolls 8 dice to talk a
  bureaucrat around. That's one more than a Face-5 character with a social
  skill and a Body tag (5 + 2 = 7).

### 4. High Fantasy: passing as a pilgrim (Weird, then Face)

The Cathedral-City lets no monsters through its gate. Hum pulls itself in
tight, changes the colour of its own chitin, and holds the exact shape of a
hooded pilgrim.

- **Roll A: the transformation.** It's reshaping its own willing body, so
  nothing resists but reality: **Weird 2**.
  - Tags: `Transform` + `Animal` + `Insects` = **3** (the Specific tag stacks
    with its Form)
  - **Pool: 5 dice**
- **Roll B: getting past the gate warden.** "Weird makes an effect happen;
  using the effect is a separate roll." The warden's suspicion resists, so
  the base is **Face 3**.
  - Tags: `Pheromones` (a soothing, trustworthy scent) = **1**. It has no
    `Deception`, and `Intimidating` actively works *against* it here.
  - **Pool: 4 dice.**
- The transformation is the *harder* half: Weird 2 is Hum's worst Stat by
  far. That's odd for a creature whose entire nature is being a
  shape-shifting cloud. `Camouflage` would have fit perfectly, but Hum spent
  its tags elsewhere.

## Things Noticed While Building

- **Body A + any Stat Points from Skills lets you max two Stats.** The Body
  chapter says a Body-4 character "can max one Stat and still have real
  points left over, but cannot max two." With Skills C that's 9 + 1 = 10
  points, which buys Body 5 **and** Mind 5 exactly (5 + 5). With Skills B
  it's 11 points: 5 + 5 + 1 = Body 5 / Mind 5 / Face 2. The sentence is only
  true of Body's points on their own and should say so, or add a hard cap.
- **Body A looks like the strongest column.** Stats count twice: they're
  both your dice *and* your consequence pools. Hum has 12 pool points
  (5/4/3), where Tamsin (Body C) has 7. The Designer's Note compares Body
  and Skills at rank 3, but the jump from 6 to 9 Stat Points at rank 4 is
  the largest step anywhere in the book, and that exchange-rate check
  doesn't account for the extra pool points.
- **Monsters have no drawbacks.** "Truly strange or monstrous" comes with
  no mechanical cost. Nothing in the rules covers having no hands (can Hum
  pick a lock or climb a rope?), creatures that can't speak, being
  vulnerable to fire or smoke, or being barred from towns. Everything
  depends on GM fiction. A "Monstrous Complication" or a GM-invoked
  weakness would make Body A cost something.
- **Which Magic Form covers a non-human's own body?** The `Body` Form is
  "humans and humanlike bodies". Reshaping an insect swarm falls under
  `Animal` (plus `Insects`). A dragon or a robot PC who wants self-magic
  needs a different Form than a human does, and the rules never say this.
- **Can Specific magic tags be reskinned?** "`Control` compels a beast in a
  fantasy world and overrides a security system in the sprawl." That shows a
  *Technique* bending between realms. Whether `Insects` becomes "drones" or
  `Pheromones` becomes "signals" in cyberpunk is unaddressed, and in
  scenario 2 the difference is 2 dice.
- **"Your First Skill Adds +1" conflicts with the tag rules.** Taken
  literally, it suggests only the first applicable skill adds a die. The
  Tags section says every applicable tag adds one. As currently written,
  the section doesn't seem to do anything at all, because there's no
  penalty it replaces and every skill is already +1. It either needs a real
  mechanical effect or should be removed.
- **Read Minds (Feat) vs. mind-reading magic.** The spell table says reading
  a guarded suspect's thoughts is a Face roll. The Feat reads "surface
  thoughts" and never mentions a roll. When does the Feat need a roll, and
  when does a resisting mind turn it into a spell roll? The Feat also stacks
  awkwardly with `Perceive` + `Mind`: is the Feat just a free version of a
  roll?
- **Feats are mechanically fixed, but their re-skin doesn't always fit.**
  Walk Through Walls re-skinned as "the swarm pours through cracks" should,
  by the rules, still get through a seamless vault door ("functions
  identically regardless of source"). The fiction strains. Should the Body
  flavour limit it? The rules say no.
- **Lifestyle doesn't fit non-humans.** Stuff D gives "basic clothes, pocket
  change". A wasp swarm has no pockets. That's only flavour, but the table
  assumes a humanoid.
- **Weird is capped low even for inherently weird creatures.** Hum's most
  "natural" act (reshaping itself) runs on its weakest Stat. That's fine by
  the letter of the rules, but a Body-A monster doing monstrous-body things
  rolls fewer dice than a lockpick. A Body tag like `Camouflage` covers
  some of this. Whether an innate transformation should be a Body roll or a
  Weird roll isn't addressed.
- **Face pools for monsters.** The Face pool means "social standing". What
  is the standing of a thing no society accepts, and how does it restore
  Face through "recognition" when every town bars the gate? The system
  works, but the fiction needs guidance.
- **The Face-cap scenario rolls big for a monster.** With `Pheromones`,
  `Control`, `Mind`, `Intimidation` and `Intimidating`, a Face-3 swarm hits
  the 5-tag cap on social compulsion (8 dice). Tags from *different
  columns* stack easily into the cap, which makes the 5-tag cap the real
  limiter rather than Stats. That's worth watching as a balance lever.

## Reflection on the Build Process

### What seemed good

- **The ABCD matrix makes the concept decision for you, quickly.** "Body A
  = truly strange or monstrous" turned a vague seed into a sheet in minutes.
  Each column's opening table (points / tags / Feats per rank) was the only
  reference I needed during the arithmetic, and every number was easy to
  audit.
- **One formula for everything.** Stat + tags, max 5 tags, 4+ succeeds. I
  never had to look up a subsystem. A sting, a drone hijack, a social
  compulsion and a self-transformation all used the same line, and that's
  the system's biggest strength.
- **"What resists?" is an excellent question.** It settled every
  magic-Stat choice in seconds (knight's plate is Body, secured drone net is
  Mind, customs officer is Face, own willing body is Weird). The *Spells and
  Their Stats* table is the best page in the book for a new player.
- **The double-cost 5th pip is a small, elegant brake.** It made "Body 5"
  feel like a real commitment (half my points) without needing a separate
  cap rule.
- **Tags from different sources look the same at the table.** Body, Skill
  and Magic tags stacked with no special cases, which is how I reached 8
  dice on scenario 3 with a Face of only 3.

### What seemed bad

- **Stats are dice *and* health, and Body A buys the most Stats.** Nothing
  else in the build is double-counted. Body 4's 9 Stat Points (up from 6 at
  rank 3) is the biggest jump in any column, and it also makes the character
  the hardest to wear down. Hum ended up with no Stat below 3 and 12 total
  pool points. That's not "strange with trade-offs", it's just better.
- **Stuff D cost Hum nothing that mattered.** Taking Stuff at D is meant to
  hurt, but a creature with no hands wasn't going to carry gear anyway, and
  Max(Tier, Stat) is worthless when every Stat is already 3–5. High Stats
  make Stuff redundant. The two columns overlap instead of complementing
  each other.
- **Monstrousness has only upsides.** The rules give flavour ("a dragon, an
  angel") but no mechanical friction. I had to invent all the drawbacks
  (no hands, no speech, fire) in prose, and none of them are enforceable.
- **Placeholder chapters left gaps I noticed during scenarios.** Target
  numbers (GM chapter), Travel Between Realms, and the realm chapters'
  Body/Magic sections are all lorem ipsum. So I could build pools but not
  judge whether 5 vs. 9 dice is "good", and I had no official guidance on
  what a swarm looks like in cyberpunk.

### What was cool

- **Reskinning across realms was the most fun part.** Bone-wasps became
  chrome micro-drones, which became silicate mites, and each one suggested
  scenes on its own. The idea that a Body Tag like `Natural Weapon` stays
  just as strong while completely changing shape is a great hook.
- **Feats as "a door dice can't open."** Fly and Walk Through Walls changed
  *what problems Hum even faces* (stairs, doors, locked rooms stop
  mattering). That's a more interesting power than +dice, and it's what made
  the character feel monstrous rather than just statistically large.
- **Specific magic tags invite invention.** `Pheromones` and `Insects` came
  straight out of the concept and stacked properly with their Forms. That's
  the Lady Blackbird DNA working well.
- **Split rolls ("Weird makes it happen, using it is separate").** Scenario
  4's split roll (transform on Weird, then fool the warden on Face) produced
  a genuinely interesting result: the monster found the *magic* easy-ish
  and the *acting* hard. That's a character moment the rules created.

### What was disappointing

- **The loudest trait of the concept didn't use Body at all.** Hum's
  defining act (being a shape-shifting cloud) fell to Weird 2, its worst
  Stat. Body A bought raw power, but "strange" had no dice behind it unless
  I spent a scarce Body Tag on `Camouflage`. "Truly strange" delivered
  mostly "truly strong".
- **Only 10 Body Tags, all humanoid-coded.** Nothing on the list covers
  swarms, amorphous bodies, extra limbs, size, armoured hide, or
  non-standard senses. Every Body-A concept ends up picking from the same
  ten that an elf picks from, and "invent with GM approval" carries all the
  weight.
- **Read Minds felt like a weaker duplicate of my own magic.** I already
  had `Perceive` + `Mind`, and the spell table already covers mind-reading
  as a Face roll. The Feat was mainly a free, rules-light version of
  something I could already roll for.
- **Lifestyle and the Face pool assume a human.** Small things, but they
  broke immersion right when the seed asked me to lean into strangeness.

### What was unclear

- **Whether "Your First Skill Adds +1" limits skills to one per roll.** I
  counted two skills in scenario 1 based on the general Tags rule, but the
  section title strongly suggests otherwise.
- **Whether Specific magic tags reskin between realms** (`Insects` into
  drones?). That's a 2-die swing, decided purely on GM mood.
- **Which Form covers a non-human character's own body.** Is it `Body`
  ("humanlike") or `Animal`? What about a robot or an elemental?
- **When a Feat needs a roll.** Does Read Minds against a resisting
  mind turn into a Face roll? Can Walk Through Walls (reskinned as "through
  cracks") really get through a seamless vault door?
- **Whether an innate transformation is Body or Weird.** Reshaping your own
  willing body is Weird by the table, but for a Body-A shapeshifter that
  feels backwards.
- **How to judge pool sizes without target numbers.** I don't know if 9
  dice trivialises a paladin, or if 4 dice at the gate is hopeless.

### Recommendations to improve the system

1. **Balance the fact that Stats count as both dice and health.** Options:
   (a) cut Body rank 4 to 8 Stat Points, (b) make consequence pools a fixed
   size (e.g. 3 each) plus a smaller bonus, or (c) give Body A a mandatory
   *Monstrous Complication*: a GM-invokable weakness (no hands, can't pass
   as human, vulnerable to X) that costs dice or pool points when it comes
   up. (c) also solves the "monsters have no drawbacks" problem and makes
   Body A play *strange* rather than just *strong*.
2. **Add an explicit "Stat ceiling" rule and fix the Body-chapter
   sentence.** Either state "no character may start with more than one Stat
   at 5", or reword the text so it says only Body's points can't max two.
   At the moment, Body A + Skills C/B builds break the book's own claim.
3. **Rewrite "Your First Skill Adds +1" or delete it.** As written it has
   no mechanical effect, and it creates confusion about multiple skills.
   If the intent is "multiple skills stack like any tags", say so in one
   sentence. If the intent is "only one skill per roll", say *that*.
4. **Write a short "Non-Human Travellers" sidebar in Body.** It should say:
   which magic Form covers your own body (always your own nature's Form, or
   a new `Self` Form), whether innate shapeshifting can use Body instead of
   Weird when it's physical, how Lifestyle and the Face pool read for
   monsters, and 6–10 more exotic Body Tags (`Swarm`, `Amorphous`,
   `Armoured Hide`, `Huge`, `Many-Limbed`, `Alien Senses`).
5. **Clarify when Feats need a roll.** Add one line per Feat type:
   *Continuous* Feats never need a roll to function. *Action* Feats need no
   roll unless something resists, and then roll per "what resists?" with
   the Feat allowing the attempt. Also state that the Feat's effect sets
   what's possible, and the fiction of its source can't shrink it (or say
   that it can).
6. **State the reskin rule for all tags.** "Every tag — Technique, Form,
   Specific, Body, Stuff — reskins with its realm. Its *narrowness* travels,
   its *object* translates." That removes the GM-mood 2-die swing.
7. **Make Stuff matter to high-Stat characters.** Stuff Tags are the only
   part of an item that helps a Body-5 character. Consider letting an
   item's tier add +1 die when it's at or above your Stat, or have a Stuff
   rank grant a Feat at rank 2. Either way, Stuff D shouldn't be a free
   dump for monsters.
8. **Fill in target numbers before further balance testing.** Even a
   rough table (1 = routine, 2 = hard, 3 = heroic, 4+ = legendary) would
   show whether 8–9 dice pools break the game or are the intended top end.
