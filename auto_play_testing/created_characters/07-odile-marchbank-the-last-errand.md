# Odile Marchbank, the Last Errand

*Built by: subagent, seed: A very young or very old traveller — a runaway kid, or a retired adventurer on one last trip. Body at D. Someone who survives by being underestimated., 2026-09-23.*

*Rules source: `rulebook.tex` + `chapters/`.*

## Concept

Odile Marchbank is eighty-one. Sixty years ago she and her husband Harold
were the kind of dungeon-delvers who got paid to go in first and never
wrote anything down. They retired to a seaside boarding house and spent
forty quiet years telling lies to the other residents about how dull their
youth had been. Harold's last wish was to have his ashes scattered "on the
far side of the world, where we always said we'd go." When the wound in the
world opened in the boarding-house garden, Odile packed her carpetbag,
tucked Harold's urn under one arm, took her cane, and stepped through. She
decided the far side of the world was the far side of *all* of them.

She is slow and stooped, and her hands shake until the moment they need
not to. She's hard of hearing, except when she isn't. Bandits wave her
past, guards help her down steps, and ganger kids try to snatch her
handbag. All of them make the same mistake. Her rule, learned in a hundred
tombs: *never be the most dangerous-looking thing in the room.*

## Stats

**Stat Points:** Body rank 1 → **2** + Skills rank 4 → **4** = **6 points**
(Stuff and Magic grant none).

All Stats start at 1. Costs: 1→2 = 1, 2→3 = 1, 3→4 = 1, 4→5 = 2.

| Stat | Raise | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Body | stays 1 | 0 |
| Mind | 1 → 4 | 1 + 1 + 1 = **3** |
| Face | 1 → 4 | 1 + 1 + 1 = **3** |
| **Total** | | **6 / 6** |

- **Body 1 · Mind 4 · Face 4**
- **Weird 1** (Magic rank 2 − 1)
- **Pools:** Body 1 / Mind 4 / Face 4 (Weird has no pool)

*Alternative considered:* Face 5 costs 1+1+1+2 = 5, which leaves 1 point
for Mind 2. Having 4 in both matched "sharp and charming" better than
"charming and slow". Because the 5th pip costs double, the 4/4 split gives
two strong Stats where 5/2 gives one very strong one. That's a real choice,
and the double cost does its job here.

## Body Tags (0)

Body rank 1 gives **0 Body Tags**. The rules say "that absence is the
point". It's a problem for this concept, though: the thing that defines
Odile, being frail and looking harmless, has no place on the sheet under
Body. (See *Rules Issues*.)

### Skill-granted Special Feats (rank 4 → 2)

- **Turn Invisible**, sourced as *Master Misdirection* (a skill option the
  catalogue lists). Nobody looks at the old woman sitting still in the
  corner. The fiction is that she's still *there*, just unseen. It follows
  the Feat's own limit: "a fast dash or a shout gives you away."
- **Read Minds**, sourced as *Cold Reading* (a skill option the catalogue
  lists). Sixty years of watching the faces of people about to betray her.

## Magic (rank 2: 3 Tags, Weird 1, 0 Feats): hedge-witchery

Magic rank 2 → **3 Tags Known**, **Weird 1**, **0 Feats**.

- **Technique:** `Perceive`
- **Form:** `Mind`
- **Specific:** `Tea Leaves`: divination by the dregs in a cup. It's
  narrower than Perceive, so it's a valid Specific tag, and it stacks.

Count: 1 + 1 + 1 = **3 / 3**.

*Re-skinning:* the realm chapters say Magic "is hacking" in Cyberpunk and
"science and engineering" in Space. It's unclear whether Odile's tea leaves
*turn into* hacking (reading dregs of data in log files?) or stay tea
leaves that happen to work there. I've assumed they keep their look and the
realm just accepts them. (See *Rules Issues*.)

- **Speak with Animals**, sourced as *Universal Translator*: **Harold's

## Four Scenarios

The GM chapter's *Setting the Target Number* is still placeholder text, so
the odds below assume a **target of 2**: "≥2" means at least a partial
success, and ">2" means a clean success. At a 50% chance per die, the
expected number of successes is half the pool.

### 1. High Fantasy: the toll on the coach road (Face)

Bandits stop the mail coach and order everyone out to be searched. Odile's
cane is in her hand and her hairpins are in her hair. She wants the captain
to skip her and turn his back, so she stumbles, drops her spectacles, and
asks him in a trembling voice to fetch them because her eyes aren't what
they were.

First, **Read Minds** (Feat, Action, no roll stated): she reads the
captain's surface thoughts. He's jumpy, his men don't respect him, and he
badly wants this over quickly. That doesn't add a die, but it tells her
which act to put on: the doddering grandmother who wastes his time.

- **What resists?** His suspicion, a person's will → **Face 4**
- **Tags that clearly apply:**
  - `Deception`: it's a lie.
  - `Playing Harmless`: this is exactly what the skill is for.
  - `Performance`: the dropped spectacles are stagecraft.
  - `Concealable` (Cane): it's the reason the search doesn't find a sword.
  - (`Persuasion` is a stretch because she isn't making a case, so it's
    left out.)
- **Tags: 4** (under the cap of 5)
- **Pool: 4 + 4 = 8 dice.** ≥2: 96%, >2: 86%.

**Stress point:** three of those four tags are Skills that describe *the
same act* from slightly different angles. Nothing in the rules stops this.
A Skills-A character with a few invented social skills will sit at 3–4
tags on every lie she tells. (See *Rules Issues*.)

### 2. Cyberpunk: the alley (Body, through an item)

A ganger kid, chromed and twitchy, pins her against a wet wall and puts a
knife to her throat for her handbag. Odile lets her knees buckle and
whimpers, then brings the cane up. The blade clears the shaft and flicks
the knife out of his hand before he has finished laughing.

- **What resists?** His grip and his body → Body.
- **Base:** acting through the cane → Max(Tier 3, Body 1) = **3**
- **Tags:**
  - `Blades`: swordplay.
  - `Disarming`: the goal is to take the knife.
  - `Concealable`: he never saw a sword coming.
  - `Playing Harmless`: arguably applies, because the reason he's standing
    close and careless is that he dismissed her. The skill's default Stat
    is Face, but "other Stats may apply if the fiction supports it." GM
    call. Counted here.
- **Tags: 4**
- **Pool: 3 + 4 = 7 dice.** ≥2: 94%, >2: 77%.

**The catch:** her **Body pool is 1**. One failure, or a partial success
where the GM charges her a Body point, and she's at 0. From then on she
takes **−1 die on every Body roll**, including rolls through the cane
(the rules say so explicitly), so the same move is 6 dice. After that,
further Body losses go to the party's shared **Coherence**. Odile can't
be killed by the rules as written. She just drains her friends.

If she tried to *run* instead: Body 1 with no Athletics (she doesn't have
it) → **1 die**, and **0 dice** once her Body pool is empty. The rules
never say whether a 0-die pool is allowed. On the scooter it's
Max(Tier 2, Body 1) = 2 + `Piloting` + `Fast` = **4 dice**, which is much
better. That's the point of having the scooter.

### 3. Space Adventure: which berth? (Mind, magic)

Slavers have snatched a party member somewhere on a 400-berth orbital
dock. Odile sits in the dockworkers' canteen with a pot of synth-tea. She
pours for the tired stevedores coming off shift, listens to their
complaints, then collects the cups and reads the dregs for the one berth
none of them will talk about.

- **What resists?** Something hidden → **Mind 4**
- **Tags:**
  - `Perceive`: divination.
  - `Tea Leaves`: this is literally what the tag is.
  - `Mind` (magic form): what she's reading is the drinkers' memories, left
    in the cup.
  - `Perception`: she also watches which dockhand flinches.
  - (`Languages` might apply for the alien crews' cups. It would fit under
    the cap, but it's a stretch, so it's left out.)
- **Tags: 4**
- **Pool: 4 + 4 = 8 dice.** ≥2: 96%, >2: 86%.

**Stress point:** there's a shortcut that makes this roll pointless. She
could use **Read Minds** on the most nervous stevedore. The Feat says she
"perceive[s] their surface thoughts" and doesn't mention a roll. But the
*Spells and Their Stats* table says "Read the thoughts of a suspect
guarding a secret" is a **Face** roll. Is the Feat automatic, or does it
still need a Face roll? If it's automatic, a 1-slot Feat replaces an
8-die spell and makes her magic column redundant. (See *Rules Issues*.)

### 4. High Fantasy: the coachman bleeding out (Weird vs. Mind)

Scenario 1 went partially wrong and the coachman caught a crossbow bolt in
the gut. Odile kneels in the road. She could heal him *magically*, or with
sixty years of field surgery.

**Option A: hedge-magic healing.** The rules say healing a willing ally is
**Weird**, since "flesh doesn't resist being mended".

- **Base:** Weird **1**
- **Tags:** `Medicine` (anatomy clearly helps). She has no `Create` and no
  `Body` form. `Perceive` might help her sense the wound, but that's a
  stretch. Tags are "not a prerequisite," so she can attempt this with no
  magic tags at all.
- **Pool: 1 + 1 = 2 dice** (3 if `Perceive` is allowed). ≥2: 25%.

**Option B: pull the bolt and stitch.** It's mundane, so what resists?
The wound is complicated → **Mind 4**.

- **Tags:** `Medicine` + `Perception` (finding where the head went) +
  `Craft` (the needlework) = **3**
- **Pool: 4 + 3 = 7 dice.** ≥2: 94%.

She stitches him. **Stress point:** the magic route is 2 dice and the
mundane route is 7. For a Magic-C character, Weird 1 is almost never the
best option. Worse, the "what resists?" rule applied literally to mundane
healing ("flesh doesn't resist being mended") would *also* send the
surgery to Weird. A Magic-D surgeon would then have Weird **0** and couldn't
heal anyone. I read Mind here because the alternative is absurd, but the
rules don't say so. (See *Rules Issues*.)

## Rules Issues, Ambiguities & Balance Concerns

1. **Nothing stops overlapping Skills from stacking.** Scenario 1 stacked
   `Deception` + `Performance` + `Playing Harmless` for one lie. Body, Vehicle,
   Weapon and other tag menus are "paired into weight classes so no single
   pick outshines the rest," but Skills have no such guard. Invented Skills
   are checked only by "GM approval." A Skills-A character reaches the
   5-tag cap in their specialty with almost no effort.
2. **"Your First Skill Adds +1" is confusing.** It reads as if it grants
   something special to the *first* Skill, which suggests later Skills
   don't add dice. But the Tags section and the Intro say every applicable
   tag adds one die. As written the section adds nothing to the normal tag
   rule, and it invites the reading "only one Skill per roll." Either that
   *is* the intended rule (which would also fix issue 1), or the section
   should go.
3. **No minimum pool size.** Body 1 with an empty Body pool gives a base of
   **0**. With no tags that's a 0-die pool. Is the action an automatic
   failure, a 1-die roll, or something else? The rules don't say.
4. **Fragile characters can't die. They drain Coherence instead.** Body
   pool 1 means one hit empties it, and after that every loss comes out of
   the party's shared Coherence. There's no "taken out" rule. The frailest
   character in the party becomes a leak in everyone's shared resource.
   Coherence refill is also still a TODO.
5. **Weird 1 at Magic C is nearly useless.** It's the same size as an
   untrained Stat. Almost any Weird effect can be reframed as a Mind or Face
   action with a far bigger pool (scenario 4). Magic C's 3 tags mostly
   end up stacking onto Mind/Face rolls rather than enabling Weird ones.
6. **Magic without tags.** "Tags… are not a prerequisite or a recipe for
   casting." So a Magic-2 character can attempt *any* Weird effect (conjure
   gold, teleport) with 1 die and no relevant tags. What do tags *restrict*,
   if anything? What stops a Magic-D character from casting with Weird 0
   plus a mundane Skill tag?
7. **"What resists?" is written for all actions but only tested on magic.**
   How to Play says to pick the Stat "that fits what pushes back against the
   action." For mundane healing, carrying a willing passenger, or cooking,
   the literal answer lands on Weird. That's a trap for non-magical
   characters.
8. **Do Feats need rolls?** Read Minds and Turn Invisible read as
   automatic, but the magic table has "Read the thoughts of a suspect…" as a
   Face roll and "Turn invisible" as a Weird roll. Is the Feat a free
   version of the spell? Then a Feat makes those spells redundant. Is it the
   right to *attempt* the spell? Then how is it different from casting with
   no tags?
9. **Skill-sourced impossible Feats across realms.** *Master Misdirection*
   invisibility makes sense against bandits. Does it fool a thermal camera
   in Cyberpunk or a sensor sweep in Space? Feats are supposed to "work
   identically regardless of source," but the fiction of a skill-sourced
   Feat keeps pushing against that.
10. **Item Feats vs. losing Stuff.** A Stuff Feat "comes from one specific,
    ownable item." Stuff can be stolen and is only restored in the next
    realm, yet Feats "always travel with you." If a thief takes Harold's
    hearing aid, does she lose Speak with Animals for the rest of the realm?
    Does that item cost points? (The reference build hit the same question.)
11. **"Legendary weapon" appears at the wrong tier.** The Priority Matrix
    says Stuff B gets "a legendary weapon," and the Stuff table says rank 3
    is "Nice Stuff — a legendary weapon." But the Weapon catalogue puts
    "Legendary Blade" at **Tier 4**, which Stuff B can't buy (Tier 3 is
    "Enchanted Blade").
12. **Example skills that aren't in the catalogue.** `Larceny` (How to Play
    example) and `Driving` (Stats from Objects example) don't exist. The
    catalogue has `Piloting`, and nothing for locks or pickpocketing.
13. **Item tier makes the Body Stat irrelevant in a fight.** A 3-point cane
    gives Body 1 Odile the same combat base as a Body-3 warrior. That's
    great for this concept ("the cane does the work"), but it makes Body
    Stat Points a poor buy for anyone who plans to fight with a weapon. Body
    mostly buys pool size and unarmed or athletic rolls.
14. **No hook for being underestimated.** Body D gives no tags, and there
    are no negative or "flaw" tags, so the character's defining trait can
    only go on the sheet as an invented *Skill*. That's why
    `Playing Harmless` exists, and it's also why it's suspiciously broad
    (it arguably applied to a Body roll in scenario 2).
15. **Lifestyle has no mechanics.** "Good tools" at Well Off: does that
    mean a tag, a base tier, or permission to attempt things? It's unclear
    whether a first-aid tin from Lifestyle counts toward scenario 4.
16. **Does Magic re-skin?** The realm chapters say Magic *is* hacking in
    Cyberpunk. It's unclear whether a tea-leaf witch's tags change look,
    or just keep working in unfamiliar surroundings.
17. **Target numbers are still lorem ipsum,** so none of the odds above can
    be judged against intended difficulty.

## Reflection on the Build Process

**What seemed good**

- The ABCD step took about ten seconds and forced a real trade-off. Putting
  Body at D and Skills at A *is* "an old woman who's done everything."
- The Stat arithmetic is easy to check. The double-cost 5th pip created a
  real choice (4/4 vs 5/2) and didn't feel like a tax.
- All four columns granting Feats means a Body-D character still gets
  three impossible things, all fitting the concept (invisible in a corner,
  reading faces, talking to pigeons). A weak Body didn't mean a weak
  character.
- Max(Tier, Stat) is elegant, and it's the rule that made this concept work
  mechanically. The frail woman with the Tier-3 cane is exactly as
  dangerous as she should be *while holding the cane*.
- The single flat Stuff Tag pool (4 tags across 3 items) was fast and made
  me think about which item deserved the personality.

**What seemed bad**

- Skills A plus invented skills is the most reliably strong thing on the
  sheet. Fourteen tags with no overlap guard meant every Face or Mind roll
  I built landed at 7–8 dice without trying.
- Body 1 is *mechanically* fragile in a way that punishes the party rather
  than the character. One point of pool, then Coherence.
- Magic C felt like an accessory. Three tags that mostly stack onto rolls
  I'd have made anyway, and a Weird of 1 that lost to mundane options every
  time I compared them.
- Several important answers depend on "GM call": whether invented skills
  are allowed, whether a Face skill applies to a Body roll, whether Feats
  need rolls. That's normal for a narrative game, but here those calls
  swing pools by 2–5 dice.

**What was cool**

- The "what resists?" question is a great interpretive tool when it
  works. In the alley, "his grip" gave Body, and in the canteen, "the
  hidden berth" gave Mind, both instantly.
- Re-skinning the scooter (pony-trap → mobility scooter → hover-chair) is
  delightful and needed no rules at all.
- Skill-sourced Feats (*Master Misdirection*, *Cold Reading*) let a
  completely mundane old woman do impossible things *as a matter of
  expertise*. That's a distinctive and flavourful design.
- Harold's urn plus Persistence Points plus tattoos is an excellent
  character hook, and the rules hand it over for free.

**What was disappointing**

- The seed's core idea, *survives by being underestimated*, has no
  mechanical support. Body D gives nothing, and "frail" can't be a tag. I
  had to invent a skill to put the concept on the sheet at all.
- Weird is the most distinctive Stat in the game, but at Magic C it barely
  exists. I never had a reason to roll it except to show it was worse.
- Many of the chapters that should frame play (target numbers, travel,
  realm tone) are still lorem ipsum, so I couldn't tell whether 7–8 dice
  is "competent" or "overwhelming."

**What was unclear**

- Whether multiple Skills stack, given the "First Skill Adds +1" section.
- What happens with a 0-die pool, and whether a PC can ever be taken out.
- Whether Feats like Read Minds and Turn Invisible are automatic or need a
  roll, and how they relate to the matching entries in the spell table.
- Whether an item that grants a Feat costs Stuff Points, and what happens
  to the Feat when the item is stolen.
- Whether the "what resists?" rule applies to mundane actions where nothing
  resists (healing, conjuring by hand, cooking).
- The "legendary weapon" wording at Stuff B against the Tier-4 Legendary
  Blade.

**Recommendations to improve the system**

1. **Settle multiple Skills per roll.** Either state "one Skill per roll;
   other tag types stack" (this also clears up the First Skill section), or
   add a clear anti-redundancy rule: "each tag must bring a distinct edge;
   two tags describing the same act count once."
2. **Set a pool floor and a "taken out" rule.** For example: pools never go
   below 1 die. And when a PC's pool is empty and they take another hit,
   they're *taken out of the scene*, and the party chooses between that and
   paying Coherence. That makes frailty the character's problem and gives
   the party a real choice.
3. **Split Feats from rolls explicitly.** A Feat *opens the door* (you can
   read minds at all, without spell tags), but a guarded mind still calls
   for the normal roll. Then list the Feat–spell overlaps in the spell table.
4. **Limit "what resists?" to contested actions.** Add: "If nothing resists
   a *mundane* action, it either just happens or uses the Stat for the
   difficulty involved (surgery is Mind). Weird is only for effects that are
   impossible without magic."
5. **Make Magic C worth taking.** Options: raise Weird to 1/2/3/4 (so
   Magic C is 2), or let Magic-C characters add their Weird as a bonus
   whenever a magic tag applies.
6. **Add an optional "flaw tag" at Body D** (Frail, Young, Tiny, Old). It
   adds a die when the flaw *helps* (being overlooked, fitting through a
   gap) and the GM may invoke it for complications. That supports a whole
   family of concepts (kids, the elderly, the sickly) that are currently
   invisible on the sheet.
7. **Fix the catalogue inconsistencies:** "legendary weapon" at Stuff B, the
   `Larceny`/`Driving` example skills, and whether a Feat's item costs
   points and survives theft.
