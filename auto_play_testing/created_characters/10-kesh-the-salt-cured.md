# Kesh, the Salt-Cured

*Built by: subagent, seed: Nothing but the shirt on their back — Stuff at D (a deserter, an escaped prisoner, a monk who renounced possessions; a fighter who makes do with bare hands and whatever's lying around), 2026-09-23.*

*Rules source: `rulebook.tex` + `chapters/`.*

## Concept

Prisoner Nine spent nineteen years in a warlord's salt-pits: digging by day,
fighting in the pit by night so the guards had something to bet on. Salt
got into everything. His skin went grey and hard as crust, and his lungs
learned to live on the brine-fog of the drowned lower galleries. The guards
woke the fighters at any hour, until one day he just stopped needing sleep.
He got out by swimming three hundred yards through a flooded drain shaft
that nobody else could survive.

A hill monastery took him in afterwards. He took one vow there and kept it:
*own nothing*. Every possession he ever had was a leash: the chain, the
number, the pit-fighter's collar. When the wound in the world opened, he
walked through it barefoot, with a brand on his cheek and nothing in his
hands. He fights with his hands, or with whatever's closest, and puts it
down afterwards.

## Four Scenarios

### 1. High Fantasy: bandits on the pass road

Five bandits stop a pilgrim cart on a mountain switchback. Kesh is walking
with the pilgrims. The leader grabs a girl. Kesh wants her free and the
leader on the ground. He has two ways to do it.

**Option A: whatever's lying around.** He kicks the axle-pole loose from
the cart and sweeps it into the leader's knees.

- **What resists?** The leader's body → **Body**.
- **Base:** he's acting *through* an object, so Max(axle's tier, Body 5).
  An unowned axle has no tier in the rules. Whatever the GM says, it can't
  beat 5, so the base is **5**.
- **Tags:** `Improvised Weapons` + `Strong` + `Fast` = **3**.
  (`Brawling` and `Natural Weapon` don't apply because he's holding a pole.)
- **Pool: 8 dice.**

**Option B: bare hands.** He steps in and drives two knuckles into the
nerve under the leader's arm, so the knife hand opens.

- **What resists?** The leader's body → **Body**. Is a paralysing strike
  "striking" (Body) or "changing a creature against its will" (Face)? See
  Rules Issues. Here it's treated as a strike.
- **Base:** Body **5**.
- **Tags:** `Brawling` + `Natural Weapon` + `Strong` + `Fast` +
  `Pressure Points` = **5 (at the cap)**. `Control` and magic-`Body` would
  make 7, and the cap cuts them.
- **Pool: 10 dice.** That's the game's maximum, from a man who owns
  nothing.

The monk with empty hands out-rolls the monk with a weapon, which fits the
character. But it also means **Stuff D costs him nothing** on the rolls that
define him (see Rules Issues).

### 2. Cyberpunk: the ripperdoc's back door

Kesh is sheltering a runaway lab-kid in a clinic basement in the sprawl.
Two corporate retrieval enforcers turn up at the door. He doesn't want a
fight in a room full of patients, so he wants them to leave. Holding their
eyes, he crushes the steel door-frame in one hand, slowly.

- **What resists?** Their nerve, "a person's will" → **Face** (Magic
  chapter, *Which Base Stat?*).
- **Base:** Face **2**.
- **Tags:** `Intimidation` + `Strong` (the crushed frame is the threat, so
  it clearly applies) = **2**. `Tough` and `Natural Weapon` are stretches;
  not counted.
- **Pool: 4 dice.**

**The competing reading:** *Taking a Track Down* (Shape of a Session) says
"a terrifying display of strength might roll **Body** to shake their Face."
Under that reading it's Body 5 + `Intimidation` + `Strong` +
`Natural Weapon` = **8 dice**. That's twice the pool, from the same fiction.
The two chapters contradict each other, and a sharp player will always pick
Body. Played here by the stricter "what resists" rule: **4 dice**.

Suppose he fails. The enforcers laugh, and the patients see the branded
monk back down. He loses **1 Face (2 → 1)**, and now the fight he wanted to
avoid is on.

### 3. Space Adventure: the vented freighter

Someone has sabotaged the freighter *Ninth Mercy* and blown the mid-deck
airlocks. The crew is sealed in the fore section, and the saboteur is
hiding somewhere in the vacuum-dark aft.

- **Walking into vacuum:** **no roll.** Needs No Air is Continuous.
- **30 hours of searching without rest:** **no roll.** Never Sleep.
- **Crossing a sheared corridor gap:** **no roll**, because nothing's in
  doubt. Blink.
- **Finding the saboteur:** frost has formed on every surface since the
  vent. Kesh reads the scuffs in it the way he read fighters' feet in the
  pit.
  - **What resists?** Something hidden → **Mind**.
  - **Base:** Mind **5**.
  - **Tags:** `Perception` (tracking is listed under it) = **1**.
    `Survival` covers "navigation, foraging, shelter", which isn't tracking
    here; not counted.
  - **Pool: 6 dice.**
- **Afterwards: opening the sealed bulkhead from the ship's console.** It's
  a Mind action through an object: Max(console tier 3, Mind 5) = **5**. He
  has no `Hacking` or `Craft`, so no tags. **Pool: 5 dice.** Once again the
  object's tier does nothing for him.

This scene shows how much the Feats carry him. Three potential obstacles
(air, fatigue, the gap) disappear before the dice come out.

### 4. High Fantasy: back to the salt-pits (pools running dry, and Coherence)

Kesh leads the party back to the pits to free the fighters he left behind.
It's a long night. The party's **Coherence is 5** at the start. His pools
are full on arrival (the rules refill pools on entering a new world):
**Body 5 / Mind 5 / Face 2**.

| Beat | Action | Roll | Result | Sheet after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tunnel fights against guard patrols, several exchanges | Body 5 + `Brawling`, `Natural Weapon`, `Strong`, `Fast`, `Pressure Points` = **10** | Two failures and two partials over the fight; the GM takes a Body point each time | Body **1** |
| 2 | He shouts to the chained fighters to rise up (Face: their fear resists) | Face 2 + `Intimidation` ("commanding") = **3** | Fail: they spit at the man who ran and left them | Face **1** |
| 3 | He tries again, citing the monastery's teaching (`Lore`: "other Stats may apply if the fiction supports it") | Face 2 + `Intimidation` + `Lore` = **4**. Under the strict "first Skill" reading, possibly only **3**; see Rules Issues | Fail | Face **0** → **−1 die on all Face rolls** |
| 4 | The Warden fights him in the old pit with a hooked chain | Body 5 + 5 tags = 10 → Body pool is still 1, so **10** | Partial: the Warden goes down 1 Body, and the GM's cost takes his last Body point | Body **0** → **−1 die on all Body rolls** |
| 5 | Warden, round two | Body 5 + 5 tags − 1 = **9** | Fail. His Body pool is already empty, so the loss "does not stack" and **goes to Coherence** | Coherence **4** |
| 6 | The chain drags him toward the brine vat. The GM announced beforehand that a fall means 2 points. He tries to Blink out of the hold | **Which Stat?** The Magic table says teleporting out of a grapple is **Weird**, but his Blink is Skill-sourced "Impossible Speed", which is Body. Ruled Body here: 5 + `Fast` + `Athletics` − 1 = **6** | Fail: into the vat. 2 points, both to Coherence | Coherence **2** |
| 7 | Dragging himself out of the brine, he presses his own nerve-points to shut off the pain and close the gashes (healing yourself is **Weird**; nothing resists) | Weird 1 + magic-`Body` + `Pressure Points` + `Medicine` = **4**. Weird has no pool, so **no −1 penalty** | Success. How much Body does it restore? Nothing in the rules says; the GM rules 1 | Body **1**, penalty gone |
| 8 | Final blow on the Warden | Body 5 + 5 tags = **10** | Critical: the Warden is settled outright | — |
| 9 | The freed fighters see him beat the Warden. The GM rules that "earning recognition" restores Face | — | Face restored in full | Face **2** |

**Final state:** Body 1 / Mind 5 / Face 2 · **party Coherence 2**. Kesh took
3 of the party's shared 5 Coherence by himself in one night. Nothing in the
rules says whether it comes back.

Two things this scene exposes:
- Beat 7, **self-healing through Weird**, is the obvious way out once a pool
  is empty. It dodges the empty-pool penalty and pulls the character back
  above 0, and nothing limits how often he can do it.
- If Coherence had hit 0, the "reliable option" is to tear the party into
  the next realm, where **every pool refills in full**. The worst
  catastrophe in the game doubles as a full heal.

## Things Noticed While Building

**Contradictions**

- **"What resists?" vs. "a display of strength might roll Body to shake
  their Face."** The Magic chapter says a will resisting means Face. The
  Conflicts section openly allows Body to attack the Face track. For a
  Body-5/Face-2 character that's 8 dice against 4 on every intimidation
  (Scenario 2). The rulebook needs to say whether the *track attacked* and
  the *Stat rolled* can differ when a will is what resists, or whether that
  example is only meant for mindless or indirect effects.
- **"A Body-4 character can max one Stat… but cannot max two" is wrong once
  Skills points are added.** Body 4 + Skills 3 gives 11 points: two 5s plus
  a 2. Body 4 + Skills 4 gives 13: two 5s plus a 4. Either the sentence
  should say "from Body points alone", or a cap on maxed Stats is needed.

**Ambiguities**

- **"Your First Skill Adds +1" reads as if only the first Skill counts.**
  The Tags section says every applicable tag adds a die, but this heading
  suggests a second or third Skill might not. It matters in Scenario 4,
  beat 3 (`Intimidation` + `Lore`) and whenever `Brawling` +
  `Improvised Weapons` + `Athletics` all fit. The rule's actual intent
  (no penalty for having no Skill) should be stated without the "first"
  wording.
- **Nerve-strike / paralysis: Body or Face?** "Striking a creature is Body;
  *changing* a creature against its will is Face." Is a paralysing
  pressure-point strike a strike or a change? The same fiction can land on
  Body 5 or Face 2 depending on how the player describes it.
- **Which Stat for a Feat when a roll is needed?** Feats "function
  identically regardless of source", but the Magic table rules teleporting
  out of a grapple as Weird, and a Skill-sourced Blink ("Impossible Speed")
  is plainly physical. Weird also skips the empty-pool penalty, so the
  answer matters (Scenario 4, beat 6).
- **Unowned and improvised objects have no tier or tags.** "Stats from
  Objects" lists "a car, a rifle", with no mention of ownership. So can a
  Stuff-D character pick up a guard's Tier-3 rifle and roll with tier 3?
  The rules don't say. If yes, Stuff D is only a small penalty for anyone
  who can grab loot. Found items also have no rule for tags; I had to
  invent the `Improvised Weapons` Skill so the seed's "whatever's lying
  around" did anything at all.
- **How much does healing restore?** Weird healing of a willing target is
  defined, but not how many pool points a success gives back. "The GM
  decides" is the only guidance.
- **Magic Form `Body` has the same name as the Stat and Pool `Body`.**
  "Roll Body + `Body`" is confusing to say out loud at the table.
- **`Natural Weapon` for conditioned human fists** isn't on the listed
  examples (claws, fangs, horns, spines), so it's GM approval territory. It
  also stacks with `Brawling` on every punch, which looks like double-dipping
  on one fictional fact.

**Balance concerns**

- **Stuff D costs a Body-5 fighter almost nothing.** Max(Tier, Stat) can't
  raise a Body-5 base, because Stuff tops out at Tier 4 outside the
  capstone. So the only thing Stuff buys him on Body rolls is item tags,
  and Body A + Skills B already give him more applicable tags than the
  5-tag cap allows. His unarmed punch is **10 dice**, the game's ceiling
  (Scenario 1B). "Losing Your Stuff" can't touch him either, because
  there's nothing to smash, steal, or disarm. Stuff D really bites on Mind
  and Face rolls through tools, and on Piloting.
- **The 5-tag cap turns breadth into dead weight.** With 17 tags on the
  sheet, around 7 apply to any punch, and the cap throws the extras away.
  The Designer's Note exchange rate ("1 tag ≈ 0.5 Stat Point") breaks down
  above the cap. A character built this way hits 10 dice on their core
  action whatever the column split.
- **Weird dodges empty-pool penalties.** Weird has no pool, and routing
  actions through it (self-healing, and possibly Blink) skips the −1.
  Repeated Weird self-heals are an uncapped way to keep personal pools
  above 0 and keep losses out of Coherence.
- **Coherence has no refill rule** (a TODO in the book). And the suggested
  Coherence-0 catastrophe (pulled into the next realm) triggers the
  full pool refill on entering a new world. The worst outcome in the game
  is also a free full heal.
- **Target numbers are undefined.** The GM chapter's *Setting the Target
  Number* is lorem ipsum, so it's impossible to judge how a 4-die Face roll
  compares with a 10-die Body roll in practice, or how often criticals
  (sixes outnumbering the target) happen with pools of 10.
- **Feats remove whole obstacles.** In Scenario 3, three of four obstacles
  vanished with no roll. That's working as designed ("a door dice cannot
  open"), but a Body-A + Skills-B build gets 3 Feats even with Stuff D and
  Magic C, and the Feat-stacking cap is still an open TODO.

## Reflection on the Build Process

### What seemed good

- **The ABCD priorities made the seed easy to build.** "Nothing but the
  shirt on their back" translated straight to Stuff D, and the other three
  letters fell into place in about a minute. The priority matrix's prose
  ("Truly strange or monstrous") also pushed the concept to be better: a
  plain strong man wasn't allowed at Body A, so he turned into someone
  salt-cured and brine-lunged. The rules improved the fiction.
- **The per-column tables are consistent and quick to read.** Every column
  has the same shape (points, tags, Feats at 3/4), so building a character
  is mostly table look-ups, and the arithmetic is simple to audit.
- **"What resists?" is a strong rule when it gives a clear answer.** The
  tunnel fights, the frost tracking and the self-heal all picked their Stat
  instantly, and the choice felt earned.
- **Consequence pools and the empty-pool penalty did what they're for.**
  Watching Face 2 go to 0 in two failed rallying attempts, then taking −1
  die, turned a mechanical weak spot into the dramatic heart of Scenario 4.
  A Face-2 pool is very fragile, and that produced good story.
- **Coherence works as shared tension.** When Kesh's overflow cost the
  whole party three points, it felt like a real cost to the group, which is
  the intent.

### What seemed bad

- **Stuff D is nearly free for the build it was pitched at.** The seed
  asked for a fighter who "has to make do". In practice, Max(Tier, Stat)
  means a Body-5 character never gains anything from an item's tier, and
  Body A + Skills B already fill the 5-tag cap. So "making do" didn't cost
  him a single die on a punch. Stuff D only hurt on Mind and Face rolls
  through tools, which he didn't need. The low column should hurt in the
  character's own core area, and here it doesn't.
- **The tag cap is the real ceiling, and hitting it is easy.** With 17 tags
  on the sheet, most fights come down to "Stat + 5 = 10". A character who
  roughly doubles his available tags gets nothing for the extra ones.
  Creation rewards breadth, but play throws it away.
- **Contradictory Stat selection.** Having one chapter say "a will resists →
  Face" while another says "a display of strength can roll Body against
  Face" means every high-Body, low-Face character will try the second
  reading. That's a hole in the game's central decision.
- **Stat Points stack too well across columns.** Body A + Skills B produced
  *two* maxed Stats. The Body chapter's own text says that can't happen,
  which suggests the Skills Stat Points were never balanced against the
  5th-pip cost.

### What was cool

- **Three Feats carried a whole scene.** Walking into vacuum, searching for
  30 hours and Blinking across a sheared corridor, all with no rolls, felt
  like being a legend. The idea of Feats as "a door dice can't open" really
  lands.
- **Re-skinning is simple and satisfying.** One Feat or tag works as monk
  breath-discipline, cyberpunk biofeedback and space-opera Force-grump with
  no rules changes.
- **The empty-handed monk out-rolls the armed monk.** Bare hands (10 dice)
  beat the improvised axle (8 dice). It's thematically perfect for this
  character, even though it's the symptom of a balance problem above.
- **Magic C works as flavour, not a full wizard.** Three tags plus Weird 1
  gave him a small, specific mystical edge (pressure points, self-healing)
  that raised mundane rolls without taking over the character.

### What was disappointing

- **"Whatever's lying around" has no mechanical support.** The seed's most
  distinctive idea (the chair leg, the chain, the cart axle) had no rule. I
  had to invent a custom Skill to make it matter at all. Found objects have
  no tier, no tags and no guidance.
- **Nothing for the GM to take away.** "Losing Your Stuff" is a key
  pressure lever, and a Stuff-D character is immune to it. Disarm, theft
  and a broken sword don't touch him. The drama of having nothing
  disappears once nothing can be lost.
- **Target numbers are lorem ipsum.** I built a 10-die pool and a 4-die
  pool and couldn't say how different they feel at the table, because
  there's no guidance on difficulty. Most balance judgments are guesswork
  until that section exists.
- **Healing and Coherence are open ends.** The most dramatic beat (clawing
  back from 0 Body with a self-heal) came down to "the GM decides how
  much", and Coherence spent by the party never comes back.

### What was unclear

- Whether every applicable Skill adds a die, or only the first ("Your First
  Skill Adds +1").
- Whether a paralysing or crippling strike counts as "striking" (Body) or
  "changing a creature against its will" (Face).
- Which Stat a Feat rolls with when a roll is needed, especially when its
  source (a Skill-based Blink) contradicts the Magic table (teleporting out
  of a grapple is Weird).
- Whether Stats from Objects applies to unowned or found objects, and what
  tier they have.
- Whether pairs of Body Tags in the same "weight class" (e.g. `Strong` +
  `Natural Weapon`) are meant to be taken together, and whether they should
  both stack on the same punch.
- How much a successful heal restores, and whether it can take a pool above
  what was lost.
- Whether a Body-A character *must* be non-human, or whether "truly strange"
  just means exceptional.

### Recommendations to improve the system

1. **Make low Stuff cost something in the character's core area.** Options:
   (a) let item tier *add* a die when it's at least your Stat, instead of
   Max(Tier, Stat), so gear helps strong characters too; or (b) make
   improvised or found items explicitly Tier 1–2 with 0–1 tags that break
   on a failure, so a Stuff-D character can grab things but can't rely on
   them. Also give the GM an equivalent lever for Stuff D (for example,
   they're always the one without supplies, a disguise or a way in).
2. **Settle Stat selection in one place.** Pick one rule: either the Stat
   is always chosen by *what resists*, or you may roll any Stat against
   any track. Then rewrite the other chapter's example to match. My
   recommendation: keep "what resists" for choosing the Stat, and say that
   a display of strength is rolled with Face with `Strong` counting as a
   tag.
3. **Rethink the 5-tag cap, or reduce tags handed out at the top ranks.**
   Right now high-rank columns give tags that can't be used. Either (a)
   lower Skills Known and Body Tags at ranks 3–4, or (b) change the cap to
   something like "a maximum of 2 tags from any one column", so breadth
   across columns matters and single-column stacking stops.
4. **Bound Stat Points across columns.** Either limit how many Stats can
   start at 5 (e.g. only one), or charge more for the 5th pip, and correct the Body chapter's "cannot max two" sentence
   either way.
5. **Rename "First Skill Adds +1".** Something like "No Skill, No
   Penalty", with a clear line saying every applicable Skill adds its die.
6. **Specify Feat rolls.** Add one line: "When a Feat needs a roll, choose
   the Stat by what resists, as with any action; the Feat's source column
   never changes the Stat."
7. **Stop Weird from bypassing pools.** Either give Weird a pool of its own
   that drains Coherence directly, or say a Weird self-heal can't be used
   while any of your pools is at 0 (or restores only 1 point, once per
   scene).
8. **Write the Coherence refill rule, and make sure a Coherence-0
   catastrophe isn't also a full heal.** For example: tearing into the next
   realm refills pools *but* Coherence only returns to 3, or everyone
   arrives with one pool empty.
9. **Fill in target numbers.** Even a short ladder (1 = routine, 2 = hard,
   3 = heroic, 4+ = legendary) with notes on how it plays against pools of
   3, 6 and 10 would make every other balance conversation possible.
