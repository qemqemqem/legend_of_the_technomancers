\section*{Stat Plates --- a Rectangle Means ``Roll This''}

A Stat is an icon and a number in a rectangle. The rectangle is the whole
signal: rectangle means a number you roll with, oval means a tag.

\bigskip
\statplate{body}{3}\quad \statplate{mind}{2}\quad \statplate{face}{4}

\bigskip
With the name spelled out, for the character sheet:

\bigskip
\statplatenamed{body}{Body}{3}\quad
\statplatenamed{mind}{Mind}{2}\quad
\statplatenamed{face}{Face}{4}

\bigskip
Inline in a sentence: Mara vaults the gap on \statplate{body}{3} plus her
Athletics \dice{2}, so she throws \dice{5} dice. She would have talked her
way past instead on \statplate{face}{1}, which is why she jumped.

\section*{Tag Ovals --- and Where the Tag Came From}

A tag is a word in an oval carrying a small provenance icon. Once tags come
from body, stuff, skills \emph{and} magic, that icon is what keeps a sheet
legible --- you can see instantly which of your tags can be taken away from
you.

\bigskip
\atrtag{magic}{Fire}\quad
\atrtag{magic}{Create}\quad
\atrtag{body}{Winged}\quad
\atrtag{body}{Armoured}\quad
\atrtag{skill}{Piloting}\quad
\atrtag{item}{Blessed Blade}\quad
\atrtag{item}{Grapnel}

\bigskip
The filled variant marks a tag that is live on the current roll:

\bigskip
\atrtagon{magic}{Create}\quad
\atrtagon{magic}{Water}\quad
\atrtagon{magic}{Food}\quad
\atrtag{magic}{Destroy}\quad
\atrtag{magic}{Flying}

\smallskip
\begin{example}
Rooster conjures a can of soda. Base \statplate{body}{2}; three tags apply
and rank~\rating{3} permits three, so that is \dice{5} dice. The greyed
ovals are tags Rooster has but cannot bring to bear here.
\end{example}
