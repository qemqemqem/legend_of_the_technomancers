\chapterart{col-body.png}

\lettrine{B}{ody} is your physical self --- the shape and substance you
carry from world to world. Your Body rank sets three things at once: how
many points you have to raise your base \keyword{Stats}, how many
\keyword{Body Tags} describe your physical form, and --- if you rank high
enough --- which \keyword{Special Feats} let you do things no ordinary
roll of the dice can.

% ---- Section 1: Character Creation ---------------------------------
\section{At Character Creation}

Your Body rank --- set when you assigned priorities at character
creation, see the table above --- grants three things: Stat Points, Body
Tags, and, at rank \rating{3} and \rating{4}, Special Feats. The next
three subsections cover each in turn.

\subsection*{Spending Your Stat Points}
All three Stats --- \keyword{Body}, \keyword{Mind}, and \keyword{Face} ---
start at \dice{1}. Spend your Stat Points to raise them, following one
rule: \emph{the fifth pip costs double}.

\begin{statblock}[Cost to Raise a Stat]
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.42\linewidth} Y@{}}
\dice{1}~$\to$~\dice{2} & \dice{1} point\\[2pt]
\dice{2}~$\to$~\dice{3} & \dice{1} point\\[2pt]
\dice{3}~$\to$~\dice{4} & \dice{1} point\\[2pt]
\dice{4}~$\to$~\dice{5} & \dice{2} points (double)\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{statblock}

Maxing a single Stat all the way to \dice{5} costs \dice{5} points total
--- so a Body-\rating{4} character can max one Stat and still have real
points left over, but cannot max two.

\begin{example}
Kira is Body \rating{2}: \dice{4} points. She raises Face from \dice{1} to
\dice{3} (\dice{2} points) and Mind from \dice{1} to \dice{3}
(\dice{2} points): \textbf{Body~1, Mind~3, Face~3}.
\end{example}

\subsection*{Choosing Your Body Tags}
A \keyword{Body Tag} is a word in an oval that describes something your
body can simply \emph{do} --- \emph{Strong}, \emph{Fast}, \emph{Keen
Senses}. Whenever a tag clearly applies to what you're doing, add one die
for it to your pool, exactly like a Magic tag. Pick your tags from the
Common Body Tags list at the end of this chapter, or invent your own with
your GM's approval.

Body Tags are unlike every other resource on your sheet: they are
\emph{inherent}. No one can steal them, break them, or take them away --
they travel with you always. When you cross into a new realm, your tags
stay exactly as powerful, even as they take on a new shape: \emph{Strong}
might describe an ogre's muscle in one world and a mech suit's hydraulics
in the next. See each genre chapter for local translations.

\subsection*{Special Feats}
Some things are not a bigger pool --- they are a door that dice alone
cannot open. \keyword{Special Feats} are exceptional, often impossible
capabilities: flight, reading minds, becoming shadow. At Body \rating{3}
you choose \dice{1} Special Feat; at Body \rating{4} you choose \dice{3}
in total. Draw from the Special Feats catalogue at the end of this
chapter, or work with your GM to build one suited to your realm.

Like Body Tags, a personal Special Feat re-skins rather than disappears
when you change worlds: a dragon's wings become a jetpack, a psychic's
mind-reading becomes a neural hack. It belongs to \emph{you}, not the
setting, so it always travels.

\begin{notebox}[World-Granted Feats]
Not every Special Feat is personal. Some are a property of a
\emph{realm} rather than a character --- an ambient rule the GM sets up
that lets \emph{every} traveller in that world do one impossible thing,
whether or not they spent a single point on Body. In High Fantasy,
anyone present may \keyword{Appeal to the Gods}; no one bought it, and no
one carries it home. If you want to keep a world-granted Feat after you
leave its realm, spend a \keyword{Persistence Point} to make it
permanently yours, exactly as you would fix any other feature in place.
Each genre chapter offers its own example.
\end{notebox}

% ---- Section 2: Rules -----------------------------------------------
\section{Body in Play}

The \keyword{Body} Stat serves double duty: it measures raw physical
power \emph{and} the size of your Body pool --- how much physical harm
you can shrug off before you go down. Mind and Face work the same way
for mental strain and social standing; see \emph{Consequences and Your
Pools} in \emph{How to Play}.

Body rank also determines what physical form you take when you cross
into a new realm --- an exceptional body stays exceptional, but it wears
a different shape. Your Stats, Tags, and Feats all travel with you; only
their fictional dressing changes.

% ---- Section 3: Options ---------------------------------------------
\section{Your Body}

\subsection*{Common Body Tags}
Ten tags, paired into five weight classes so no single pick outshines the
rest --- each pair covers the same kind of ground at roughly the same
strength.

\skillcat{Power}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \atrtag{body}{Strong} --- Lift, break, and carry what ordinary people cannot.
  \item \atrtag{body}{Natural Weapon} --- Claws, fangs, horns, or spines that need no forging.
\end{itemize}

\skillcat{Speed \& Grace}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \atrtag{body}{Fast} --- Outrun, outreact, and strike before others can respond.
  \item \atrtag{body}{Graceful} --- Move with unnatural balance and precision, even where others would stumble.
\end{itemize}

\skillcat{Resilience}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \atrtag{body}{Tough} --- Shrug off wounds, poison, and exhaustion that would fell anyone else.
  \item \atrtag{body}{Regenerating} --- Recover from injury and hardship faster than anyone has a right to.
\end{itemize}

\skillcat{Senses \& Stealth}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \atrtag{body}{Keen Senses} --- See, hear, or smell far beyond human limits, day or night.
  \item \atrtag{body}{Camouflage} --- Your form blends into your surroundings at will.
\end{itemize}

\skillcat{Presence}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \atrtag{body}{Striking} --- Beautiful, magnetic, or impossible to ignore.
  \item \atrtag{body}{Intimidating} --- Your presence alone puts people on edge.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Special Feats}
Eight feats to start from, all common --- available in any realm and
reflavoured to fit. Each one bypasses exactly one hard constraint (gravity,
privacy of thought, the need to breathe, and so on), which keeps them
roughly matched in power even though their flavour differs wildly.
World-granted Feats, like High Fantasy's \emph{Appeal to the Gods}, are
not drawn from this list --- see the notebox above, and each genre
chapter for its realm's example.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \keyword{Fly} --- Take to the air under your own power.
  \item \keyword{Read Minds} --- Perceive surface thoughts and feelings directly.
  \item \keyword{Form Shadow} --- Become or merge with shadow, briefly incorporeal.
  \item \keyword{Needs No Air} --- Water, smoke, poison gas, even the vacuum of space; none of it can suffocate you.
  \item \keyword{Never Sleep} --- You do not need rest the way others do, and are never caught unaware by the night.
  \item \keyword{Speak with Animals} --- Every beast understands you, and you it.
  \item \keyword{Walk Through Walls} --- Solid matter is a suggestion, not a barrier.
  \item \keyword{Turn Invisible} --- Vanish from sight at will, for as long as you hold still enough to matter.
\end{itemize}
