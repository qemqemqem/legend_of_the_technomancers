\lettrine{S}{tuff} is what you carry from world to world. It covers your
equipment, your wealth, and the larger things you stake out and call your
own --- bases, ships, fortresses. Rank determines not just what you have,
but how much of the world bends to you.

\subsection*{Re-Kitting on Arrival}

The catalogue is deliberately \keyword{generic}. What you own is a
\emph{kind} of thing --- a mount, a special weapon, a base --- and you
decide what that thing actually \emph{is} each time you enter a new realm.
A \emph{special weapon} might be a flaming sword under a fantasy sun and a
plasma rifle in the sprawl; it is the same line on your sheet either way.
Each genre chapter lists suggested translations, so you always have a
world-appropriate version ready to hand.

\section{Vehicles}
\artbanner{vehicle-steed.png}
A vehicle carries you, your party, and your gear across the realm --- and,
with a \keyword{Persistence Point}, from one realm into the next without
changing shape. Vehicles come in three levels, each a step up in speed,
capacity, and sheer presence.

\gearentry{Mount}{2}{The humblest way to travel faster than your feet. A
mount is a single-rider workhorse: it gets you there, carries a little
cargo, and asks little in return. It has no armour to speak of and no room
for the whole party, but it is yours, and it is quick.}
\genretrans{a warhorse in high fantasy, a motorbike in the neon 1980s, a hoverboard on the station concourse.}

\gearentry{Basic Vehicle}{3}{A proper conveyance with room for a few
companions and their kit. It shrugs off rough roads, hauls real cargo, and
can take a knock without falling apart. This is the workaday vehicle of the
realm --- reliable, unremarkable, and exactly enough.}
\genretrans{a covered cart or coach, a family car, a battered cargo skiff.}

\gearentry{Advanced Vehicle}{4}{Fast, tough, and built to turn heads or
win fights. An advanced vehicle outruns, out-hauls, or out-fights anything
short of a named threat. It carries the whole party in comfort or in
armour, and it changes what encounters are even possible.}
\genretrans{a racing chariot or war-wagon, a race car or armoured van, a gunship or a battle tank.}

\section{Everything Else}
The remaining gear follows the same pattern: pick the line, pay the rank,
and decide what it looks like in the realm you are standing in.

\begin{gearlist}{Lifestyle \& Cash}
\gear{The Basics}{2}{Some cash, appropriate clothes, and basic tools. You get by.}
\gear{Well Off}{3}{Well dressed, generally nice things, spending money, good tools.}
\gear{Rich}{4}{The finest version of every tool you need, fine clothes, and a wallet flush with cash.}
\end{gearlist}

\begin{gearlist}{Weapons}
\gear{Basic Weapon}{2}{A sword, a pistol --- a dependable killer.}
\gear{Special Weapon}{3}{Something with a trick to it: a flaming sword, a bazooka, or the like.}
\gear{Legendary Weapon}{4}{A weapon out of story --- world-shaping, named, and feared.}
\end{gearlist}

\begin{gearlist}{Armour}
\gear{Light Armour}{2}{Padding and leathers --- turns aside the first, glancing blow.}
\gear{Heavy Armour}{3}{Plate, a ballistic vest, a hardsuit --- real, worn protection.}
\gear{Master Armour}{4}{Armour of legend or high craft, protection few can match.}
\end{gearlist}
