\chapter*{The Wound in the World}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{The Wound in the World}

\artinline{story-threshold.png}

\begin{readaloud}
Three travellers stood at the edge of an impossible door.
It had no frame. No hinges. No lock. It was simply a wound
in the hillside --- a slit of white-gold light burned into
the heather and earth, spilling radiance across the slope
as if the sky had split and forgotten to close again.
\end{readaloud}

\lettrine{M}{ara} felt it before she saw it. Something between
a sound and a pressure --- a low hum that climbed through the
soles of her boots and settled in her back teeth. She had tracked
outlaws across three kingdoms, broken a siege by daylight and a
cursed lock by torchlight, and never once felt her sword hand
tremble. It trembled now.

``You first,'' Kira said. She was leaning on her silver-topped
cane with the air of a woman waiting for a slow carriage, watching
the light the way she watched everything --- as if it were a
problem she had already halfway solved.

``Diplomatic of you.''

``Pragmatic. Your reflexes are better than mine.'' Kira tilted her
head a fraction. ``And you want to. I can see it.''

Mara could not deny it. The light pulled at her the same way a
good fight did --- the same bright certainty that something was
about to change, that the world was cracking open along a seam it
had kept hidden, and she could step through or she could not. She
had never been good at \emph{not}.

Behind them, Rooster cleared his throat. He was youngest of the
three, and the only one of them who had known from the beginning
what the wound was. He had read it in a text so old the vellum had
gone translucent, and he had spent six weeks tracking these two
down because he could not go through alone. His coat was patched at
both elbows. His left hand glowed faintly at the knuckles --- a
symptom of the working he had done to find this place, power he
had not quite finished spending.

``It leads somewhere,'' he said. ``It has to. The records call
these things \emph{thresholds}. Not doors, exactly. More
like\ldots{} the space between a word and its meaning.''

``Illuminating,'' Kira said.

``The world on the other side isn't the one we left. It's ---
adjacent. Related. The same story told differently. Everything we
know will still be true. It'll just look different. Feel different.
Sound different.'' He hesitated. ``Smell different.''

Mara reached into the light. Just her fingers, the way you test
water.

The warmth that came back was not heat. It was \emph{velocity}.
Motion she could feel but not name, the sensation of a city at
night moving too fast to hold in mind all at once, neon and
rain and a thousand strangers with a thousand destinations and no
one looking at the sky.

She stepped through.
