\begin{notebox}[Designer's Note: Body Against Skills]
Body and Skills split the three Stats between them. Body sets your
\keyword{Body} Stat and adds Body Tags --- what you \emph{are}. Skills
buys \keyword{Mind} and \keyword{Face} and adds Skills Known --- what
you have \emph{learned}. Because each column gets its own letter, no
character can be \keyword{A} in both, so no one starts with two Stats
at \dice{5}. The strongest spread anyone can build is
\textbf{5/3/3}: Body \keyword{A}, Skills \keyword{B}.
\todo{Rebalanced 2026-09-24. Skills now grants more Stat value than
Body (two Stats to Body's one) while Body carries more tags; the old
"1 tag $\approx$ 0.5 Stat Point" parity check no longer lines up.
Confirm the two columns feel equally worth taking in play.}
\end{notebox}

\lettrine{S}{kills} cover everything mundane and learned --- combat
technique, social grace, technical knowledge, and physical discipline.
Where your Stats describe raw potential, Skills describe what you have
actually practised. A high \keyword{Body} means you move well; the
\keyword{Blades} skill means you know how to use a sword.

\section{At Character Creation}

\rating{2} & You know a lot.\\[3pt]
\rating{3} & A master of your craft, or a jack of all trades.\\[3pt]
\rating{4} & A master of many things.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{statblock}

Your Skills rank --- set when you assigned priorities at character
creation, see the table above --- grants Stat Points and a number of
Skills Known, and, at rank \rating{3} and \rating{4}, Special Feats.
Your Stat Points go to \keyword{Mind} and \keyword{Face}. Skills
Feats are legendary but mundane; see \emph{Special Feats} for the rules
and the list.

\subsection*{Spending Your Stat Points}
Mind and Face both start at \dice{1}. Spend your Stat Points to raise
them, following one rule: \emph{the fifth pip costs double}.

\begin{statblock}[Cost to Raise a Stat]
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.42\linewidth} Y@{}}
\dice{1}~$\to$~\dice{2} & \dice{1} point\\[2pt]
\dice{2}~$\to$~\dice{3} & \dice{1} point\\[2pt]
\dice{3}~$\to$~\dice{4} & \dice{1} point\\[2pt]
\dice{4}~$\to$~\dice{5} & \dice{2} points (double)\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{statblock}

Maxing a Stat all the way to \dice{5} costs \dice{5} points, so only
Skills \rating{4} can do it --- and then the other Stat stays at
\dice{2}. At Skills \rating{3} you might split evenly at 3/3 or lean
into one at 4/2; at Skills \rating{4}, 4/4 or 5/2.

\begin{example}
Kira is Skills \rating{3}: \dice{4} points. She raises Mind from
\dice{1} to \dice{3} (\dice{2} points) and Face from \dice{1} to
\dice{3} (\dice{2} points). With Body \rating{2}, her Stats are
\textbf{Body~2, Mind~3, Face~3}.
\end{example}

\subsection*{Choosing Your Skills}
% REAL RULE, 2026-09-22: Skills are boolean, exactly like Magic and Body
% tags --- you know a skill or you don't. There is no individual skill
% rank and nothing to spend points on beyond simply choosing which
% skills you know.
A \keyword{Skill} is a tag like any other --- the trained kind. You
either know it or you don't; there is no individual rank to raise. Pick
as many skills as your Skills Known allows from the catalogue below, or
invent your own with your GM's approval. Like any tag, a known skill
adds one die whenever it applies, and the first skill that applies to a
roll adds one extra (see \emph{Your First Skill Adds +1}, below).

% ---- Section 2: Rules -----------------------------------------------
\section{Skills in Play}

When you act, choose the skill that covers what you are doing, then pair
it with whichever \keyword{Stat} best fits how you are doing it. The skill
sets the domain; the Stat sets the approach. Roll Stat + Skill dice and
count successes.

\begin{example}
Mara vaults a canyon gap using \keyword{Body} \dice{3} and
\atrtag{skill}{Athletics}. As her first (and only) applicable Skill,
Athletics adds \dice{2} dice, so she rolls \dice{5} dice. Three come up 4 or
higher --- three successes. The GM had called for two. She lands clean,
with one success to spare.
\end{example}
\subsection*{Your First Skill Adds +1}
% REAL RULE, 2026-09-23: see \emph{Your First Skill Adds +1} in
% \emph{How to Play} for the full rule --- restated here so a player
% reading Skills in isolation has it too. N applicable Skills = N+1 dice;
% the bonus counts toward the five-tag cap.
A Skill has to clearly fit the action to count, same as any tag. Every
Skill that fits adds \dice{1} die, and the first one adds \dice{1}
extra: one Skill gives \dice{2} dice, two give \dice{3}, three give
\dice{4}. The bonus counts toward the five-tag limit on any roll, and
there is no penalty if no Skill fits. See \emph{Your First Skill Adds +1}
in \emph{How to Play} for the full rule.

\subsection*{Choosing What You Know}
With a fixed number of Skills Known, the real choice is which skills
make the cut --- a wide net of common skills so little catches you
flat-footed, or a shorter, stranger list built around what your
Traveller has actually lived through. An unlisted skill simply isn't
one you have; the fiction, and the GM, decide what that costs you.
