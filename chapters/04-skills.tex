\chapterart{col-skills.png}

\lettrine{S}{kills} cover everything mundane and learned --- combat
technique, social grace, technical knowledge, and physical discipline.
Where your Stats describe raw potential, Skills describe what you have
actually practised. A high \keyword{Body} means you move well; the
\keyword{Blades} skill means you know how to use a sword.

\end{tabularx}

Your Skills rank --- set when you assigned priorities at character
creation, see the table above --- grants two things: Stat Points and a
number of Skills Known. Stat Points feed the very same pool Body feeds
(see \emph{Spending Your Stat Points} in \emph{Body}), just at a lower
rate --- Skills sharpens what you can do, but Body is what actually
shapes your Stats.

\subsection*{Choosing Your Skills}
% REAL RULE, 2026-09-22: Skills are boolean, exactly like Magic and Body
% tags --- you know a skill or you don't. There is no individual skill
% rank and nothing to spend points on beyond simply choosing which
% skills you know.
A \keyword{Skill} is a tag like any other --- the trained kind. You
either know it or you don't; there is no individual rank to raise. Pick
as many skills as your Skills Known allows from the catalogue below, or
invent your own with your GM's approval. Like any tag, a known skill
adds one die whenever it applies --- no more, no less, regardless of
which column it came from.

% ---- Section 2: Rules -----------------------------------------------
\section{Skills in Play}

When you act, choose the skill that covers what you are doing, then pair
it with whichever \keyword{Stat} best fits how you are doing it. The skill
sets the domain; the Stat sets the approach. Roll Stat + Skill dice and
count successes.

\begin{example}
Mara vaults a canyon gap using \keyword{Body} \dice{3} and
\atrtag{skill}{Athletics}, so she rolls \dice{4} dice. Three come up 4 or
higher --- three successes. The GM had called for two. She lands clean,
with one success to spare.
\end{example}

\subsection*{Choosing What You Know}
With a fixed number of Skills Known, the real choice is which skills
make the cut --- a wide net of common skills so little catches you
flat-footed, or a shorter, stranger list built around what your
Traveller has actually lived through. An unlisted skill simply isn't
one you have; the fiction, and the GM, decide what that costs you.
