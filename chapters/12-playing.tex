\section{Degrees of Success}
% REAL RULE, 2026-09-22 design notes: tie = partial, beat = success,
% more 6s than the target = critical (overrides the tiers below it).
Once you have rolled your pool and counted successes --- each die showing
\dice{4}, \dice{5}, or \dice{6} --- compare the total to the target number
the GM set before you rolled.

\begin{statblock}[Reading the Result]
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.36\linewidth} Y@{}}
Fewer successes than the target & \keyword{Failure} --- it does not go your way.\\[3pt]
Successes equal to the target & \keyword{Partial Success} --- you get what you wanted, but at a cost or complication.\\[3pt]
More successes than the target & \keyword{Success} --- it goes exactly as you hoped.\\[3pt]
\dice{6}s rolled outnumber the target & \keyword{Critical Success} --- overrides every tier above.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{statblock}

Check for a critical first: if the number of dice showing \dice{6} is
\emph{greater than} the target, that critical result stands no matter
what your total success count was. Otherwise, read the result off your
total successes as above.

\begin{example}
Rooster forces a jammed blast door with \keyword{Body} \dice{2} and
\atrtag{skill}{Athletics} --- \dice{3} dice against a target of \dice{1}.
He rolls \dice{6}, \dice{6}, \dice{3}. Two dice came up \dice{4} or
better --- two successes, more than the target, an ordinary
\keyword{Success}. But two of those dice show a \dice{6}, and two is also
more than the target of \dice{1}. The critical check wins: Rooster gets a
\keyword{Critical Success} instead --- the door doesn't just open, it
comes off its hinges.
\end{example}
