\lettrine{L}{egend of the Technomancers} runs on one simple roll. Whenever
the outcome of an action is in doubt --- and only then --- you gather a
handful of six-sided dice, roll them all at once, and count your
\keyword{successes}. Everything else in this book is just a way of deciding
how many dice you get to throw.

\artbanner{play-dice.png}

\section{The Core Roll}
% REAL RULE: d6 dice pool, success on 4/5/6, beat GM target.
Build a pool of \dice{d6} dice, roll them, and count every die that comes
up \keyword{4, 5, or 6} --- each of those is one success. The GM sets a
target number; meet or beat it and you succeed. The more successes you
roll, the better it goes.

\begin{statblock}[The Core Roll]
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.30\linewidth} Y@{}}
\keyword{Dice} & Roll one \dice{d6} for each die in your pool.\\[3pt]
\keyword{Success} & Each die showing \keyword{4, 5, or 6} counts as one success.\\[3pt]
\keyword{Target} & The GM sets how many successes the task needs.\\[3pt]
\keyword{Result} & Meet or beat the target and you succeed.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{statblock}

Every pool is built the same way: a \keyword{base Stat}, plus \emph{one}
source of expertise. That source is either a \keyword{Skill} --- if you are
doing something mundane --- or the \keyword{Magic tags} that apply, if you
are doing something fantastic.
\[
\text{\keyword{Stat} (1--5)} \;+\; \text{\keyword{Skill} (0--5)} \;=\; \text{pool}
\]
\[
\text{\keyword{Stat} (1--5)} \;+\; \text{\keyword{applicable Tags}} \;=\; \text{pool}
\]

That is the whole engine. Magic is not a separate subsystem with its own
maths --- it is the same roll, reached by a different route.

\section{Stats}
% REAL RULE (Notion, 2026-09-21, decided with Chris): THREE broad base
% stats — Mind, Body, Face. No separate Magic/Arcane stat: magic hangs off
% these same three, which stops one versatile stat from dominating play.
Every character has three broad base \keyword{Stats}. Everyone begins with
\dice{1} in each; character creation can raise them as high as \dice{5}.

\begin{statblock}[The Three Stats]
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.30\linewidth} Y@{}}
\leavevmode{\color{\realmaccent}\atricon{body}}\ \keyword{Body} & Physical action and the handling of objects --- striking, lifting, running, throwing. It also serves as your \emph{durability}.\\[3pt]
\leavevmode{\color{\realmaccent}\atricon{mind}}\ \keyword{Mind} & Puzzles, analysis, knowledge, and perception.\\[3pt]
\leavevmode{\color{\realmaccent}\atricon{face}}\ \keyword{Face} & Dealing with people --- persuasion, deception, presence, command.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{statblock}

On the page and on your sheet, a Stat is written as its icon and its number
in a rectangle --- \statplate{body}{3}, \statplate{mind}{2},
\statplate{face}{4} --- while a tag is a word in an oval carrying a small
mark for where it came from: \atrtag{magic}{Fire} from magic,
\atrtag{body}{Winged} from your body, \atrtag{skill}{Piloting} from
training, \atrtag{item}{Grapnel} from something you carry. Rectangles are
numbers you roll with; ovals are the permissions that let you act at all.

These three cover the action whether it is mundane or magical. Shapeshifting
is \keyword{Body}; scrying is \keyword{Mind}; a charm laid on a guard is
\keyword{Face}. Classify by \emph{what the action accomplishes}, not by how
it is achieved --- so moving an object with your thoughts is still
\keyword{Body}.

\section{Stats from Objects}
% REAL RULE (Notion, 2026-09-21): an item can supply the base stat, using
% its tier. "Higher of the two" is the working idea, flagged for testing.
A base Stat does not have to come from your body. When you act
\emph{through} a thing --- a car, a rifle, a warhorse, a cutting deck ---
that object can supply the base Stat itself, using its \keyword{tier} in
place of your own number. Take the higher of the two.

\begin{example}
Kira drives a rank-\rating{3} car through a chase. Her own \keyword{Body} is
\dice{2}, but the car's tier is \dice{3}, so the car supplies the base. With
\keyword{Driving} \dice{2} she rolls \dice{5} dice.
\end{example}

\section{Skills}
% REAL RULE: skills 0-5, pair with a stat.
\keyword{Skills} also run from \dice{0} to \dice{5} and cover everything
mundane and learned --- driving, acrobatics, lore, stealth, and combat
skills such as blades or martial arts. When you act, pair the relevant
skill with whichever Stat best fits what you are attempting, then roll the
combined pool.

\begin{example}
Mara vaults a canyon gap. She has \keyword{Body} \dice{3} and the
\keyword{Athletics} skill \dice{2}, so she rolls \dice{5} dice. Three come
up 4 or higher --- three successes. The GM had called for two. She lands
clean, with one success to spare.
\end{example}

\section{Magic Rolls}
% REAL RULE (Notion, 2026-09-21, decided): magic pool = base stat + one
% point per applicable tag, capped by the tag limit for your Magic rank.
% This SUPERSEDES the older "two dice per tag" rule.
Magic uses the same roll. Take the base Stat that fits what you are
accomplishing, then add \keyword{one die for each applicable tag} you have
--- up to the tag limit set by your Magic rank. See \emph{Magic} for the
tag lists and limits.

\begin{example}
Rooster has \keyword{Body} \dice{2} and Magic \rating{3}, with the tags
\emph{create, destroy, fire, water, food, flying}. Conjuring a can of soda
is a physical result, so the base is \keyword{Body}. Three tags apply ---
\emph{create}, \emph{food}, \emph{water} --- and rank~3 permits three. That
is \dice{2}~+~\dice{3}~=~\dice{5} dice.
\end{example}

\begin{notebox}[Stats and the Four Columns]
The four columns of character creation are not the same thing as the three
Stats. \keyword{Body}, \keyword{Skills}, and \keyword{Magic} ranks tell you
how much you may buy in each area; \keyword{Stuff} governs the gear you
carry. The Stats --- Body, Mind, Face --- are what you actually roll.
\end{notebox}
