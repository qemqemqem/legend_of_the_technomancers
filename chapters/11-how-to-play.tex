\lettrine{L}{egend of the Technomancers} runs on one simple roll. Whenever
the outcome of an action is in doubt --- and only then --- you gather a
handful of six-sided dice, roll them all at once, and count your
\keyword{successes}. Everything else in this book is just a way of deciding
how many dice you get to throw.

\artbanner{play-dice.png}

\section{The Core Roll}
% REAL RULE: d6 dice pool, success on 4/5/6, beat GM target.
Build a pool of \dice{d6} dice, roll them, and count every die that comes
up \keyword{4, 5, or 6} --- each of those is one success. The GM sets a
target number; meet or beat it and you succeed. The more successes you
roll, the better it goes.

\begin{statblock}[The Core Roll]
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.30\linewidth} Y@{}}
\keyword{Dice} & Roll one \dice{d6} for each die in your pool.\\[3pt]
\keyword{Success} & Each die showing \keyword{4, 5, or 6} counts as one success.\\[3pt]
\keyword{Target} & The GM sets how many successes the task needs.\\[3pt]
\keyword{Result} & Meet or beat the target and you succeed.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{statblock}

Every pool is built the same way: a \keyword{base Stat}, plus whatever
\keyword{tags} apply. A tag is a word in an oval that says you have some
edge on this roll --- and it makes no difference which of the four
columns it came from.
\[
\text{\keyword{Stat} (1--5)} \;+\; \text{\keyword{applicable tags}} \;=\; \text{pool}
\]
\atrtag{skill}{Blades} from training, \atrtag{body}{Strong} from your
body, \atrtag{magic}{Fire} from magic, \atrtag{item}{Grapnel} from
something you carry --- all four look the same on the page, and all four
add to the same pool the same way. \keyword{Magic} is not a separate
subsystem with its own maths; it is the same roll, reached by a different
route.

\subsection*{One Source Per Roll}
% REAL RULE, 2026-09-22: tags on a single roll must all come from the
% same column. Andrew flagged this needs playtesting before it's final.
Every tag you add to a pool must come from the \emph{same} column ---
\keyword{Body}, \keyword{Stuff}, \keyword{Skills}, or \keyword{Magic}.
You cannot reach into two columns on one roll: a \atrtag{body}{Strong}
tag and a \atrtag{skill}{Blades} tag never stack together, even if both
would plausibly apply. Pick the single source that best fits the action
before you count your tags.

\begin{example}
Mara swings a sword she can barely lift. She has \atrtag{body}{Strong}
and \atrtag{skill}{Blades}, and both would help --- but she must choose
one column, not both. She picks \keyword{Skills}: \keyword{Body}
\dice{3} plus \atrtag{skill}{Blades} is \dice{4} dice. Her raw strength
does not also pile on.
\end{example}

to remember, but may be too restrictive --- or, if it turns out tags
already self-limit in practice, too paranoid a guardrail. Validate
whether allowing tags from multiple columns on one roll is actually
"busted" before locking this in.}

\subsection*{Five Tags, Maximum}
% REAL RULE, 2026-09-22: one flat cap for the whole system, replacing
% Magic's old per-rank "Tags per Spell" column. Simpler to remember, and
% a Magic-rank-scaled limit was redundant with Tags Known anyway (a
% low-rank caster doesn't know enough tags to hit five in the first
% place).
No matter the source, a single roll never adds more than \dice{5} tags
to its pool. This is the one limit that applies everywhere --- Body,
Stuff, Skills, and Magic alike --- so you never need to look up a
different cap depending on what column you're rolling from.

\section{Consequences and Your Pools}
% REAL RULE, 2026-09-22: each Stat has a companion pool (size = Stat
% rating) that pays the price when a roll built on that Stat fails. A
% shared party Coherence pool (starts at 5) absorbs the overflow once a
% Stat's own pool runs dry. Full adjudication advice lives in The GM's
% Role, in The Shape of a Session.
Failure is not just a missed roll --- it costs something. Each of your
three Stats has a companion pool the same size as its rating: a
\keyword{Body} pool, a \keyword{Mind} pool, and a \keyword{Face} pool.
When you fail a roll, the GM may take a point from the pool that the
consequence threatens. Usually it matches the Stat you rolled --- a
failed Body roll costs Body, a failed Face roll costs Face --- but a
failed attempt to outwit a foe could leave you physically hurt instead.

\begin{statblock}[Your Three Pools]
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.22\linewidth} Y@{}}
\keyword{Body Pool} & Physical harm --- wounds, exhaustion, injury.\\[3pt]
\keyword{Mind Pool} & Mental strain --- fear, confusion, a different kind of exhaustion.\\[3pt]
\keyword{Face Pool} & Social standing --- humiliation, broken trust, burned bridges.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{statblock}

\subsection*{Coherence: The Party's Last Line}
When a pool would lose a point and it is already empty, the loss comes
out of \keyword{Coherence} instead --- one shared pool for the whole
party, starting at \dice{5}. Coherence isn't tied to any one character;
it is the party's grip on the world they are currently standing in, and
everyone draws from the same reserve.

\todo{When does Coherence refill --- every session, on arrival in a new
realm, never without a specific in-fiction event? Not yet specified;

If Coherence ever reaches \dice{0}, something catastrophic happens, at
the GM's discretion --- the world itself has stopped holding together
around the party. One reliable option: they are torn loose from the
realm entirely and pulled into the next one, a clean and dramatic way to
close out a session. See \emph{For the Game Master} for more on running
this.

\section{Stats}
% REAL RULE (Notion, 2026-09-21, decided with Chris): THREE broad base
% stats — Mind, Body, Face. No separate Magic/Arcane stat: magic hangs off
% these same three, which stops one versatile stat from dominating play.
Every character has three broad base \keyword{Stats}. Everyone begins with
\dice{1} in each; character creation can raise them as high as \dice{5}.

\begin{statblock}[The Three Stats]
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.30\linewidth} Y@{}}
\leavevmode{\color{\realmaccent}\atricon{body}}\ \keyword{Body} & Physical action and the handling of objects --- striking, lifting, running, throwing.\\[3pt]
\leavevmode{\color{\realmaccent}\atricon{mind}}\ \keyword{Mind} & Puzzles, analysis, knowledge, and perception.\\[3pt]
\leavevmode{\color{\realmaccent}\atricon{face}}\ \keyword{Face} & Dealing with people --- persuasion, deception, presence, command.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{statblock}

On the page and on your sheet, a Stat is written as its icon and its number
in a rectangle --- \statplate{body}{3}, \statplate{mind}{2},
\statplate{face}{4} --- while a tag is a word in an oval carrying a small
mark for where it came from: \atrtag{magic}{Fire} from magic,
\atrtag{body}{Winged} from your body, \atrtag{skill}{Piloting} from
training, \atrtag{item}{Grapnel} from something you carry. Rectangles are
numbers you roll with; ovals are the permissions that let you act at all.

These three cover the action whether it is mundane or magical. Shapeshifting
is \keyword{Body}; scrying is \keyword{Mind}; a charm laid on a guard is
\keyword{Face}. Classify by \emph{what the action accomplishes}, not by how
it is achieved --- so moving an object with your thoughts is still
\keyword{Body}.

\section{Stats from Objects}
% REAL RULE (Notion, 2026-09-21): an item can supply the base stat, using
% its tier. "Higher of the two" is the working idea, flagged for testing.
A base Stat does not have to come from your body. When you act
\emph{through} a thing --- a car, a rifle, a warhorse, a cutting deck ---
that object can supply the base Stat itself, using its \keyword{tier} in
place of your own number. Take the higher of the two.

\begin{example}
Kira drives a rank-\rating{3} car through a chase. Her own \keyword{Body} is
\dice{2}, but the car's tier is \dice{3}, so the car supplies the base. With
\atrtag{skill}{Driving}, she rolls \dice{4} dice.
\end{example}

\section{Skills}
% REAL RULE, 2026-09-22: a Skill IS a tag --- not a separate resolution
% path, and not a ranked one either. You know it or you don't; a known
% skill adds exactly one die, same as any other tag.
A \keyword{Skill} is a tag like any other on your sheet --- the trained
kind. You either know it or you don't; there is no individual rank to
raise. \atrtag{skill}{Athletics} and \atrtag{skill}{Blades} each add
exactly one die when they apply, same as a Body, Magic, or Stuff tag.
Skills cover everything mundane and learned --- driving, acrobatics,
lore, stealth, and combat skills such as blades or martial arts. When
you act, pair the relevant skill with whichever Stat best fits what you
are attempting, then roll the combined pool.

\begin{example}
Mara vaults a canyon gap. She has \keyword{Body} \dice{3} and knows
\atrtag{skill}{Athletics}, so she rolls \dice{4} dice. Three come up 4 or
higher --- three successes. The GM had called for two. She lands clean,
with one success to spare.
\end{example}

\section{Magic Rolls}
% REAL RULE (Notion, 2026-09-21, decided): magic pool = base stat + one
% point per applicable tag. This SUPERSEDES the older "two dice per tag"
% rule. The old per-rank "Tags per Spell" limit was removed 2026-09-22
% in favour of the system-wide five-tag cap (see "Five Tags, Maximum"
% above) --- one rule to remember instead of a rank lookup.
Magic uses the same roll. Take the base Stat that matches what is
resisting you, then add \keyword{one die for each applicable tag} you
have --- up to the system-wide limit of \dice{5} tags on any roll. See
\emph{Magic} for the tag lists.

\begin{example}
Rooster has \keyword{Body} \dice{2} and Magic \rating{3}, with the tags
\atrtag{magic}{Create}, \atrtag{magic}{Destroy}, \atrtag{magic}{Fire},
\atrtag{magic}{Water}, \atrtag{magic}{Food}, \atrtag{magic}{Flying}.
Shattering a window across the street with a hurled fist of water is a
physical blow, so the base is \keyword{Body}. Two tags apply ---
\atrtag{magic}{Destroy}, \atrtag{magic}{Water} --- comfortably under the
five-tag cap. That is \dice{2}~+~\dice{2}~=~\dice{4} dice.
\end{example}

\begin{notebox}[Stats and the Four Columns]
The four columns of character creation are not the same thing as the three
Stats. \keyword{Body}, \keyword{Skills}, and \keyword{Magic} ranks tell you
how much you may buy in each area; \keyword{Stuff} governs the gear you
carry. The Stats --- Body, Mind, Face --- are what you actually roll.
\end{notebox}
