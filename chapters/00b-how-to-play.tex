\lettrine{A}{cross the Realms} runs on one simple roll. Whenever the
outcome of an action is in doubt --- and only then --- you gather a handful
of six-sided dice, roll them all at once, and count your \keyword{successes}.
Everything else in this book is just a way of deciding how many dice you
get to throw.

\artbanner{play-dice.png}

\section{The Core Roll}
% REAL RULE: d6 dice pool, success on 4/5/6, beat GM target.
Build a pool of \dice{d6} dice, roll them, and count every die that comes
up \keyword{4, 5, or 6} --- each of those is one success. The GM sets a
target number; meet or beat it and you succeed. The more successes you
roll, the better it goes.

\begin{statblock}[The Core Roll]
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.30\linewidth} Y@{}}
\keyword{Dice} & Roll one \dice{d6} for each die in your pool.\\[3pt]
\keyword{Success} & Each die showing \keyword{4, 5, or 6} counts as one success.\\[3pt]
\keyword{Target} & The GM sets how many successes the task needs.\\[3pt]
\keyword{Result} & Meet or beat the target and you succeed.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{statblock}

For an ordinary action, your pool is a \keyword{Stat} plus a \keyword{Skill}.
Each runs from \dice{0} to \dice{5}, so a pool is always somewhere between
\dice{0} and \dice{10} dice.
\[
\text{\keyword{Stat} (0--5)} \;+\; \text{\keyword{Skill} (0--5)} \;=\; \text{pool (0--10 dice)}
\]

\section{Stats}
% REAL RULE: four Vampire-style stats, 0-5. Strength doubles as Durability.
Every character has four basic \keyword{Stats}, each rated from \dice{0} to
\dice{5}, in the manner of \emph{Vampire: the Masquerade}.

\begin{statblock}[The Four Stats]
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.28\linewidth} Y@{}}
\keyword{Strength} & Physical power. It also serves as your \emph{durability} --- how much harm you can shrug off.\\[3pt]
\keyword{Agility} & Speed, reflexes, and precision.\\[3pt]
\keyword{Charisma} & Presence, persuasion, and force of personality.\\[3pt]
\keyword{Intellect} & Reason, knowledge, and quickness of mind.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{statblock}

\section{Skills}
% REAL RULE: skills 0-5, pair with a stat.
\keyword{Skills} also run from \dice{0} to \dice{5} and cover everything
mundane and learned --- driving, acrobatics, lore, stealth, and combat
skills such as blades or martial arts. When you act, pair the relevant
skill with whichever Stat best fits what you are attempting, then roll the
combined pool.

\begin{example}
Mara vaults a canyon gap. She has \keyword{Agility} \dice{3} and the
\keyword{Athletics} skill \dice{2}, so she rolls \dice{5} dice. Three come
up 4 or higher --- three successes. The GM had called for two. She lands
clean, with one success to spare.
\end{example}

\section{Magic Rolls}
% REAL RULE: magic pool = 2 dice per applicable tag, up to the tag limit,
% so it also lands in the 0-10 range.
Magic works the same way, but its pool comes from your \keyword{tags}
rather than a Stat and a Skill: you roll \keyword{two dice for every
applicable tag}, up to the tag limit set by your Magic rank. Because that
limit tops out at five tags, a magical action --- like a mundane one ---
always uses somewhere between \dice{0} and \dice{10} dice. See
\emph{Magic} for the full system.

\begin{notebox}[Stats and the Four Columns]
Your \keyword{Body} rank determines your starting Stats --- see \emph{Body}
for the full breakdown. The same pattern will apply to Skills and Magic: the
column rank sets your starting pool. Stuff is the exception: it governs what
gear you can carry, not a rolled stat.
\end{notebox}
