% REAL (from Notion): "Magic" is whatever makes a setting feel fantastic.
\lettrine{M}{agic} is whatever makes a setting feel fantastic --- literal
spellcasting in high fantasy, hacking in a cyberpunk sprawl, the Force
in a space opera, or tinkering and engineering in a world of hard science.
When you enter a realm, decide together what Magic \emph{looks like} there.

% ---- Section 1: Character Creation ---------------------------------
\section{At Character Creation}

Your Magic rank --- set when you assigned priorities at character
creation, see the table above --- determines how many \keyword{tags} you
know. It also sets your \keyword{Weird} Stat; see \emph{The Weird Stat}
below. At rank \rating{1} you know no magic at all. However many tags
you know, no single roll ever applies more than \dice{5} of them --- the
same system-wide cap that applies to every other kind of tag; see
\emph{Five Tags, Maximum} in \emph{How to Play}. At rank \rating{3} and
\rating{4} you also gain Special Feats; see \emph{Special Feats} for the
full catalogue and rules.

Choose your tags from Your Tags below before play begins. A tag's meaning
bends to fit the realm --- \emph{control} compels a beast in a fantasy
world and overrides a security system in the sprawl.

% ---- Section 2: Rules -----------------------------------------------
\section{How Magic Works}

% REAL RULE (Notion, 2026-09-21, decided with Chris): pool = base stat +
% one die per applicable tag. SUPERSEDES the earlier "two dice per tag".
A spell is rolled exactly like anything else: a \keyword{base Stat} plus
your expertise. For magic, that expertise is your tags. Take the Stat that
matches what \emph{resists} the spell, then add \keyword{one die for each
applicable tag} --- up to the system-wide limit of \dice{5} tags on any
roll. Count successes (\dice{4}, \dice{5}, or \dice{6}) against the
difficulty the GM sets.
\[
\text{\keyword{Stat} (1--5)} \;+\; \text{\keyword{applicable Tags}} \;=\; \text{pool}
\]
\todo{Confirm and state explicitly: casting requires at least one
applicable tag; with zero applicable tags the action cannot be attempted
as magic at all. Per 2026-09-22 design notes.}

Because magic runs off the same three Stats as everything else, there is no
such thing as a character who is good at ``magic'' in general. Telekinesis
leans on \keyword{Body}, divination on \keyword{Mind}, a glamour on
\keyword{Face} --- so the way you work magic expresses who you are.

\subsection*{Which Base Stat?}
% REAL RULE, 2026-09-22: no new mechanic here --- magic uses the three
% Stats exactly as anything else does. Stated explicitly because it's the
% single most common point of confusion for new players.
% REAL RULE, 2026-09-23: the Stat is chosen by asking "what resists?" ---
% the spell's appearance doesn't matter, only what pushes back.
A spell is just another action, so pick its Stat the way you would for
anything else, by asking one question: \keyword{what is resisting you?}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \keyword{Body} --- matter resists: weight, distance, gravity, a
    wall, a body.
  \item \keyword{Mind} --- something hidden or complicated resists: a
    secret, a puzzle, a lock, a system.
  \item \keyword{Face} --- a person's will resists: their nerve, loyalty,
    suspicion, or pride.
  \item \keyword{Weird} --- nothing resists but reality itself; see
    \emph{The Weird Stat} below.
\end{itemize}
Ask what resists, not what the spell looks like. A fireball that blasts a
door off its hinges is \keyword{Body}. The same fireball sent over the
guards' heads to make them run is \keyword{Face}: nobody is struck, and
what resists is their nerve. Cleverness doesn't change the answer either
--- melting a vault door by commanding its molecules is still
\keyword{Body}, because the steel is what pushes back. The table
\emph{Spells and Their Stats} on page~\pageref{tab:spellstats} works
through many more cases.

Striking a creature is \keyword{Body}: its hide and bulk resist.
\emph{Changing} a creature against its will --- turning it into a toad,
teleporting it into a cell, possessing it, banishing it --- is
\keyword{Face}, because what resists is its will to stay itself. A willing
creature doesn't resist at all, so healing an ally or reshaping your own
body is \keyword{Weird}.

As with any roll, a base Stat can also come from an item instead of your
body (see \emph{Stats from Objects} in \emph{How to Play}). Hacking a
mainframe through a computer terminal uses the terminal's tier as the
base, in place of your own \keyword{Mind}, if the terminal's tier is
higher.

\begin{question}{Does that mean my wizard should work out and get buff to move boulders with her mind?}
Yes. That is exactly how it works. Moving a boulder is a \keyword{Body}
action no matter how you accomplish it --- telekinesis included. A
wizard who wants to reliably shift heavy things with her mind should put
points into \keyword{Body}, same as anyone else who wants to be strong.
\end{question}

\subsection*{The Weird Stat}
% REAL RULE, 2026-09-22: a fourth base Stat, derived (not bought), equal
% to Magic rank minus 1. Only usable when nothing else fits.
Some magic doesn't lift anything, solve anything, or persuade anyone ---
it just happens, and reality bends to let it. Conjuring a can of soda,
making butterflies appear from nowhere, turning lead into gold: nothing in
the scene pushes back, so none of Body, Mind, or Face applies.

For exactly these moments, you have a fourth base Stat: \keyword{Weird}.
It equals your \keyword{Magic} rank minus one (\dice{0} at Magic
\rating{1}, up to \dice{3} at Magic \rating{4}), and it is the only Stat
you cannot buy directly --- it rises only when your Magic rank does.
Use Weird \emph{only} when nothing but reality resists; if matter, a
puzzle, or a will pushes back, use that Stat instead.

\keyword{Teleportation} is Weird. You never cross the space in between,
so the distance, the wall, or the grip you slip out of never gets the
chance to resist --- only reality does. Flying across the same chasm is
\keyword{Body}: you cross every metre, and gravity pulls the whole way.

Weird makes an effect happen; \emph{using} the effect is a separate roll.
Turning invisible is Weird. Sneaking past the guards while invisible is
whatever sneaking always is.

\begin{question}{So my weak wizard can just teleport the boulder instead of lifting it?}
Yes. The boulder's weight never gets to push back, so it is Weird. That is
fine: Weird never rises above \dice{3}, so a wizard who wants to move
boulders \emph{reliably} still does best to get strong.
\end{question}

Every genre chapter may set its own tone for what counts as Weird enough
to need it --- see each realm's Magic section for local guidance.

Every act of magic pairs what you \emph{do} (a Technique) with what you
\emph{affect} (a Form): \emph{create}~+~\emph{fire} to kindle a flame,
\emph{perceive}~+~\emph{mind} to read a thought.

\begin{example}
Rooster is a rank~\rating{3} magic user with \keyword{Body} \dice{2} and the
tags \emph{create, destroy, fire, water, food, flying}. They want to shatter
a window across the street by hurling a fist of water at it. Whatever the
means, the outcome is a physical blow landing on glass, so the base is
\keyword{Body}. Two tags apply --- \emph{destroy} and \emph{water} ---
comfortably under the five-tag cap. Rooster rolls
\dice{2}~+~\dice{2}~=~\dice{4} dice. (Conjuring a can of soda, by contrast,
strikes nothing and moves nothing; that is a job for \keyword{Weird}.)
\end{example}

% ---- Section 3: Options ---------------------------------------------
\section{Your Tags}

Choose your tags from the Techniques and Forms below, plus any Specific
tags you invent. The Techniques and Forms are drawn from \emph{Ars Magica};
the tag-selection system is inspired by John Harper's \emph{Lady Blackbird}.

\subsection*{Techniques --- what you do}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Create} --- Produce something from nothing, or restore a thing to its whole, ideal state.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Perceive} --- Sense, read, and learn; uncover the hidden connections between things.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Transform} --- Change one thing into another, and direct the mechanisms of change itself.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Destroy} --- Break down, wither, and unmake; hasten damage, decay, and dissolution.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Control} --- Move and command matter, or compel the actions of living things.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Forms --- what you affect}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Animal} --- Beasts of every kind, from the fish of the sea to the birds of the air.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Water} --- Liquids of all kinds, from a roaring flood to a still, clear pool.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Air} --- Air, wind, and weather; the medium of true flight.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Body} --- Humans and humanlike bodies, and those that once had souls.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Plant} --- Plants and trees, and all plant matter --- even dead wood and cloth.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Fire} --- Fire, heat, and light in all their restless forms.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Image} --- Illusions and phantasms; the appearance of things, never their substance.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Mind} --- Minds, thoughts, memories, and emotions --- and incorporeal spirits.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Earth} --- Solids, especially earth and stone: the foundation of the world.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Magic} --- Raw magical power itself; the stuff of spells and the beings made from it.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Specific Tags --- your own}
You may also choose \emph{any} tag you like, so long as it is more specific
in application than the Techniques and Forms above. A specific tag is
narrower, but it stacks with them for a bigger bonus. A few examples:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Gems} --- Precious stones and crystals, cut or raw.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Smoke} --- Smoke, mist, and haze.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Thorns} --- Barbs, brambles, and things that wound.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Hearing} --- Sound and the sense of it.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Reading} --- Written words and their meaning.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Protection} --- Wards, shields, and defence.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Throwing} --- Hurling and propelling objects.
  \item \atrtag{magic}{Flying} --- Flight and being borne aloft.
\end{itemize}
