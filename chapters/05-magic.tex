% REAL (from Notion): "Magic" is whatever makes a setting feel fantastic.
\lettrine{M}{agic} is whatever makes a setting feel fantastic --- literal
spellcasting in high fantasy, hacking in a cyberpunk sprawl, the Force
in a space opera, or tinkering and engineering in a world of hard science.
When you enter a realm, decide together what Magic \emph{looks like} there.

% ---- Section 1: Character Creation ---------------------------------
\section{At Character Creation}

% REAL RULE: Magic ranks + tag counts (from Notion). Keep verbatim.
\begin{statblock}[Magic Ranks]
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.16\linewidth} Y@{}}
\rating{1} & You do not know magic.\\[3pt]
\rating{2} & You can cast a few spells. \emph{Choose 3 tags; apply up to 2 to a spell.}\\[3pt]
\rating{3} & You know magic. \emph{Choose 7 tags; apply up to 3 to a spell.}\\[3pt]
\rating{4} & You are a \emph{fucking wizard}. \emph{Choose 12 tags; apply up to 5 to a spell.}\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{statblock}

Assign one of the four priorities (A through D) to Magic. Your rank
determines how many \keyword{tags} you know and how many you may apply
to a single spell. At rank \rating{1} you know no magic at all.

Choose your tags from Your Tags below before play begins. A tag's meaning
bends to fit the realm --- \emph{control} compels a beast in a fantasy
world and overrides a security system in the sprawl.

% ---- Section 2: Rules -----------------------------------------------
\section{How Magic Works}

% REAL RULE (Notion, 2026-09-21, decided with Chris): pool = base stat +
% one die per applicable tag. SUPERSEDES the earlier "two dice per tag".
A spell is rolled exactly like anything else: a \keyword{base Stat} plus
your expertise. For magic, that expertise is your tags. Take the Stat that
fits what the spell \emph{accomplishes}, then add \keyword{one die for each
applicable tag} --- up to the limit set by your Magic rank (\dice{2} at
rank~2, \dice{3} at rank~3, \dice{5} at rank~4). Count successes
(\dice{4}, \dice{5}, or \dice{6}) against the difficulty the GM sets.
\[
\text{\keyword{Stat} (1--5)} \;+\; \text{\keyword{applicable Tags}} \;=\; \text{pool}
\]

Because magic runs off the same three Stats as everything else, there is no
such thing as a character who is good at ``magic'' in general. Shapeshifting
leans on \keyword{Body}, divination on \keyword{Mind}, a glamour on
\keyword{Face} --- so the way you work magic expresses who you are.

Every act of magic pairs what you \emph{do} (a Technique) with what you
\emph{affect} (a Form): \emph{create}~+~\emph{fire} to kindle a flame,
\emph{perceive}~+~\emph{mind} to read a thought.

\begin{example}
Rooster is a rank~\rating{3} magic user with \keyword{Body} \dice{2} and the
tags \emph{create, destroy, fire, water, food, flying}. They want to conjure
a can of soda. That is a physical object appearing, so the base is
\keyword{Body}. Three tags apply --- \emph{create}, \emph{food}, and
\emph{water} --- and rank~3 permits three. Rooster rolls
\dice{2}~+~\dice{3}~=~\dice{5} dice.
\end{example}

% ---- Section 3: Options ---------------------------------------------
\section{Your Tags}

Choose your tags from the Techniques and Forms below, plus any Specific
tags you invent. The Techniques and Forms are drawn from \emph{Ars Magica};
the tag-selection system is inspired by John Harper's \emph{Lady Blackbird}.

\subsection*{Techniques --- what you do}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \keyword{Create} --- Produce something from nothing, or restore a thing to its whole, ideal state.
  \item \keyword{Perceive} --- Sense, read, and learn; uncover the hidden connections between things.
  \item \keyword{Transform} --- Change one thing into another, and direct the mechanisms of change itself.
  \item \keyword{Destroy} --- Break down, wither, and unmake; hasten damage, decay, and dissolution.
  \item \keyword{Control} --- Move and command matter, or compel the actions of living things.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Forms --- what you affect}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \keyword{Animal} --- Beasts of every kind, from the fish of the sea to the birds of the air.
  \item \keyword{Water} --- Liquids of all kinds, from a roaring flood to a still, clear pool.
  \item \keyword{Air} --- Air, wind, and weather; the medium of true flight.
  \item \keyword{Body} --- Humans and humanlike bodies, and those that once had souls.
  \item \keyword{Plant} --- Plants and trees, and all plant matter --- even dead wood and cloth.
  \item \keyword{Fire} --- Fire, heat, and light in all their restless forms.
  \item \keyword{Image} --- Illusions and phantasms; the appearance of things, never their substance.
  \item \keyword{Mind} --- Minds, thoughts, memories, and emotions --- and incorporeal spirits.
  \item \keyword{Earth} --- Solids, especially earth and stone: the foundation of the world.
  \item \keyword{Magic} --- Raw magical power itself; the stuff of spells and the beings made from it.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Specific Tags --- your own}
You may also choose \emph{any} tag you like, so long as it is more specific
in application than the Techniques and Forms above. A specific tag is
narrower, but it stacks with them for a bigger bonus. A few examples:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \keyword{Gems} --- Precious stones and crystals, cut or raw.
  \item \keyword{Smoke} --- Smoke, mist, and haze.
  \item \keyword{Thorns} --- Barbs, brambles, and things that wound.
  \item \keyword{Hearing} --- Sound and the sense of it.
  \item \keyword{Reading} --- Written words and their meaning.
  \item \keyword{Protection} --- Wards, shields, and defence.
  \item \keyword{Throwing} --- Hurling and propelling objects.
  \item \keyword{Flying} --- Flight and being borne aloft.
\end{itemize}
