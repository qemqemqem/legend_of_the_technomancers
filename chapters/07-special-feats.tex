\lettrine{S}{ome things} are not a bigger pool --- they are a door that
dice alone cannot open. A \keyword{Special Feat} is an exceptional, often
impossible capability: flight, reading minds, walking through walls.
Every one of the four columns --- \keyword{Body}, \keyword{Skills},
\keyword{Magic}, and \keyword{Stuff} --- grants them at high rank, so
rather than duplicate one catalogue four times, they live here instead.

\begin{statblock}[Special Feats Granted, Any Column]
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{0.16\linewidth} Y@{}}
\rating{1} & None.\\[3pt]
\rating{2} & None.\\[3pt]
\rating{3} & \dice{1} Special Feat.\\[3pt]
\rating{4} & \dice{2} Special Feats in total.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{statblock}

This applies independently to each column. A character who reaches rank
\rating{3} in both Skills and Magic gets one Feat from each --- two
Feats total, not one. \todo{Open question, 2026-09-22: is there any cap
on how many Feats can stack this way? A character who maxed all four
columns to rank 4 could in principle hold eight. Needs a decision, then
playtesting, before that's treated as intended rather than an oversight.}

% ---- Section 1: Rules ------------------------------------------------
\section{How You Get a Feat}

When a column reaches rank \rating{3} or \rating{4}, choose your new
Feat (or Feats) from \emph{Your Feats} below, or work with your GM to
build one suited to your realm. Note which column granted each Feat ---
it decides how you'll explain the Feat re-skinning later, not how it
works mechanically, since a Feat functions identically regardless of
source.

\begin{example}
Kira takes \keyword{Body} to rank \rating{3} and chooses \keyword{Fly}
--- for her, that's a pair of retractable wings. If she'd taken
\keyword{Stuff} to rank \rating{3} instead and picked the same Feat, it
would be a jetpack. Either way, she can take to the air; only the
fictional dressing differs.
\end{example}

A personal Special Feat re-skins rather than disappears when you change
worlds, exactly like a Body Tag: a dragon's wings become a jetpack, a
psychic's mind-reading becomes a neural hack. It belongs to \emph{you},
not the setting, so it always travels with you.

\section{Rolling for a Feat}
% REAL RULE, 2026-09-23: Continuous Feats never roll; Action Feats do.
% Every Feat entry states which, and names the usual Stat. Added after
% playtesters 02 03 04 05 06 07 10 all asked whether Feats need rolls
% and which Stat they use (notably a Skill-sourced Blink vs the Magic
% chapter's "teleporting is Weird").
Every Feat says in its heading whether it needs a roll.

\textsc{Continuous} Feats never need one. Having the Feat is enough:
you fly, you don't need air, you understand the dog. If you then
\emph{do} something hard with it, like outrunning a storm on the wing
or talking a guard dog into looking away, that is an ordinary roll for
the action, not a roll for the Feat.

\textsc{Action} Feats do need a roll each time you use them. Roll the
Stat named in the Feat's heading, add every tag that fits, and the GM
sets the target by how hard the moment is (see \emph{Setting the Target
Number} in \emph{For the Game Master}). Blinking across an
empty room is easy. Blinking out of a troll's grip with its friends
watching is not. On a failure, the Feat misfires, falls short, or costs
you, like any other failed roll.

A Feat already grants the impossible part, so a Feat roll never uses
\keyword{Weird}, whichever column the Feat came from. A Blink bought
with Skills and one bought with Magic both roll \keyword{Body}. If the
fiction makes a different Stat a clearly better fit, the GM can switch
it, just as for any roll (see \emph{Base Stat} in \emph{How to Play}).

\section{World-Granted Feats}
% REAL, moved from 02-body.tex 2026-09-22 --- generalized: this was
% framed as a Body-specific notebox before Special Feats had its own
% chapter, but a world-granted Feat isn't tied to any one column.
Not every Special Feat is personal. Some are a property of a
\emph{realm} rather than a character --- an ambient rule the GM sets up
that lets \emph{every} traveller in that world do one impossible thing,
whether or not they spent a single point on Body, Skills, Magic, or
Stuff. In High Fantasy, anyone present may \keyword{Appeal to the Gods};
no one bought it, and no one carries it home. If you want to keep a
world-granted Feat after you leave its realm, spend a
\keyword{Persistence Point} to make it permanently yours, exactly as you
would fix any other feature in place. Each genre chapter offers its own
example.

\section{Your Feats}

Ten Feats to start from, all common --- available in any realm and
reflavoured to fit. Each one bypasses exactly one hard constraint
(gravity, privacy of thought, the need to breathe, and so on), which
keeps them roughly matched in power even though their flavour differs
wildly. Every entry names whether it's an \textsc{Action} (something you
choose to do, in the moment) or \textsc{Continuous} (something that's
simply always true about you, nothing to activate), and whether it
needs a roll: Continuous Feats never do, and Action Feats name the Stat
they usually roll (see \emph{Rolling for a Feat}, above). It then lists the
columns that plausibly grant it with a short example of what that looks
like. Not every Feat fits every column, and that's fine --- pick
whichever combination fits the character you're building, or propose
your own to your GM.

\begin{feat}{Fly}{Continuous}{No roll}
Take to the air under your own power, for as long as the fiction
allows. No roll is needed just to stay aloft --- roll only when
something about the flight itself is in doubt: outrunning a storm,
threading a canyon at speed, carrying more weight than you should.
\featsource{body}{Wings}{A dragon's leathery wings, feathered pinions, or a build simply light enough to ride the wind.}
\featsource{skill}{Wingsuit Mastery}{Momentum and airflow control pushed so far past ordinary limits it reads as true flight.}
\featsource{magic}{Levitation}{A standing flight spell woven into your very self, no incantation needed to sustain it.}
\featsource{item}{Jetpack}{Strap it on, thumb the ignition, and go; a hoverbike or grav-boots work the same way.}
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Read Minds}{Action}{Roll Mind}
Focus on someone nearby and perceive their surface thoughts and
feelings directly --- not their deepest secrets, just whatever's
sitting at the front of their mind right now. It takes attention; you
can't read a whole room at a glance the way you can see it.
\featsource{body}{Telepathic Nerves}{A nervous system wired for empathy so raw it borders on hearing thoughts.}
\featsource{skill}{Cold Reading}{Interrogation and body-language mastery so precise it might as well be psychic.}
\featsource{magic}{Telepathy}{A spell that opens a channel into someone's head, willing or not.}
\featsource{item}{Thought-Scanner}{A neural interface or handheld device that translates brainwaves into words.}
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Form Shadow}{Action}{Roll Body}
Become or merge with shadow at will, turning briefly incorporeal ---
slip under a door, ride along a wall, or simply vanish into the dark
for as long as you hold the shape.
\featsource{body}{Shadow-Born}{A physiology that was never entirely solid to begin with.}
\featsource{skill}{Total Infiltration}{Stealth training so complete it borders on genuinely not being there.}
\featsource{magic}{Umbral Shift}{A spell that unbinds you from solid form for as long as shadow covers you.}
\featsource{item}{Phase Cloak}{A field generator that renders you as insubstantial as the shadow you're standing in.}
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Needs No Air}{Continuous}{No roll}
Water, smoke, poison gas, even the vacuum of space --- none of it can
suffocate you. This runs all the time; there's nothing to activate and
nothing to sustain.
\featsource{body}{Adapted Lungs}{Gills, or lungs (if you can even call them that) built for anything.}
\featsource{skill}{Breath Control}{Training so extreme your body simply stopped needing what it used to.}
\featsource{magic}{Ward of Breath}{A standing spell that keeps the air, or the absence of it, from ever mattering.}
\featsource{item}{Rebreather}{A void suit, a rebreather rig, or an internal air recycler built into you.}
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Never Sleep}{Continuous}{No roll}
You do not need rest the way others do, and you're never caught
unaware in the dead of night. Whatever your party does on watch, you
can do all night, every night.
\featsource{body}{Tireless Physiology}{A body that simply doesn't accumulate fatigue the normal way.}
\featsource{skill}{Iron Discipline}{Training and willpower pushed so far past ordinary limits that sleep became optional.}
\featsource{magic}{Sustaining Working}{A quiet, standing spell that keeps you rested without ever resting.}
\featsource{item}{Stim Implant}{A biomechanical implant or drug regimen that keeps you sharp indefinitely.}
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Speak with Animals}{Continuous}{No roll}
Every beast understands you, and you understand it back --- not just
simple commands, but genuine conversation, limited only by how much an
animal actually has to say.
\featsource{body}{Beast-Bond}{An empathic connection to living things that was simply always there.}
\featsource{skill}{Animal Handling, Mastered}{A lifetime spent reading creatures so well it might as well be language.}
\featsource{magic}{Beast-Tongue}{A standing spell that translates every growl, chirp, and howl.}
\featsource{item}{Universal Translator}{A device tuned to catch and render non-human speech patterns.}
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Walk Through Walls}{Action}{Roll Body}
Solid matter is a suggestion, not a barrier. Choose to phase through a
wall, a locked door, or a stretch of solid stone --- you can't linger
inside it, but you can cross it.
\featsource{body}{Malleable Form}{A body that's never entirely committed to being solid.}
\featsource{magic}{Phase Step}{A spell that lets solid matter pass through you, rather than the other way around.}
\featsource{item}{Phase-Door Rig}{Short-ranged and expensive; it opens matter just long enough for you to pass.}
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Turn Invisible}{Action}{Roll Body}
Vanish from sight at will, for as long as you hold still enough to
matter --- a fast dash or a shout gives you away, but standing
motionless in a shadowed doorway, no one will find you.
\featsource{body}{Chameleon Skin}{A physiology built to disappear against any backdrop.}
\featsource{skill}{Master Misdirection}{Sleight of hand and stagecraft so complete it plays as genuine vanishing.}
\featsource{magic}{Invisibility}{The classic working: light bends around you until you choose to be seen again.}
\featsource{item}{Cloaking Suit}{Active camouflage woven into a cloak, coat, or full bodysuit.}
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{See Through Solid Matter}{Action}{Roll Mind}
Focus your gaze and it passes straight through walls, skin, and stone,
showing you whatever's hidden on the other side. It takes
concentration, so you can't do it passively while doing something else
demanding.
\featsource{body}{Deep-Spectrum Eyes}{Eyes built to see wavelengths ordinary vision never could.}
\featsource{skill}{Structural Reading}{An engineer's or surgeon's eye trained to "see" what's inside from the outside.}
\featsource{magic}{Scrying Sight}{A spell that peels back one layer of the world at a time.}
\featsource{item}{Scanner Rig}{A handheld or implanted scanner that renders solid matter transparent on a display.}
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Blink}{Action}{Roll Body}
Cover a short distance in an instant --- across a room, over a gap,
out of a grapple --- too fast for anyone watching to actually follow
the motion.
\featsource{body}{Blink-Step}{An innate, reflexive short-range teleport wired into your body.}
\featsource{skill}{Impossible Speed}{Movement so explosively fast it looks like teleportation to anyone without your training.}
\featsource{magic}{Short Teleport}{A quick, low-cost teleport spell, measured in feet, not miles.}
\featsource{item}{Short-Range Teleporter}{A wrist-mounted or backpack unit good for one quick jump at a time.}
\end{feat}
