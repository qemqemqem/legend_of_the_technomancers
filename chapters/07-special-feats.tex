\lettrine{S}{ome things} you simply \emph{do}. A roll asks whether you
manage something; a \keyword{Special Feat} means the question never
comes up. You fly. You breathe in the void. You know when you are being
lied to. No dice, no effort, no doubt.

\begin{statblock}[Special Feats Granted by Skills or Magic]

\rating{2} & None.\\[3pt]
\rating{3} & \dice{1} Special Feat.\\[3pt]
\rating{4} & \dice{2} Special Feats in total.\\
\end{tabularx}
\end{statblock}

Skills and Magic each grant Feats separately. A character at rank
\rating{3} in both Skills and Magic has two Feats: one Skills Feat and
one Magic Feat. Body grants \keyword{Special Traits} instead, at the
same ranks (see \emph{Special Traits} in \emph{Body}). Stuff grants no
Feats; the remarkable things you own are \keyword{Gadgets}, bought with
Stuff Points (see \emph{Gadgets} in \emph{Stuff}).

% ---- Section 1: Rules ------------------------------------------------
\section{How Feats Work}

\subsection*{Always On}
A Feat never needs a roll. It has no uses to count and nothing to
recharge. If it describes something you are, it is always true. If it
describes something you do, you do it whenever you like.

\subsection*{The Fiction, Not the Dice}
A Feat never adds dice, removes dice, or changes a target number. It
changes what is \emph{true}, and the GM calls for rolls based on what is
true. If you are invisible, the guards' eyes are no obstacle, and there
is no roll to slip past them --- unless their dogs can still smell you,
in which case \emph{that} is the roll.

\subsection*{Hard Things Are Still Rolls}
Doing something difficult \emph{with} a Feat is an ordinary roll.
Flying is free; outflying a dragon is a \keyword{Body} roll. Hearing a
guard's thoughts is free; prying loose the secret she is trying not to
think about is a spell.

\subsection*{Limits Are the Point}
A Feat does exactly what it says and no more. Its limits are part of
the Feat, and they are what keep it fair. When a situation is unclear,
the GM reads the Feat narrowly.

\section{Choosing Your Feats}

When Skills or Magic reaches rank \rating{3} or \rating{4}, choose a
Feat from that Column's list below. Each Column's Feats are pitched
differently on purpose:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \keyword{Magic} Feats are the strongest and most versatile.
    Reality bends around you.
  \item \keyword{Skills} Feats are mundane and a little weaker. Nothing
    on the list is impossible, only legendary.
\end{itemize}
You may also build your own Feat with your GM. Measure it against its
Column's list: it should feel about as strong as its neighbours.

\subsection*{Feats Across the Realms}
Your Feats travel with you and re-skin to fit each new realm, like your
tags. Flight might be leathery wings in one world and hip-mounted
thrusters in the next.

\renewcommand{\realmaccent}{columnMagic}
\section{Magic Feats}
\artinline{feat-magic-stormcaller.png}

\begin{feat}{Blink}
Teleport to anywhere you can see within a stone's throw, as often as you
like. You take only yourself and what you carry.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Far-Caster}
Your spells reach anything you can see, however far away: the far side
of a valley, a ship on the horizon, a face in a tower window.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Hearth}
Wherever you rest for the night becomes your hearth. Spirits, scrying,
and hostile magic cannot reach you or the companions sleeping near you
there. Swords still can. From anywhere in the same realm, an hour of
stillness brings you, and anyone holding your hands, back to your last
hearth.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Lasting Works}
Your spells on places and things last until you end them or someone
breaks them: a wall of ice stays standing, a light keeps burning, a
lock stays sealed. Spells on people and creatures still fade as usual.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Legion}
Any spell you can cast on one person, you can cast on a small group
standing together, about as many as could share a table. Roll once for
each target; every failure carries its own cost.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Mind-Hearing}
Meet someone's eyes and you hear their surface thoughts: whatever they
are thinking right now, one person at a time, and only in a language
you speak. They feel watched while you listen. Deeper secrets still
take a spell.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Realm-Sense}
You know when something does not belong in the realm you are in: other
travellers, smuggled artifacts, and the thin places where realms meet.
You can feel which way they lie, and you can tell which realm a thing
or person came from, and where a thin place leads.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Siege-Mage}
Your spells work at the scale of buildings. What would break a door
breaks a gatehouse; what would knock down a soldier topples a tower.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Spirit-Sight}
You always see ghosts, spirits, invisible creatures, and magic itself:
auras, wards, curses, and spells as they are woven.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Stormcaller}
The weather within sight follows your will. Clearing the sky or calling
a storm takes a few minutes, not a roll. Putting lightning exactly where
you want it is still a spell.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Tongue of All Things}
Speak with animals, plants, spirits, and objects, and understand their
answers. They know only what they know: a door remembers who passed
through it, not their names.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Wards}
Hostile spells cast on anyone or anything near you simply fail. Only a
spell aimed at you directly gets through.
\end{feat}

\renewcommand{\realmaccent}{columnSkills}
\section{Skills Feats}
\artinline{feat-skills-quick-change.png}

\begin{feat}{Always Armed}
Searched, stripped, or jailed, you still have a few small things hidden
on you: a blade, a lockpick, a length of wire, a coin for a bribe.
Nothing bigger than your palm.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Appraiser}
At a glance, you know what an object is worth, roughly where and when it
was made, whether it is a fake, and who nearby would buy it. Magic items
tell you only that they are magic.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Architect's Eye}
Walk around a building, or through one floor of it, and you know its
layout, where the walls are thin, and where someone would put a vault.
Not what is inside.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Beast-Friend}
Animals never attack you unless they are provoked, starving, or
commanded, and tame animals take to you at once: guard dogs let you
pass, strange horses let you ride. That doesn't mean they do what you
say.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Coroner}
Examine a body and you know how the person died, roughly when, and
whether they were moved. Not who did it, but something about them: tall
or short, left- or right-handed, skilled or clumsy, and whether the
victim knew them.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Field Medic}
Tend someone's wounds for an hour and injuries that would take weeks to
heal are gone in a day or two. Nothing you tend festers. You cannot
bring back what is lost, and a fight in progress is no place for it.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Fixer}
In any town, within a day, you find out who sells what, legal or not.
They still set the price.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Forgettable}
No one remembers your face afterwards unless you gave them a reason to.
Cameras and portraits still work.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Honest Face}
Strangers assume you belong wherever you are: a servant, a guest, a
local. It lasts until you do something that doesn't belong.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Lie-Sense}
You can tell when someone lies to your face, if you can see them and
speak their language. You learn that it is a lie, not what the truth
is, and half-truths slip past.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Never Lost}
You always know where you are, which way is north, and the way back to
anywhere you have been. In a new realm, this takes a day to settle in.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Polyglot}
You become fluent in any human language after a few hours of hearing
it.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Quick-Change}
In a minute, with whatever is to hand, you disguise yourself well enough
to pass at a glance as a different kind of person: a guard, a servant, a
priest. Not as a specific person.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Read the Room}
When you walk into a scene, you know who is in charge, who is armed, and
who is nervous.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Survivalist}
In the wild, you and a few companions never go hungry, thirsty, or
without shelter, and you can read the land: tomorrow's weather, safe
water, and what has passed along a trail. Where there is truly nothing,
you cannot find it.
\end{feat}

\begin{feat}{Unforgettable}
Anyone who meets you remembers you and your name forever, for good or
ill. Those who liked you go out of their way to help you again.
\end{feat}
